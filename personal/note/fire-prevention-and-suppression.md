## Reporting a Fire by Phone

- **Stay calm** — keep your composure before dialing.
- **State the emergency** — what is happening (fire, where it's spreading).
- **Give the location** — building, floor, room, nearest landmark.
- **Report the type of fuel burning** — e.g. electrical, gas, liquid fuel, solid materials.
- **Give your name** — so officers know who is reporting.
- **Give a call-back number** — in case they need to reach you for details.

## Fire by Size

| Level | Description | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Small fire** | Flames on a person or small localized flames | Water can put it out — act immediately |
| **Medium fire** | A fire spreading inside a house/building | Should be brought under control **within 5 minutes** or it's too late |
| **Spreading fire** | A medium fire that has burned past the 5-minute mark | Do **not** attempt to fight it — evacuate and let the fire department handle it |

## Surviving a Fire in a High-Rise Building

- **Climb down with improvised ropes (floors 2–3 only):** tie bedsheets/cloth to something solid and sturdy, then climb down slowly.
- **With children:** tie the child securely to your body, then climb down slowly (floors 2–3 only).
- **In a true high-rise:** your priority is getting *down* — use the fire escape/stairwell, and follow the **green exit signs**.
- **Every building has firefighting equipment — find it** if escape is impossible:
  - A **fire hose reel** ("hose rag" in the course slang) — the hose is at least **30 m long**. If there's no water, it can double as a **rope for climbing down**.
  - Per building regulations, fire equipment cabinets are spaced roughly **67 m apart** — you're never far from one.
  - If you must decide **who climbs down first**: lightest person first, heaviest last — this maximizes the number of survivors given the rope's strength limit.
- **Smoke is the real killer.** Building fires fill the space with **hydrogen cyanide** and other toxic gases. If smoke is so thick you can't see the way — **do not walk into it**. You will be incapacitated by smoke inhalation before you reach safety. Instead:
  - Stay in your room and **wait for firefighters**.
  - Place a **wet towel at the base of the door** — this signals firefighters that someone is inside **and** blocks smoke from seeping in.
- **Make yourself visible:** position yourself where others can see you, so people know the building is occupied and so you can reach moving air.
- **If nobody notices you're inside:** throw objects out (shoes, pillows, anything) to attract the attention of people below.
- **If you must cross through smoke:** wring out a cloth until damp (not dripping), cover your nose and mouth, and move through — the water in the fabric filters the smoke.
- **No goggles / no cloth?** Fill a large plastic bag with clean air, put it over your head, and seal the opening with your hands. This gives you roughly **5 minutes** of breathable air to get through.

## Afternoon Lecture: Fire Behavior

### How Fires Start

A fire needs three things — remove any one of them and the fire dies (the **fire triangle**):

1. **Fuel (เชื้อเพลิง)**
   - **Solids** — paper, wood, clothing, plastic, rubber
   - **Liquids** — various oils, thinner, alcohol
   - **Gases** — LPG, flammable gases
2. **Oxygen** — air feeding the combustion.
3. **Heat / ignition sources**
   - Sunlight
   - Friction
   - Piled-up, poorly ventilated fuel (spontaneous heating from stacking)

### The Fire Triangle in Practice

- **Combustion consumes oxygen:** air is ~21% oxygen, and combustion cannot sustain itself below ~16% oxygen — this is why smothering works.
- **Heat** raises the fuel's temperature until it **vaporizes** — it's the vapor, not the solid/liquid itself, that burns.
- **Fuel** may be solid, liquid, or gas.
- **Remove any one element and the fire goes out:**
  - **Remove/isolate the fuel** (shut the gas valve, clear the fuel bed)
  - **Remove the air** (smother — blanket, foam, CO₂)
  - **Remove the heat** (cool with water)

## Fire Classification (ISO 3941 / NFPA)

> ⚠️ The course mixed the two standards. The table below reconciles them: **ISO 3941** puts *gases* in Class C and *energized electrical* outside the letter classes, while **NFPA 10** folds flammable gases into Class B and uses Class C for energized electrical equipment. Always check which classification your extinguisher is rated for.

| Class | Fuel | Standard notes | Correct response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Solids** — wood, paper, fabric, plastic | Same in both standards | **Cool with water** (or ABC dry chemical) |
| **B** | **Liquids** — gasoline, benzine, gasohol, alcohol. *(NFPA also includes flammable **gases** here; ISO puts gases in Class C)* | Never use water — it spreads the liquid | **Smother fast** — foam, CO₂, or dry chemical; cut off the fuel source |
| **C (ISO) / B (NFPA)** | **Gases** — propane, LPG | Shut the valve first if safe | **Cut heat and fuel origin ASAP** — then extinguish residual flames |
| **C (NFPA)** | **Energized electrical** — panels, appliances, wiring | Water conducts electricity | **CO₂ or dry chemical**; **cut the power / trip the breaker**; use **non-conductive material** (dry wood, rubber, fabric) to pull victims or items away from the source |
| **D** | **Metals & reactive chemicals** — magnesium, sodium, potassium | Water makes metal fires explode | Use **specific Class D dry powder** (e.g. copper/sodium chloride agents) — trained personnel only |
| **K** | **Cooking oil / kitchen fires** — deep fryers, woks | Water causes a violent fireball | **Cut the fuel and heat**, then use a **wet chemical (Class K) extinguisher** — never water, never a blanket soaked in anything flammable |

## Fire Extinguishers

### Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) — Under Pressure
- CO₂ stored at **800–900 PSI at 60 °F**; cylinder rated to at least **3375** (per course notes)
- CO₂ vapor is **~1.5× heavier than air**, so it blankets the fire and displaces oxygen
- Leaves no residue — good for electrical equipment
- ⚠️ Discharge is extremely cold (−78 °F / −57 °C): avoid holding the horn; frostbite risk

### Halotron (BF2000 / BF36) — Clean Agent
- Pressurized with **nitrogen at 40 PSI at 70 °F**
- Boiling point **25 °F**
- Vapor is **~5× heavier than air** — effective smothering
- Clean, residue-free alternative to the old Halon 1211; suitable for electronics and server rooms

### Dry Chemical *(the course cut off here — filled in)*
- **ABC (monoammonium phosphate)** — the all-purpose extinguisher for solids, liquids, gases, and energized electrical
- **BC (sodium bicarbonate / Purple K)** — for liquids, gases, and electrical only
- Stored-pressure design, most common type in buildings
- ⚠️ The powder is corrosive to electronics and hard to clean — CO₂ is better for sensitive equipment

### How to Use Any Extinguisher — **PASS**
1. **P**ull the pin
2. **A**im at the **base of the fire** (not the flames)
3. **S**queeze the handle
4. **S**weep side to side
- Keep your back to the exit. If the extinguisher empties and the fire isn't out — **leave immediately**.

## Fire Prevention — Full Picture

Fire prevention works at three levels: **engineering/design**, **management/inspection**, and **daily behavior**. The course focused on the last one — here's the rest.

### Daily Behavior (Home & Office)

- **Electrical:** don't overload outlets or daisy-chain power strips; replace frayed cables; turn off irons/heaters when leaving.
- **Cooking:** never leave hot oil unattended; keep a pot lid nearby (slide it over the pan to smother a grease fire — never water).
- **Storage:** don't pile combustibles near heat sources or in direct sun; keep stairwells and fire exits **clear at all times**.
- **Gas (LPG):** check hoses/regulators for cracks; smell gas → open windows, don't switch anything on/off, shut the valve, evacuate.
- **Detection:** smoke alarms on every floor; test monthly, replace batteries yearly.
- **Plan:** know two ways out of every room; agree on a meeting point outside; practice it.

### Special Hazards (the ones actually causing fires today)

- 🔋 **Lithium batteries — power banks, phones, e-bikes, EVs:** don't charge on beds/sofas, don't charge overnight unattended, use original chargers, retire swollen or damaged batteries. Battery fires self-supply oxygen and resist water — **prevention is the only reliable defense**.
- 🕯️ **Candles, incense, mosquito coils:** a leading cause of Thai house fires, especially at night. Extinguish completely before sleeping; keep away from curtains, bedding, and paper.
- 🔥 **Hot work (welding, grinding, cutting):** use a permit system, keep combustibles cleared within ~10 m, and post a **fire watch for at least 30 minutes after work ends** (embers reignite).
- 🧪 **Flammable chemicals:** approved storage cabinets, SDS/MSDS on hand, never stored near exits or heat sources.
- 🚭 **Smoking:** designated areas only, deep ashtrays — careless smoking is a top cause of fatal night fires.

### Building & Engineering Controls

- **Compartmentation:** fire doors and fire-rated walls hold a fire in one room — keep fire doors **closed** (never wedged open).
- **Automatic systems:** sprinklers, smoke/heat detectors, kitchen hood suppression, gas suppression in server rooms.
- **Exits & signage:** fire escapes, emergency lighting, green exit signs; corridors and stairwells must stay clear of storage — a blocked exit is both illegal and deadly.
- **Smoke control:** pressurized stairwells and smoke exhaust vents; know where the refuge floor/area is in your building.

### Inspection & Maintenance Schedule

| Item | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Extinguisher visual check (pin, gauge, seal, hose) | Monthly |
| Extinguisher professional service | Annually |
| Smoke alarm test | Monthly |
| Smoke alarm battery | Replace yearly |
| Fire hose reel check | Monthly |
| Sprinkler valves / fire pump test | Quarterly–annually per building schedule |
| Fire drill | **At least once a year** (Thai law) |
| Fire door & exit path check | Weekly housekeeping |

### Organization & People

- **Fire warden/marshal per floor:** sweep assigned areas, head count at the assembly point, lead evacuation.
- **Fire prevention plan** (OSHA 1910.39 is a good model): list all major fire hazards, safe handling/storage of hazardous materials, ignition sources and their controls, fire protection equipment, and **who is responsible for maintaining it**.
- **Drills:** the Thai Ministerial Regulation on workplace fire safety (Clause 30, published in Ratchakitcha 2013) requires employers with **10+ employees** to run combined firefighting + evacuation drills for all employees **at least once a year** — and all employers share the duty when multiple companies occupy one building.
- **Vulnerable occupants** (children, elderly, people with disabilities): assign a buddy and a personal evacuation plan — they need extra time.
- **Most fatal fires happen at night.** Sleep with bedroom doors closed: a closed door holds back smoke and heat for precious minutes.

### Thai Legal Framework

- **พ.ร.บ. การป้องกันและระงับอัคคีภัย พ.ศ. 2542** (Fire Prevention and Suppression Act B.E. 1999) — the parent law this training is named after.
- **Ministerial regulations on workplace fire safety** (Ratchakitcha, 2013) — drills, training, fire wardens, hazard controls.
- **Building Control Act regulations** — fire equipment placement (fire department connection points spaced ≤ 64 m apart), exits, and fire protection systems.

## What NOT to Do

- ❌ Never use **water** on grease, chemical, metal, or electrical fires
- ❌ Never use the **elevator** during a fire
- ❌ Never open a **hot door** — check with the back of your hand first; if hot, use the other exit
- ❌ Never go back inside for belongings
- ❌ Never fight a fire that's past the **5-minute / spreading** stage — evacuate
