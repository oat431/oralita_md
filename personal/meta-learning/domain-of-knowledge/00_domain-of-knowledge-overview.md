# Domain of Knowledge: The Crosswalk Index

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Parent:** [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory), and the follow-up question waiting behind it: "how do we properly learn each kind of knowledge?"
> **What this is:** 25 domain notes, harvested from 7 maps of knowledge, deduplicated. One domain, one note. Every note shows which maps claim the domain, under what name, and what that means for learning it.

## How to use this folder

- **Start here, then pick a domain.** This note is the map of the maps: the crosswalk matrix below tells you where any domain sits in every classification.
- **Each domain note has the same shape:** what the domain studies, who claims it (its own crosswalk row), its method and culture, its seams with neighbors, and a stub for *how to learn it*.
- **The "how to learn" sections are deliberately stubs.** They are the landing site for question 2 (the planned [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] follow-up note on learning each kind). When that note lands, the stubs get filled.
- **Reading order:** 01 to 25 follows the six method families (formal, empirical, design, social, humanities, normative), not importance. Any entry point works; the links are the map.

Snow and Biglan are not domain lists: their entries ("hard-pure") are categories *over* domains, so they appear in each domain note's "method and culture" section instead. Faculties and research specialties are excluded as non-epistemic (bureaucratic and community-sized, not knowledge-shaped).

## Seams worth watching

The best reading in the matrix is the borders. Highlights:

- **Mathematics is the only domain all seven maps claim.** Everything else is missing somewhere.
- **Computing has no home in UNESCO 1973.** The nomenclature predates computing as a field: proof that maps are historical artifacts.
- **Religion has no home in UNESCO 1973** either (nearest: Ethics 71 and Arts/Letters 62), while Dewey gives it a whole class (200).
- **Philosophy has no home in IB TOK**: the course that studies knowledge left philosophy out as a "subject", because the whole course *is* philosophy.
- **Law is a social science for the OECD** (Social 5.5), a professional field for UNESCO (Juridical 56), and a class of its own for Dewey (340).
- **Geography is double-homed**: physical geography lives with earth science, human geography with social science (OECD only claims the human half: 5.7).
- **UNESCO 62 merges arts and letters**; Dewey splits them (700 vs 800); Thai splits them too (ศิลปะ vs วรรณคดี inside ภาษาไทย).
- **Aristotle ranked history below science**: he thought poetry more philosophical than history, because history only records particulars. History only became a knowledge area much later.

## The six method families

The families come from [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]], Section 7, and the domain numbering follows them:

| Family | How claims earn trust | Domains |
|---|---|---|
| **Formal** | proof from axioms | [[01_mathematics]] |
| **Empirical** | controlled observation and experiment | [[02_physics]], [[03_chemistry]], [[04_life-sciences]], [[05_earth-and-space-sciences]] |
| **Design** | it works, it fails safely, trade-offs managed | [[06_medicine-and-health]], [[07_agriculture-and-veterinary]], [[08_engineering-and-technology]], [[09_computing-and-ict]] |
| **Social** | evidence + models + interpretation of people | [[10_psychology]], [[11_economics]], [[12_sociology-and-demography]], [[13_political-science]], [[14_anthropology]], [[15_geography]] |
| **Humanities** | interpretation, argument, sources | [[16_history]], [[17_language-and-linguistics]], [[18_literature]], [[19_the-arts]], [[20_philosophy]] |
| **Normative** | judgment in context (phronesis) | [[21_ethics]], [[22_religion-and-theology]], [[23_law]], [[24_education]], [[25_business-and-management]] |

Families are the primary tag; every domain note also names its leaks. Medicine is design *and* empirical *and* normative; geography is social *and* empirical; language is humanities *and* pure skill (knowing-how). The leaks are not classification errors: they are the places where the territory refuses to be flat.
