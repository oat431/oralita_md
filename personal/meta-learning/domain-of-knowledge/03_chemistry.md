# Chemistry

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is chemistry as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Chemistry studies substances: what they are made of, how they are built, and how they transform. It is the **central science** in a literal sense: it takes physics' laws from above and supplies the molecules that [[04_life-sciences]] studies below. Biglan counts it hard-pure, but the chemical industry gives it one of the largest hard-applied wings in all of science. The maps file it beside physics under natural science (UNESCO's Chemistry 23, Dewey 540), and its Aristotle entry is a fossil from the centuries when chemistry had no name of its own.

## 1. What the domain studies

Chemistry studies matter at the level of substances and their transformations: composition, structure, bonding, reactions, and the mechanisms that connect them. Its characteristic questions are "what is this made of?", "why does it react this way?", and "how do I make it?": chemistry is the one natural science where making the thing is part of knowing the thing.

What counts as progress: a new substance, a new reaction or a better version of an old one, a mechanism established, a structure solved, a property explained from structure. Chemistry's map of itself is the periodic table, and its knowledge is carried partly in technique: distillation, titration, spectroscopy, synthesis. The instruments are not accessories but arguments: a spectrum is evidence. The price of this earthiness is a certain modesty about grand laws: chemistry explains by mechanism and trend rather than by one equation for everything.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | theoretical (part of natural philosophy) |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | Natural sciences |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Natural sciences 1.4 (chemical sciences) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | วิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยี (Science and Technology) |
| Dewey Decimal | 540 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Natural sciences, mathematics and statistics (05) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Chemistry 23 |

The most interesting placement is Aristotle's, because it records an absence: chemistry as such was **part of natural philosophy**, not a separate science. The domain spent centuries separating itself out (from alchemy and from physics alike) before the maps could give it a row of its own, and the maps remember the merger in their numbering: UNESCO puts Chemistry 23 right after Physics 22, Dewey puts 540 beside 530, Frascati splits 1.3 from 1.4 at the same joint. The category "natural science" simply grew another member.

The second placement story is the Frascati boundary. Chemical sciences sit at 1.4 as pure research, while the chemical industry lives in field 2 (engineering and technology). One of the world's largest knowledge-using industries is therefore filed *next to* chemistry rather than inside it: a map line between knowing a reaction and running it at scale.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **experiment at the bench**, reported so others can repeat it. Chemistry is empirical in the family sense: observation and experiment decide, but the domain adds its own demand, that a claim come with a procedure. You say what you did, in what order, with what quantities, and what came out; a result no one can reproduce is not a result. Mechanism and theory discipline the interpretation (thermodynamics and quantum mechanics from [[02_physics]] set the frame), but the compound is either what you say it is or it is not.

In Biglan's terms chemistry is **hard-pure at its core with a hard-applied wing**: high consensus and convergent on settled questions, yet tied to an industry that turns knowledge into product. Lab culture is the domain's social fact: the group, the hood, the shared instruments, the student who actually runs the reactions. And again the craft: the good chemist has **knowing-how** in the hands as well as the head, a feel for when a reaction is going wrong by its smell, its color, its timing.

A good day in the domain: a synthesis works, a spectrum confirms the structure you predicted on paper, and the yield says the route is worth publishing. Error-correction is blunt and practical: the reaction works or it does not, and the sample tells you so.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[02_physics]]: the seam of laws. Thermodynamics and quantum mechanics are borrowed capital, and physical chemistry is the border town where the two domains trade.
- [[04_life-sciences]]: the biochemistry seam. When the molecules are proteins and genes, whose knowledge is the mechanism: chemistry's or biology's? Molecular biology lives on this border.
- [[08_engineering-and-technology]]: the scale seam. Turning a reaction into a process changes the deed from knowing to building, and the maps agree: that is a different field.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

Chemistry's deed is the **experiment at the bench**, so the practitioner learns by doing: run reactions, characterize compounds, and work mechanisms on paper until electron-pushing feels like motion. Reading a synthesis is not knowing it; the domain's knowledge lives in procedures that must be executed to be understood. The empirical family's calibration lesson applies here in its most literal form: the sample either does what you claimed or it does not, and it will not flatter you.

## Key takeaways

- Chemistry is the central science: physics' laws above, life's molecules below, and the transformations in between as its own subject.
- Its Aristotle entry records an absence: once part of natural philosophy, it became a separate field only when the maps grew a row for it.
- Trust is earned at the bench: reproducible procedure, blunt error-correction, and technique carrying part of the knowledge.
- Biglan hard-pure with a hard-applied wing, and the maps put the wing (the chemical industry) in a different field entirely.
- To learn it is to do it: the chemist's deed is the working synthesis, so bench time and mechanism problems beat reading.
