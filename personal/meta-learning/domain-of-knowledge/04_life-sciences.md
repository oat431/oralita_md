# Life Sciences

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What are the life sciences as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

The life sciences study living organisms: their structure, function, growth, origin, and diversity. Evolution is the unifying idea that makes the field one field instead of a cabinet of curiosities. Every map files it as a natural science (UNESCO's Life Sciences 24, Dewey 570, Frascati's biological sciences at 1.6), but life's stubbornness about physics-style explanation gives the domain a different feel from its older sibling. Its applied sisters, [[06_medicine-and-health]] and [[07_agriculture-and-veterinary]], are close enough to claim whole chunks of it.

## 1. What the domain studies

The life sciences study living systems at every scale: molecules and cells, organs and organisms, populations and ecosystems. Its characteristic questions are "how does this work?", "how did it come to be?", and "why is it this way and not otherwise?": mechanism and history, asked about things that were shaped by descent rather than designed.

What counts as progress: a mechanism explained, a species described, an evolutionary history reconstructed, a gene's function established, an ecological pattern accounted for. Two features make the domain distinctive. First, **history rides alongside mechanism**: every organism carries the record of its descent, so explanation in biology is often a story of what came before, not only of what pushes now. Second, **variation is the norm, not noise**: the physicist's identical particles have no counterpart here, and that changes what counts as an explanation and what counts as an exception.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |

| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | Natural sciences |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Natural sciences 1.6 (biological sciences) |

| Dewey Decimal | 570 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Natural sciences, mathematics and statistics (05) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Life Sciences 24 |

The Aristotle row is the warmest cell in the whole crosswalk: not a classification but a portrait. The founder of the taxonomy of knowledge was himself a natural philosopher of animals, and the domain still carries his habit of looking closely and writing down what organisms do. The life sciences are the oldest natural philosophy that was also fieldwork, and the tension between the two cultures, naturalist and experimenter, is still visible inside the domain.

The second placement story is a subtraction. Aristotle studied animals as **theoretical** knowledge; the modern maps put the *uses* of life in entirely different fields: Medical Sciences 32 and Agricultural Sciences 31 in UNESCO, fields 3 and 4 in Frascati. The maps separate knowing life from serving life, a line practitioners feel much less clearly: the agronomist in the field and the ecologist in the same field share methods and disagree about purpose.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **controlled experiment and comparative observation**, in a domain where the objects cannot be standardized. The life sciences inherit the empirical family's demand for evidence and add the discipline of the control and the replicate, because living systems vary and noise is the environment's opinion. Theoretical elegance helps but does not settle: a beautiful mechanism that no organism actually uses is not biology's progress.

In Biglan's terms the domain is **hard-pure at its center with hard-applied borders**: high consensus on fundamentals (evolution, cell theory, genetics), convergent methods, and whole wings, biomedical and agricultural, whose question is what works. The culture is plural: molecular labs with gels and pipettes, field stations with binoculars and transects, computational biomes of sequences and models. All of them run on **knowing-how**: the trained eye that spots an abnormal behavior, the hands that keep a culture alive, the judgment about which comparison is fair.

A good day in the domain: an experiment cleanly distinguishes two hypotheses about how an organism works, or a field observation forces a rewrite of what you thought the species does. Error-correction is real but slower than physics: replicates take time, and organisms did not read your protocol.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[03_chemistry]]: the molecular seam. When the explanation is a pathway or a structure, does the knowledge belong to biology or to chemistry? Molecular biology is the shared house on this border.
- [[06_medicine-and-health]]: the application seam. Medicine borrows biology's mechanisms and adds patients, treatments, and duties: the seam runs between knowing life and healing it.
- [[07_agriculture-and-veterinary]]: the other application seam. Crops and livestock are life science in service of use, with design and norms attached.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The life scientist's deed is **observing closely and experimenting on what is observed**, so the practice that builds the domain looks like it: draw what you see, run the culture, design the control, and read the organism before the paper. Because variation is the norm, learning here means learning the exceptions along with the rule, which is why the field rewards memory organized by mechanism rather than memorized as lists. The field-bench mix is the point: a life science education done only at the desk produces the domain's characteristic half-knowledge.

## Key takeaways

- The life sciences study living systems, with evolution as the unifying idea and history riding alongside mechanism.
- The Aristotle cell is a portrait, not a classification: the domain began with someone watching animals carefully.
- The maps split knowing life (24 / 1.6) from using life (medical 32, agricultural 31), a line practitioners feel less clearly than mapmakers do.
- Biglan hard-pure at the center, hard-applied at the borders, with a culture split between naturalist and experimenter that still organizes the field.
- To learn it is to do it: the life scientist's deed is close observation plus controlled experiment, in the field and at the bench.
