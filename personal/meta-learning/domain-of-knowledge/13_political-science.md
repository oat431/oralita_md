# Political Science

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is political science as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Political science studies **power, governance, and the state**: how collective decisions are made, who makes them, and by what right. It sits on one of the domain world's oldest seams: is politics a science, as the behavioral turn insisted, or practical philosophy, as Aristotle answered? Its maps file it among the human and social sciences, but its subject leaks normative concepts like democracy and justice that no data can fully settle, and politics is also a TOK optional theme: a domain students inhabit before they study it.

## 1. What the domain studies

Political science studies how humans rule and organize themselves collectively: institutions, constitutions, parties, movements, revolutions, and war. Its questions are "who decides?", "by what authority?", "why do regimes persist or fall?", "what is legitimate?" The empirical wing counts votes and compares regimes; the normative wing asks what justice, liberty, and democracy demand. The subject matter is one every citizen has opinions about, which makes method discipline harder to keep.

What counts as progress is plural: a theory of institutions that travels across countries, an empirical finding that survives the hardest comparisons, a conceptual clarification that makes a political argument more honest. The domain's special risk is the temptation to smuggle the desired conclusion into the premises. Its special strength is that it studies the arrangements everyone lives inside, so its errors are felt publicly and corrected publicly.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |

| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | Human sciences (politics is also a TOK optional theme) |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Social sciences 5.6 (political science) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | สังคมศึกษา (Social Studies) |
| Dewey Decimal | 320 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Social sciences (03) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Political Science 59 |

The most interesting placement is once again Aristotle's: politics is **practical** science, alongside ethics, because its end is good action and good order, not contemplation or making. Modern political science largely bet that inheritance: the behavioral mid-century turn recast the field as a science of observable political behavior. IB splits the difference, listing politics as a human science while also carrying it as an optional theme: this is both knowledge to study and a situation you are already inside. The rest of the maps agree (social sciences, 320, field 59), which makes the Aristotle row the one that argues.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **comparison, evidence, and argument**. The empirical wing runs on hypothesis and experiment in its observational register: comparative case studies, statistical models of voting and institutions, natural experiments, game-theoretic models of strategy. The normative wing runs on interpretation and conceptual analysis: what does democracy require, when is authority legitimate, what does justice demand? In Biglan's terms the field is **soft**, split between soft-pure theory (political philosophy, formal models) and soft-applied practice (policy, governance, institutional design).

A good day: a comparison isolates the variable that explains regime change, or a normative argument sharpens in a way that changes what the empirical wing measures. Error-correction is peer review plus the unforgiving public: predictions about elections and interventions are visible, dated, and scored, and failed forecasts have become part of methodology training. The **normative leak** is permanent and should be admitted rather than hidden: democracy and justice are measurement problems and moral commitments at once. The habit that matters is keeping the two registers distinguishable while letting each correct the other.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[23_law]]: the deepest seam: law is the rules, politics is the power that writes and bends them, and constitutional questions belong to both.
- [[12_sociology-and-demography]]: the shared object of social order: sociology explains how orders reproduce, political science studies how they are governed and contested.
- [[21_ethics]]: the normative leak made explicit: theories of justice and legitimacy are ethics applied to collective life.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The social science practice is **arguing from evidence about power**, and the practitioner's deed is testing claims about institutions against cases while keeping normative claims visible. So learn it by pairing: study one polity empirically and one philosopher normatively, because the field is only half-learned from either side alone. Since politics is a domain you have opinions about, practice stating what evidence would change your mind: that is this domain's version of calibration.

## Key takeaways

- Political science studies power, governance, and legitimacy: the arrangements everyone lives inside and argues about.
- The oldest seam is Aristotle's own: politics as practical science versus behavioral science; modern maps record both without resolving either.
- Its normative leak is permanent: democracy and justice are empirical objects and moral commitments at once.
- Error-correction is public: elections and interventions are forecasted, dated, and scored.
- To learn it, pair evidence with argument and name what would change your mind.
