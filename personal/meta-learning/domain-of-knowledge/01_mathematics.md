# Mathematics (with Logic)

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is mathematics as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Mathematics studies structure, quantity, space, and change, justified by **proof** and nothing else: it is the purest case of the formal method family. It is also the only domain that **all seven maps claim**, and most of them place it at or near the foundation (Aristotle's theoretical sciences, the OECD's first natural science, UNESCO's field 12). Its most interesting seam is internal: **logic**, which UNESCO promotes to its own field (11) but Aristotle demoted to a mere tool. And its oldest question, discovered or invented, has the answer [[02_root-of-all-knowledge]] already gave: grown.

## 1. What the domain studies

Mathematics studies patterns and structures: number, shape, change, quantity, and the relations between them. Its characteristic questions are not "what happens?" but "**what must be true?**": Given these definitions and these axioms, what follows? Is this conjecture provable? Are these two structures secretly the same?

What counts as progress is equally distinctive: a new theorem, a new proof, a new structure that unifies old ones, or the discovery that two fields are one. Progress is permanent in a way no other domain quite matches: Euclid's proofs still stand. The price is scope: mathematics says nothing about the world until some other domain applies it.

**Logic** rides along as mathematics' twin and conscience: the study of valid inference itself, the machinery that decides what "follows" means. Where mathematics builds structures, logic studies the rules the building obeys. In this folder logic lives inside the mathematics note because the two share a method and a fate, but the maps treat their marriage very differently (Section 2).

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | Theoretical science (one of three, with physics and theology). Logic: not a science at all, a *tool* (the Organon, "instrument") |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | Mathematics: one of 5 areas of knowledge |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Natural sciences, 1.1 Mathematics (logic hides in 1.2 computer and information sciences, or in philosophy) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | คณิตศาสตร์ (Mathematics): its own learning area |
| Dewey Decimal | 510 (Mathematics); logic at 160, under Philosophy |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Natural sciences, mathematics and statistics (broad field 05) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Mathematics (12), with **Logic (11) as a separate field** |

Two observations the table is begging to make. First: mathematics is the **only domain with a claim in every map**, which is worth sitting with. Every other domain is missing from at least one worldview; mathematics is the common currency. Second: logic's status is a running disagreement. UNESCO gives it its own field, the first field in the whole nomenclature. Aristotle thought it was not knowledge but an instrument for getting knowledge. Dewey files it under philosophy. The OECD hides it inside computing. A domain's prestige in a map says as much about the map as about the domain.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **proof**. Not experiment, not testimony, not interpretation: a chain of valid inference from axioms to conclusion, checkable by anyone, forever. This is what makes mathematics the formal family's home, and it is why the "how do you know?" regress of [[02_root-of-all-knowledge]] behaves differently here: the chain legitimately stops at axioms, and the interesting question becomes *which* axioms (the fork that gave birth to non-Euclidean geometry, that note's Section 6).

In Biglan's terms mathematics is **hard-pure**: high agreement, convergent, aimed at understanding. Practitioners converge on settled questions (no one re-argues the Pythagorean theorem) and grow the field at its frontiers. But the culture hides a secret: working mathematicians run on **knowing-how** as much as knowing-that. There is taste for the right definition, feel for which lemma will fall, the "seeing" of a proof before writing it. The theorems are propositional; the craft that finds them is a skill, which is exactly why mathematics is learned by doing problems, not by reading answers.

A good day in the domain: you are lost in a structure, you try three approaches, two fail cleanly, the third collapses into a proof you can check line by line. Error-correction is total: a wrong step is simply wrong, and the community's criticism ([[04_logical-fallacies]] in pure form) is unambiguous.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[20_philosophy]]: the foundations seam. Is mathematics a branch of logic (logicism, Frege and Russell), an empirical science of patterns (Mill), or a free creation (formalism, intuitionism)? These are philosophy questions wearing mathematics clothes.
- [[02_physics]]: Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics": why does abstract structure describe the physical world so well? Physics treats mathematics as its language; mathematics treats physics as its best customer.
- [[09_computing-and-ict]]: computability and complexity are mathematics' children (Turing was a logician); discrete mathematics and algorithms are the shared border.
- [[03_chemistry]], [[04_life-sciences]]: the applied mathematics seam: once math models a domain's phenomena, whose knowledge is the model?

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The formal family's practice is **problems and proofs**, and the practitioner's deed is proving. Where language learning says "structure, then immersion," mathematics says "definitions, then struggle": rebuild the proofs yourself, do the exercises without peeking, and check every step, because in this domain error-correction is cheap and total. Reading mathematics without doing it is the formal equivalent of watching a language video and calling it fluency. The spacing and retrieval of [[05_dunning-kruger-effect]]'s calibration apply here unusually cleanly: you always find out exactly how much you know the moment you try the problem.

## Key takeaways

- Mathematics is the only domain all seven maps claim: the common currency of knowledge maps.
- Its method is proof: claims earn trust by valid inference from axioms, and the regress problem behaves differently here.
- Logic is the telling seam: UNESCO field 11 for one map, a mere tool for Aristotle, a branch of philosophy for Dewey, invisible inside computing for the OECD.
- Discovered or invented is the wrong question: the structure-constrained, mind-built answer is "grown" ([[02_root-of-all-knowledge]]).
- To learn it is to do it: the mathematician's deed is proving, so problems and rebuilt proofs beat reading, every time.
