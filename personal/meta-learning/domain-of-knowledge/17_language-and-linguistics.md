# Language and Linguistics

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is language and linguistics as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

This is the double domain where Ryle's cut lives inside one territory: **knowing-how** (speaking a language) and **knowing-that** (linguistics, the science of language). Aristotle filed the territory under productive (rhetoric and poetics), the crafts of using words well; the IB Theory of Knowledge guide demotes language to an optional theme rather than an area; the Thai curriculum splits it into two learning areas, ภาษาไทย (Thai Language) and ภาษาต่างประเทศ (Foreign Languages). Two learning paths follow from the split: immersion (learn some sentence structure then go talk to the community) and analysis (study the structure itself). What the placement tells the learner: you can master this domain in either register, and they are not the same skill.

## 1. What the domain studies

Language is both a human capacity and a set of structures, and the domain studies both. Linguistics asks what makes utterances possible at all: phonology (sound), morphology (word building), syntax (structure), semantics (meaning), pragmatics (use), plus how languages are acquired, how they change, and how they vary across communities. Its characteristic question is "**what makes this utterance possible, and why does this one sound wrong to every speaker?**"

The skill side asks a different question entirely: not why sentences work, but whether you can make them work. A person who speaks Thai knows things about Thai no grammar book contains, and cannot state most of them. That gap is not a failure; it is the shape of the domain.

Progress therefore looks different on each side. For linguistics: a grammar that explains more data, a documented language, a model of change or acquisition that survives criticism. For the skill: fluency, range, and the moment a community stops hearing you as a visitor. The maps' disagreements about this domain are, at bottom, disagreements about which of these two counts as knowledge.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | productive (rhetoric and poetics) |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | language is a TOK optional theme, not an area |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Humanities 6.2 (languages and literature) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | ภาษาไทย (Thai Language) + ภาษาต่างประเทศ (Foreign Languages): TWO learning areas |
| Dewey Decimal | 400 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Arts and humanities (02) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Linguistics 57 |

The most interesting comparison is between Aristotle and the IB guide. Aristotle's category is **productive**: rhetoric and poetics are crafts of making (speeches, poems), knowing-how with words aimed at a product. The IB guide can neither promote nor demote language: it is not an area of knowledge like history or mathematics but a theme to think with, a lens that runs through everything else. Between those two cells lies the whole question: is language a tool for getting knowledge, or an object of knowledge in its own right?

The Thai curriculum quietly answers by doubling: TWO learning areas, one for the mother tongue and one for foreign languages, with the skill and the study sharing each. UNESCO answers the other way, giving Linguistics its own field (57) and treating the science of language as the knowledge while fluency remains a competence. When maps disagree this much, look for Ryle's cut: they are not classifying the same thing.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here depends on the register. For the skill: **immersion and use**. You learn a language the way its speakers did: some sentence structure, then talk to the community, and let a million small corrections do their work. For linguistics: **data and analysis**. Claims earn trust when they account for native-speaker judgments, corpora, and fieldwork recordings, and when rival analyses are ruled out by the data.

In Biglan's terms linguistics is **soft-pure**: divergent, particularistic in data, aimed at understanding. But the culture hides its own double: fieldwork linguists live like anthropologists, corpus linguists like data scientists, and theoretical syntacticians like mathematicians looking for the rule behind the judgment. A good day differs by side: for the speaker, a conversation in which you were understood, at length, without translation in your head; for the linguist, a set of data that cleanly kills one analysis of a structure. Error-correction on the skill side is instant and generous (misunderstanding is its own feedback); on the science side it is peer review, slow and adversarial ([[04_logical-fallacies]] in careful dress).

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[18_literature]]: the seam where language becomes material for making. Linguistics studies the code; literature makes works out of it. Aristotle put rhetoric and poetics together for a reason.
- [[14_anthropology]]: linguistic anthropology studies language as social life: identity, power, community. The same recordings, questions about people rather than structure.
- [[20_philosophy]]: philosophy of language: meaning, reference, truth, and the question of how words hook onto the world. Linguistics describes the mechanism; philosophy interrogates the connection.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The domain's practice looks like its practitioners' deed, and the deed is doubled. For the skill: learn some sentence structure, then go talk to the community; immersion is the knowing-how path and no amount of grammar substitutes for it. For linguistics: collect data, argue analyses, and let speaker judgments discipline your theory. The two paths reinforce each other more than learners expect (a linguist learns languages faster, a speaker intuits structure), but neither one completes the other, and this domain is where [[05_dunning-kruger-effect]] bites hardest: fluent speakers confidently state false things about their own language.

## Key takeaways

- This is Ryle's cut inside one territory: speaking a language is knowing-how; linguistics is knowing-that; both live in the 400s.
- Aristotle calls the territory productive (rhetoric and poetics); the IB guide calls language a theme, not an area; the Thai curriculum splits it in two.
- Two learning paths follow: immersion (structure, then the community) and analysis (structure as the object).
- Linguistics earns trust like a science of data; the skill earns trust like any craft: by use.
- To learn it is to do it, and "it" means both: talk to the community and interrogate the utterances.
