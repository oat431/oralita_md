# Law

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is law as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Law studies rules, rights, and the settlement of disputes by making **two moves at once**: interpretation (what does the rule say?) and normative argument (what should it say?). Aristotle filed it under practical knowledge within politics; the OECD under social sciences; ISCED under business and law: three maps, three homes. Its practice is the **case method**: argue from decided cases, distinguish unwelcome ones, and build doctrine incrementally. Law is the seam between knowledge and profession, where a domain must both understand the world and change it on a deadline.

## 1. What the domain studies

Law studies the rules a community makes binding: what they say, how they are made, interpreted, enforced, justified. Its questions combine two registers. The interpretive: what does this statute or precedent mean, on these facts? The normative: what rule **ought** to govern, given fairness, precedent, and the doctrine's purposes?

The double move is the signature: every legal argument is partly hermeneutic (reading a text or a line of cases), partly evaluative (arguing what the rule should be). Neither half alone is law: interpretation without principle is casuistry, principle without texts is political philosophy.

Progress is distinctive: a better-reasoned judgment, a distinction that stabilizes doctrine, a statute that resolves a live conflict, a remedy that works. Law's results are decisions that bind people, not descriptions of them, and they are tested in disputes where someone wins and someone loses: approximately right is not enough.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | practical (part of politics for Aristotle) |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | Human sciences (politics theme) |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Social sciences 5.5 (law: the OECD files law under SOCIAL SCIENCES) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | สังคมศึกษา (Social Studies) |
| Dewey Decimal | 340 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Business, administration and law (04) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Juridical Sciences and Law 56 |

Every map files this domain differently, and the disagreement is the interesting part. The OECD places law under **social sciences** (5.5), a field of inquiry about social rules; ISCED under **business, administration and law (04)**, a professional field whose graduates practice a trade; UNESCO gives it a field of its own, Juridical Sciences and Law 56, neither social science nor profession but a named science.

That is the seam between knowledge and profession made visible. Is law a science that studies norms, or training for a practice that applies them? Aristotle's answer, practical knowledge within politics, anticipates the tension exactly: legal knowledge is knowledge *for doing*, judged by the quality of the decisions it produces.

## 3. Its method and culture

Method family: **interpretive and normative**. Claims earn trust through argument from authority (constitutions, statutes, precedent) and principle (fairness, purpose, rights), assembled in the **case method**: take a real dispute, argue both sides, show why one reading is better. Distinguishing cases is the core skill: finding the fact that makes an unwelcome precedent inapplicable without overruling it.

In Biglan's terms law is **soft-applied**: divergent in theory, convergent in practice, aimed at application under constraint. A good day: a hard case yields an argument faithful to the text, fair to both parties, and workable as a rule for the next case. Error-correction is adversarial by design: opposing counsel and appellate review are the built-in criticism engine, and the record is public.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[13_political-science]]: the sovereignty seam. Where does political power end and legal rule begin? For Aristotle the two were one subject; constitutions keep them entangled.
- [[21_ethics]]: the legitimacy seam. Law asks what may be compelled, ethics what should be done: an unjust law is the border case where the two domains collide.
- [[25_business-and-management]]: the compliance seam: regulation is law applied to markets, and every organization lives at this border.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The practice here is the **case method**, and the practitioner's deed is arguing a case: marshal the authorities, anticipate the counterargument, state a workable rule. Law is learned by doing advocacy and reading judgments closely: the skill is applying a general rule to contested facts while someone competent argues the other side. [[06_disagreeing-without-rage]] is native equipment here: the domain institutionalizes disagreement.

## Key takeaways

- Law makes two moves at once: interpretation of what the rule says, and normative argument about what it should.
- Every map files it differently: social science for the OECD, profession for ISCED, own science for UNESCO, practical knowledge for Aristotle: the knowledge-versus-profession seam.
- Its practice is the case method, and distinguishing cases is the core skill.
- Error-correction is adversarial by design: opposition and appeal are the criticism engine.
- To learn it is to argue it: cases, briefs, and close reading of judgments, not doctrine summaries.
