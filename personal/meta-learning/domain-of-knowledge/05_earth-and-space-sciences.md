# Earth and Space Sciences

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What are the earth and space sciences as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

This domain studies the earth and the cosmos: stars and planets, oceans and atmosphere, the solid earth and the climate system that rides on all of it. It is the one folder where the maps force an explicit merger: UNESCO gives astronomy its own field (21) and earth science another (25), and this folder joins them at mid resolution. Astronomy is among the oldest observational sciences; climate science is among the newest problem-driven ones, and their methods show the difference. Every map files them under natural science, but geography has a rival claim on the earth half (Section 4).

## 1. What the domain studies

The earth and space sciences study the planet we stand on and the universe around it: rocks, oceans, ice, air, climate, planets, stars, galaxies. Its characteristic questions are "what is out there?", "how does this planet work?", and "what will this system do next?": description first, then mechanism, then forecast.

What counts as progress: a model that explains a body of observations, a dataset that constrains a theory, a forecast that verifies, a picture of a process no one has seen (the interior of the earth, the surface of a distant world). The domain's hallmark is that **experiment is mostly unavailable**: no one reruns a past climate or rearranges a star. Evidence is therefore inference from traces: light, rocks, ice, seismic waves, and the deep time that gives the earth its history. The compensation is scope: these sciences study systems too large or too old for the laboratory, and they built the modern art of modeling in order to do it.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | theoretical (astronomy within natural philosophy) |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | Natural sciences |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Natural sciences 1.5 (earth and related environmental sciences) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | วิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยี (Science and Technology) |
| Dewey Decimal | 520 (astronomy) and 550 (earth sciences) |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Natural sciences, mathematics and statistics (05) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Astronomy and Astrophysics 21 + Earth and Space Sciences 25 (TWO fields; this folder merges them at mid resolution: say so) |

The table contains the folder's most interesting fracture: it is the only row with **two UNESCO fields**, and Dewey splits the domain the same way (520 and 550). UNESCO nomenclature 1973 draws the seam between Astronomy and Astrophysics 21 and Earth and Space Sciences 25; this folder merges them at mid resolution, and the merge is honest about one fact and dishonest about another. Honest: both are observational natural sciences that infer mechanisms from traces, and a planetary scientist works on Mars and the earth with the same toolkit. Dishonest: astronomy is in some ways the oldest observational science there is, with a lineage of careful sky-watching running back before the scientific revolution, while earth science as a modern domain is young, and its newest branch, climate science, is transdisciplinary and problem-driven in exactly the sense of Mode 2 in [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]].

That second point is the placement worth dwelling on. Astronomy grew up as pure knowledge of what is out there; climate science grew up as knowledge in service of a problem humans face. Same natural-science field, opposite relationship to application: the maps cannot show that difference at their resolution.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **observation plus model**, with the past as the laboratory. Inference from traces is the core skill: reading light, rocks, ice, and waves for the processes that made them. Models are not decoration but instruments: since experiments are unavailable, the domain tests understanding by asking what a model predicts and checking against new data (the sky always provides another observation; the earth always provides another record).

In Biglan's terms this is **hard-pure at the core with hard-applied edges**: high consensus, convergent, aimed at understanding, but with wings (resources, weather forecasting, hazard assessment, climate policy) whose question is what to do. The culture is observant and patient: fieldwork, telescopes, long instrument records, and the collaboration of teams around instruments and datasets. The **knowing-how** is in the reading: the geologist's eye for a contact, the astronomer's feel for a spectrum, the modeler's judgment about which simplification is honest.

A good day in the domain: a new dataset lands exactly where two rival models disagree and settles the argument, or a seismic signal reveals a boundary inside the earth that no one will ever see. Error-correction is real but slow: records are short, processes are many, and the system under study does not repeat itself on request.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[02_physics]]: the laws seam. Astrophysics and geophysics are physics applied to the sky and the deep earth: the tools are borrowed, but the objects are named and historicized.
- [[04_life-sciences]]: the living earth seam. Ecosystems, the history of life, and the coevolution of planet and biosphere are claims shared with biology.
- [[15_geography]]: the double-home seam. The earth's surface and its landscapes are studied here and in geography, and the difference is the deed: physical processes here, human-environment relations and spatial patterns there.
- [[01_mathematics]]: the modeling seam. The models run on applied mathematics, and the border question is always whose knowledge the model is.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The practitioner's deed is **reading traces and building models**, so the practice that builds the domain looks like that: work datasets until the pattern jumps out at you, rebuild the classic models to see what they can and cannot say, and go outside to read a landscape or a sky with someone who knows how. Because experiment is scarce here, judgment about evidence is the trained skill: which trace is decisive, which record is too short to say. Calibration matters enormously, since a forecast is a promise the world will grade later.

## Key takeaways

- One domain, two UNESCO fields: astronomy 21 and earth science 25 are merged here at mid resolution, and the merge hides as much as it fixes.
- Astronomy is among the oldest observational sciences; climate science is among the newest problem-driven ones: same field of the maps, opposite relation to application.
- The method is observation plus model, with the past as the laboratory: no experiments, so evidence is inference from traces.
- The geography seam is the double-home problem: the same planet studied by two domains whose deeds differ.
- To learn it is to do it: the earth and space scientist's deed is reading traces and testing models, in the field and at the screen.
