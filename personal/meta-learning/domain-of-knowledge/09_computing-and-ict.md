# Computing and ICT

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is computing and ICT as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Computing and ICT studies **information, computation, and the systems that process them**: algorithms, programs, networks, and digital infrastructure. It is the map-test domain: born between logic and engineering after most maps were drawn, so every map files it differently, and Dewey gives it 000, the class for knowledge too new to classify. Its method family is double: algorithms proven like mathematics (formal), systems tested like engineering (design). The seam at its center: is computing mathematics, engineering, or science?

## 1. What the domain studies

Computing studies what can be computed, how efficiently, and how to build the machines that do it. Its questions are "is this problem solvable?", "how fast?", "does this system behave correctly?", "what happens at scale?" The theory side studies algorithms, complexity, and information; the practice side builds software and networks that billions of people depend on. ICT is the applied umbrella: the systems and connections through which information moves.

What counts as progress is split by this double nature. In theory: a new algorithm, a proof that a problem is hard, an impossibility result. In practice: a system that works, scales, and stays up. The field is young enough that whole paradigms rise and fall within one career: no map has made up its mind about what the domain *is*.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | - (no home) |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | technology theme (no area) |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | split between Natural sciences 1.2 (computer and information sciences) and Engineering and technology 2 |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | วิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยี (Science and Technology) + การงานอาชีพ (Career and Technology) |
| Dewey Decimal | 000 (the general class!) |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Information and Communication Technologies (06) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | - (no home: the field was born after 1973) |

This is the row to stare at. Aristotle has no home for computing, and neither does UNESCO: the 1973 nomenclature predates the field's arrival, and computing cannot be retrofitted without distortion. The OECD splits it between natural science and engineering, admitting the double identity. Dewey's 000 is the most delightful placement in the crosswalk: the general class, the shelf for knowledge too new to classify. A domain's classification failure is data: it shows where a map's joints were cut for an older world.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **proof for algorithms, testing for systems**. An algorithm's correctness can be proven like a theorem; a deployed system can only be tested, monitored, and patched. This dual citizenship makes computing the only domain that lives in two method families at once: **formal** (proof, models, logic) and **design** (build, test, fail, iterate). In Biglan's terms it straddles hard-pure theory and hard-applied practice, which is why departments, curricula, and maps keep fighting about where it belongs.

A good day: a bound tightens, a test suite goes green, a system survives its first real load. Error-correction is unusually cheap and well-instrumented: compilers catch you, tests catch you, monitors catch you. The culture's deepest habits are abstraction and modularity: name things well, hide complexity, make the pieces replaceable. The moral weight sits in reliability and security: computing's artifacts run the world's money, medicine, and elections, so "it works on my machine" is the culture's canonical joke about a failure it takes very seriously.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[01_mathematics]]: logic, computability, and complexity are mathematics' children, and the seam question is whether computing is applied mathematics with better funding.
- [[08_engineering-and-technology]]: the design half of the identity: is software engineered or written? Testing, reliability, and process cross this border daily.
- [[20_philosophy]]: the deepest seam: what is computation, what is information, and can a machine think? These are philosophy questions computing cannot answer with benchmarks.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

Computing's practice is **writing, running, and reasoning about code**, and the practitioner's deed is building things that execute. So learn it by making programs work and then making them right: write, run, break, and prove in rotation, because the domain rewards both the formal proof and the working demo. Pair reading with building from the first week: its youth means textbooks age fast.

## Key takeaways

- Computing is the map-test domain: born between logic and engineering, claimed differently by every map, homeless in Aristotle and UNESCO.
- Dewey gives it 000, the general class: the shelf for knowledge too new to classify.
- Its method is double: algorithms proven (formal), systems tested (design); practitioners need both vocabularies.
- The central seam is unsettled on purpose: is computing mathematics, engineering, or science?
- To learn it is to build and to reason: code that runs teaches one half, code proven correct teaches the other.
