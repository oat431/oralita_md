# Sociology and Demography

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is sociology and demography as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Sociology studies **social structures, institutions, and collective life**; demography studies **populations**: births, deaths, migration, and their arithmetic. This folder merges the two at mid resolution, though the maps keep them apart and UNESCO gives each its own field. Sociology is where the qualitative-quantitative divide runs deepest: the domain holds two method cultures under one roof and argues about it honestly. Demography is the population science, modest in theory and hard to argue with in evidence, and both share a habit of mind: explaining individual lives by locating them in patterns the individuals never chose.

## 1. What the domain studies

Sociology studies how people are organized into groups, classes, institutions, and networks, and how those shapes constrain individual lives. Its questions are "why does this pattern recur?", "who benefits?", "how do institutions reproduce themselves?", "what holds this group together?" Demography studies the same world through vital events: who is born, who dies, who moves. Where sociology sees structures, demography sees stocks and flows.

What counts as progress is dual: a concept that names a mechanism everyone then can see (socialization, stratification, institution), a survey that measures it, or a population projection that reframes a policy debate. The domain's special strength is denaturalization: arrangements taken as inevitable are contingent, historically made, and changeable. Its special risk is the mirror of that strength: totalizing stories that explain everything and predict nothing.

The tell is Aristotle's row: he had no separate sociology at all, only politics, because the idea that society is an object of science is modern. The maps that claim the domain mostly agree (human sciences, social sciences, the 300s), with one conspicuous split: **UNESCO gives Sociology (63) and Demography (52) two separate fields**, while this folder merges them at mid resolution, treating population as society's quantitative shadow. The Thai curriculum files everything under สังคมศึกษา (Social Studies): at school level, the social sciences are one subject taught by one teacher.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **evidence about patterns**: surveys, interviews, historical comparison, ethnography, and statistics, with the choice of method carrying ideological weight. The method family is hypothesis and experiment at the quantitative end and interpretation at the qualitative end, and the famous **qualitative versus quantitative divide** is the domain's internal seam rather than an embarrassment. In Biglan's terms sociology is **soft**, split between pure work (theory of social order) and applied work (policy, evaluation, social intervention).

Demography leans harder quantitative: measurement, projection, and the discipline of accounting identities, where the numbers are large and the arguments are about assumptions rather than arithmetic. A good day across the merged domain: a pattern survives controls and interviews alike, or a projection forces a government to face its future. Error-correction is peer review plus public contestation, with data archives and transparency as the quantitative wing's answer to doubt. The honest cultures let the two wings audit each other.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[13_political-science]]: power is the shared object: sociology explains how orders reproduce, political science studies how they are governed and contested.
- [[14_anthropology]]: the method seam: sociology's surveys and structures against anthropology's fieldwork and thick description, disputing who knows a culture better.
- [[11_economics]]: the neighbor with more models: economics claims the market mechanisms, sociology claims everything the models assume away.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The social science practice is **finding patterns in collective life**, and the practitioner's deed is tracing individual outcomes back to structures. So learn it by doing both kinds of evidence: run a small survey or analyze an open dataset, then treat the same topic with interviews or observation, because the divide is the domain. Demography adds a clean discipline: take a population table and derive its consequences yourself. Keep the denaturalizing gaze: when something looks inevitable, ask when it was made.

## Key takeaways

- Sociology studies structures and collective life; demography studies populations; UNESCO gives them fields 63 and 52 separately, and this folder merges them at mid resolution.
- Aristotle had no sociology at all: the idea that society is a scientific object is modern, which is why old maps file it under politics.
- The qualitative-quantitative divide is the domain's internal seam; both kinds of evidence matter: patterns with numbers, meanings with people.
