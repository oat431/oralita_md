# Ethics

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is ethics as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Ethics studies what we ought to do and what makes a life go well: it is the home of the **normative** method family, where claims are about what ought to be rather than what is. Aristotle's name for its distinctive competence is **phronesis**, judgment in context; its method weighs cases and principles toward equilibrium. Its deepest seam is the is-ought gap: facts never force values, so every applied domain concluding with "should" must cross this border. No map quite knows where to put it, and it leaks into all 24 others anyway.

## 1. What the domain studies

Ethics studies action, character, and value: what is right, what is good, what makes a person or institution admirable or blameworthy. Its questions are not "what will happen?" but "**what should we do?**", "what do we owe each other?", "what kind of person does this habit make me?"

Progress is subtle: better casuistry (clearer readings of hard cases), tighter principles, frameworks that survive counterexamples. Ethics does not converge on one theory: the major families (consequentialist, deontological, virtue-ethical) remain in productive competition after two millennia. That is not failure: it is the shape of a domain that disagrees about its own foundations.

The domain's engine is a fact that cannot be unlearned once seen: **facts never logically force values**. How the world is never entails how it should be. Ethics is where that gap is studied, named, and carefully bridged.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |

| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | practical |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | TOK's knowledge framework uses ethics as a lens; no area of its own |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Humanities 6.3 (philosophy, ethics and religion) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | EMBEDDED in สังคมศึกษา (Social Studies, as จริยธรรม ethics) |
| Dewey Decimal | 170 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Arts and humanities (02) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Ethics 71 (its own field) |

The most interesting placement is Aristotle's, and it is easy to misread. For Aristotle ethics is not a theoretical science of goodness but a **practical** inquiry: one of three kinds of knowledge, distinguished by what it is *for* (truth, good action, or making things). Its test is not a beautiful theory but a well-lived life; its crown competence is phronesis: perception of what this situation actually requires.

The maps echo this restlessness: TOK gives ethics no home at all, using it as a lens applicable to every area. Dewey gives it a class of its own (170), UNESCO a field of its own (71), Thai education a corner of สังคมศึกษา as จริยธรรม. Four maps, four strategies: normative knowledge refuses to sit still in a taxonomy of descriptive knowledge.

## 3. Its method and culture

Method family: **normative**. Claims earn trust through cases, arguments, and the weighing of principles in **reflective equilibrium**: adjust principle to fit judgment, judgment to principle, until the strain is minimal. Thought experiments (trolley problems, veils of ignorance) are instruments, not games: they isolate one variable of a moral intuition for examination.

In Biglan's terms ethics is **soft-pure** with a strong applied pull: low consensus, divergent, forever dragged toward practice. A good day: a hard case stops looking simple; an airtight principle meets a counterexample and gets rebuilt better. Error-correction is social and slow: the check is whether the argument survives the strongest objections.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[20_philosophy]]: the parent seam. Metaethics (what makes a moral claim true?) is pure philosophy; Aristotle counted ethics as a branch of it.
- [[23_law]]: the enforcement seam. Law asks what may be compelled; ethics asks what should be done: they overlap constantly and diverge at the is-ought line.
- [[06_medicine-and-health]]: the applied seam, ethics' widest leak: consent, triage, end-of-life care import normative reasoning into a domain that cannot derive it from evidence.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The normative family's practice is **cases and arguments**, and the practitioner's deed is judgment: take a real dilemma, apply your best principles, notice where they fight, and say plainly which way you came out and why. Ethics is learned by wrestling with cases, not memorizing theories: a theory you cannot apply to a hard case is a phrase, not knowledge. Because every conclusion here carries "should," learning it means showing your work, so people who share your facts but not your values can see where the disagreement lives.

## Key takeaways

- Ethics is the normative family's home: claims about what ought to be, not what is, and no fact forces a value.
- Its competence is phronesis, judgment in context, which is why cases teach it better than theories.
- Every map relocates it: practical for Aristotle, a lens for TOK, a field for UNESCO, a class for Dewey, embedded in สังคมศึกษา for Thai education.
- It leaks into every applied domain: any domain concluding with "should" borrowed its last step from here.
- To learn it is to judge it: wrestle with hard cases and show which premise moved you.
