# Engineering and Technology

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is engineering and technology as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Engineering and technology studies the **design and making of things that work**: structures, machines, processes, and systems that serve human purposes under real constraints. It is the home of the design method family: build, test, fail, iterate, where claims earn trust by surviving use. Its error-correction culture is the **design review**, its ethics safety and trade-offs: every working design is a compromise, and maps call this productive knowledge, a compliment about usefulness and a slight about purity.

## 1. What the domain studies

Engineering studies how to make things that work: not only once, but reliably, affordably, safely, and at scale. Its characteristic questions are "can we build it?", "what fails first?", "how well does it hold?", and "what does it cost?" Technology is the wider half: the tools, systems, and techniques themselves, plus the know-how to run them.

What counts as progress is a design that works better than the last one, a failure mode engineered away, a process made cheaper or safer. Knowledge here is tested, not proven: prototypes, load tests, failure analysis, field performance. The price is scope: engineering knowledge is always about *this* design under *these* constraints.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | productive |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | technology theme (no area) |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Engineering and technology 2 |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | วิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยี (Science and Technology) + การงานอาชีพ (Career and Technology): split between two learning areas |
| Dewey Decimal | 620 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Engineering, manufacturing and construction (07) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Technological Sciences 33 |

Two placements are worth sitting with. First, Aristotle's: engineering is **productive** knowledge, the knowledge of making (*poiesis*), ranked below theoretical and practical knowledge because its end is the artifact, not understanding or conduct; modern maps rename it applied, technological, professional. Second, the Thai curriculum splits the domain across วิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยี (Science and Technology) and การงานอาชีพ (Career and Technology), separating the analytic from the vocational halves of the practice. Both moves say the same thing: maps find it hard to treat making as knowledge in its own right.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: the **design method**. Build, test, fail, iterate. A design is not proven like a theorem or confirmed like a hypothesis; it is validated against requirements and revised against failure. This is the design family at full strength: the culture treats failure as information rather than shame, because a good failure early is worth a thousand arguments.

In Biglan's terms engineering is **hard-applied**: high agreement on what counts as working, aimed at application, convergent on solutions under constraints. A good day: your test rig shows where the design is weakest, a review board tears into your assumptions before users meet them, and the iteration you ship survives tomorrow. The design review is the culture's error-correction ritual: peers, checklists, and standing skepticism aimed at the artifact, not the person. And because artifacts can kill when they fail, "good enough" is quantified and bounded: safety and trade-offs sit at the craft's center.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[02_physics]]: the science-engineering seam: physics supplies the laws, engineering supplies the constraints, and the border dispute is over whose knowledge a working device is. Science asks why, engineering asks how well.
- [[09_computing-and-ict]]: software is the newest engineering material, and the seam is live: is a program engineered or written? Testing and reliability cross the border constantly.
- [[06_medicine-and-health]]: the body as design problem: prosthetics, imaging machines, implants. When an engineered artifact becomes part of a patient, whose competence governs?

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The design family's practice is **building and breaking**, and the practitioner's deed is making things work under constraints. So learn it by building: choose a project with real limits, test it to failure, and iterate, because you only find out how much you know when the artifact meets reality.

## Key takeaways

- Engineering and technology is the home of the design method family: build, test, fail, iterate, trust earned by surviving use.
- Aristotle ranked it productive knowledge; modern maps keep the slight and rename it applied.
- Its error-correction culture is the design review; its ethics are safety and trade-offs.

- To learn it is to build it: the practitioner's deed is making things work, so projects beat textbooks.
