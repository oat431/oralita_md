# Economics

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is economics as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Economics studies **production, exchange, and the allocation of scarce resources**: prices, markets, money, growth, and policy. It is the social science with the most physics-envy, dressing its claims in models, mathematics, and prediction, and its maps mostly file it among the social sciences, though ISCED groups it with business and law. Its method family is hypothesis and experiment pushed toward the formal end: models are built like theorems and judged by their predictions, and its forecast record is its great calibration lesson: prediction failure is an invitation to hold models loosely.

## 1. What the domain studies

Economics studies how people and societies produce, distribute, and consume goods under scarcity. Its questions are "what is this worth?", "who bears this cost?", "what happens if we change the rule?", "why are some countries rich and others poor?" Microeconomics watches prices, incentives, and firms; macroeconomics watches output, employment, and money. Its unit of analysis is often the model: a simplified world where trade-offs are visible.

What counts as progress is a model that clarifies, a prediction that survives data, or a policy evaluated honestly. The domain's characteristic power is disciplined simplification: assuming people are rational gets you far enough to say useful things, until it doesn't. Its characteristic risk is mistaking the map for the territory: its models are clean in a way economies never are.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | practical (oikonomia, household management) |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | Human sciences |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Social sciences 5.2 (economics and business) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | สังคมศึกษา (Social Studies) + การงานอาชีพ (Career and Technology) |
| Dewey Decimal | 330 |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Business, administration and law (04) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Economic Sciences 53 |

The placement worth sitting with is Aristotle's: economics begins as **practical** knowledge, *oikonomia*, the art of managing a household well: knowledge for doing, not for knowing. Modern economics completed a famous promotion into a theoretical, model-building science, and the Thai curriculum preserves the older split in miniature, filing economics under สังคมศึกษา (Social Studies) and its vocational applications under การงานอาชีพ (Career and Technology). ISCED files the whole domain under Business, administration and law (04), which would horrify a theorist and please a practitioner: one domain, two reputations.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **models confronted with data**. The method family is hypothesis and experiment in its formal register: build a model with explicit assumptions, derive predictions, test them against evidence, with natural experiments and randomized trials as the strongest currency. In Biglan's terms economics is split down the middle: theory is **soft-pure** (clean models, deductive elegance, high prestige), policy and applied work **soft-applied** (messy evidence, contested values, real stakes). The two wings read each other with polite suspicion.

A good day: an identification strategy actually identifies, a prediction is confirmed out of sample, a policy evaluation changes someone's mind. Error-correction is where the domain learned its best lessons: forecast failures, missed crises, and replication troubles pushed economics toward pre-registration, transparency, and humility about causal claims. The culture runs on trade-off thinking: every policy has a cost, every intervention has an incidence, and someone always pays. That habit is the domain's real gift to its students, more durable than any model.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[25_business-and-management]]: the theory-practice seam: economics studies the system, business studies the firm, and the border moves with whoever has the better data.
- [[13_political-science]]: policy is where the two domains are one: every economic rule is a political choice, and every polity has an economy.
- [[01_mathematics]]: the methods seam: economics imports mathematics so heavily that the question of whether a proof about a stylized world says anything about the real one never quite goes away.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The social science practice here is **modeling and testing**, and the practitioner's deed is building models and checking their predictions against the world. So learn it by deriving a model's predictions yourself and then hunting for the data that could embarrass them: this domain punishes those who fall in love with their own assumptions. Forecast, record, score yourself, and let the misses teach you what the models cannot.

## Key takeaways

- Economics began as practical knowledge, household management, and became the most model-hungry social science: the maps record both identities.
- Its method is models confronted with data: explicit assumptions, testable predictions, honesty about causation.
- Biglan's split is real: theory is soft-pure, policy is soft-applied, and the two wings rarely share a culture.
- Prediction failure is the calibration lesson; to learn it, derive the prediction, then find the evidence that could break it.
