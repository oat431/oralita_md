# Psychology

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is psychology as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Psychology studies **mind and behavior**: perception, learning, emotion, development, personality, and disorder. It lives on the fault line between natural science and human science, fighting an identity war between experiments and interpretation for its whole modern life. Its error-correction culture is unusually self-conscious, because its subject matter includes the biases of its own researchers: replication and calibration are the discipline's answer to its own instrument. The maps scatter it: philosophy for Dewey, human sciences for TOK, social sciences for the OECD, and no home in Thai basic education.

## 1. What the domain studies

Psychology studies how organisms sense, think, feel, develop, and act. Its questions are "why do people behave this way?", "what produces this experience?", "how does this develop?", "can this be changed?" It spans levels at once: neurons and narratives, milliseconds and lifetimes, the laboratory and the clinic.

What counts as progress is plural: a replicated effect, a validated measure, a therapy that beats placebo, a theory that unifies findings. The domain's special vulnerability is that its object of study studies back: demand effects, self-report, and theorist bias are permanent hazards. This is why psychology became the domain most disciplined about **replication** and **calibration**, treating its own practitioners' overconfidence as a variable to be measured.

The scatter is the story. Aristotle files the soul's study inside natural philosophy; Dewey files it under philosophy at 150; the OECD and ISCED bury it inside the social sciences, while cognitive science strains toward the brain. Only UNESCO gives it its own field outright, and the Thai curriculum makes it a guest inside other subjects. Compare this row with [[01_mathematics]], which every map claims and most maps crown. Psychology is the opposite case: a domain every map has heard of and no map agrees about, because its subject sits where the natural and the human meet.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **controlled experiment, measurement, and replication**, alongside interpretation and case study in its softer reaches. No single method family owns the domain: hypothesis and experiment dominate the laboratory wing, while the interpretive wing borrows from the humanities and the clinic runs on judgment tested against outcomes. In Biglan's terms psychology is genuinely **borderline**: cognitive neuroscience is hard-pure in spirit, clinical practice is soft-applied, and social psychology sits in between, which is why psychologists from different wings can barely read each other's papers.

A good day: a pre-registered study replicates, an effect survives the controls that should have killed it, a measure predicts something outside the lab. The culture's error-correction is its proudest achievement and its hardest lesson: after its replication crises, the domain rebuilt itself around pre-registration, open data, and calibrated claims. Knowing about cognitive bias is professional hygiene here, and the gap between feeling right and being right is itself an object of study.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[04_life-sciences]]: the brain seam: is psychology biology plus statistics? Neuroscience claims the mechanisms; psychology claims the mind that mechanisms alone do not explain.
- [[12_sociology-and-demography]]: the individual-society seam: where does the person end and the group begin? Social psychology is the disputed territory.
- [[20_philosophy]]: the oldest seam of all: mind, consciousness, free will, and the reliability of introspection are philosophy questions psychology inherits and cannot dissolve with experiments.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The social science practice is **evidence against intuition**, and the practitioner's deed is testing claims about the mind, including your own. So learn it by running small studies and auditing your own judgments: design a simple experiment, check a measure's reliability, keep a calibration journal. Its hardest habit is the one [[05_dunning-kruger-effect]] describes: hold your self-assessment loosely until the numbers move.

## Key takeaways

- Psychology studies mind and behavior on the fault line between natural and human science, and its identity war is built into the maps.
- The crosswalk scatters it: philosophy for Dewey, human sciences for TOK, social sciences for the OECD, absent from Thai basic education, its own field only for UNESCO.
- Its error-correction culture is replication and calibration: the domain that studies bias had to institutionalize defenses against its own.
- Biglan's borderline status is real: cognitive neuroscience and clinical practice are different method cultures under one name.
- To learn it is to test claims, including claims about yourself: treat your confidence as a measured variable.
