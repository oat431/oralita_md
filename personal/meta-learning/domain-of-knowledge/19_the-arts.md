# The Arts

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What are the arts as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

The arts study and practice making: music, visual art, dance, theatre, design, film, and the rest of the productive family Aristotle called **poiesis**. This is the home of practice-based knowledge, and its deepest property is tacit knowledge: the artist's deed carries a layer that cannot be fully said, only shown (Polanyi's unsayable layer is thickest here). The culture's institution is the studio and the crit: critique as error-correction where nobody can cite a proof. The maps argue over its identity: UNESCO merges the arts with literature into Arts and Letters 62, while Dewey (700s) and the Thai curriculum (ศิลปะ (Arts)) keep them apart. What the placement tells the learner: this domain is learned the way it is practiced, by making, and knowledge OF art is not knowledge BY art.

## 1. What the domain studies

The arts study human making and its products: how a work is composed, performed, rendered, staged, and what a work does to those who meet it. The characteristic questions are paired like literature's but turned toward the studio: "**how do I make this work?**" and "what is this work doing that I did not intend?" One question faces the maker's bench; the other faces the audience, the critic, and the art historian.

That pairing hides the domain's most important division: knowledge **OF** art (history, analysis, criticism: propositional knowledge about works) versus knowledge **BY** art (practice: the knowing embodied in making a work). The maps mostly track the first half, because it classifies like a humanities discipline. The second half lives in studios and conservatories, where the deed is the curriculum.

Progress in the arts looks like works: a piece that reopens what the form could do, a performance that re-sees a classic, a technique taught and passed on. The error-correction is unusual and worth naming: the crit, the rehearsal, the exhibition, and the audience are the tests, and they are real tests even though nothing is proved or falsified. A work that fails does so in public, and the maker must know why.

## 2. Who claims it

| Map | What it calls it / where it sits |
|---|---|
| Aristotle, c. 350 BCE | productive (poiesis) |
| IB Theory of Knowledge, 2022 | The arts (its own area) |
| OECD Frascati, 2007 | Humanities 6.4 (arts) |
| Thai Basic Education, 2551 | ศิลปะ (Arts) |
| Dewey Decimal | 700s |
| ISCED-F 2013 | Arts and humanities (02) |
| UNESCO nomenclature, 1973 | Arts and Letters 62 (MERGED with literature!) |

The merger cell is the argument of the table. UNESCO **merges** the arts with literature into Arts and Letters 62, treating verbal and nonverbal art as one field; Dewey keeps the arts at 700s and literature at 800s; the Thai curriculum gives ศิลปะ (Arts) its own learning area. The IB Theory of Knowledge guide, meanwhile, gives The arts its own area of knowledge, which quietly claims something stronger than "nice to have": that art is a way of knowing.

Aristotle's cell is the oldest and clearest: **productive**, poiesis is making, knowledge that lives in the capacity to bring something into being. Notice what that classification does: it ranks the arts by their deed, not by their content. A map that files the arts under humanities (the OECD) is classifying art history and criticism; a map that files them under productive is classifying the studio. The same domain, two different objects of attention.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **practice and critique**. In the studio, a claim is a work, and it earns trust by surviving the crit: the structured, often brutal session where peers and teachers take the work apart and the maker defends or revises. In art history and theory, trust is earned by evidence and argument as in any interpretive discipline. The method family is practice-based and creative: the proof of the pudding is the pudding.

In Biglan's terms the arts are **soft-pure** in aim and soft-applied in practice: divergent, particularistic, aimed at both understanding and production. A good day in the domain: hours in the studio or the rehearsal room, something not working, then a revision that suddenly does, followed by criticism that shows you what you actually made. The tacit layer is the culture's signature: Polanyi's point that we can show more than we can say, that the hand knows things the mouth cannot report, which is exactly why the crit and apprenticeship replace lecture and exam.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[18_literature]]: the merger seam from the other side. UNESCO folds the two together (Arts and Letters 62); Dewey and the Thai curriculum split them. Is a poem an artwork with words or a domain of study? The classification never quite settles.
- [[17_language-and-linguistics]]: word-based arts (poetry, drama, song, calligraphy) sit on the border of making with language and making out of language.
- [[20_philosophy]]: aesthetics is the seam where philosophy asks what beauty, expression, and artistic value are: the arts' experience, philosophy's question.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The domain's practice looks like its practitioners' deed: **making, showing, revising**. You learn the arts in the studio, by making works, putting them before critics, and rebuilding from what the criticism finds, because tacit knowledge is transmitted by demonstration and apprenticeship rather than by text. Art history and theory round out the knowledge OF the domain, but they cannot substitute for knowledge BY it. Anyone who has sat through a good crit knows the domain's calibration lesson too: you learn exactly what your work is doing the moment someone else looks at it.

## Key takeaways

- The arts are productive knowledge (Aristotle's poiesis): knowledge that lives in making, not only in saying.
- Tacit knowledge is thickest here: the artist's deed has a layer that can only be shown, which is why the crit and apprenticeship are the curriculum.
- The maps vote on identity: UNESCO merges the arts with literature (62), Dewey and the Thai curriculum split them, the IB guide calls The arts an area of knowledge.
- Knowledge OF art (history, criticism) and knowledge BY art (practice) are two different things, and only the second is learned by making.
- To learn it is to do it: make the work, show it, take the criticism, revise.
