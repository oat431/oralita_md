# Physics

> **Created:** 2026-10-17
> **Part of:** [[00_domain-of-knowledge-overview]] (the crosswalk index) and [[07_kinds-of-knowledge]] (the map theory).
> **The question:** What is physics as a domain of knowledge, where do the maps place it, and what does that placement tell us about how it is learned?

## TL;DR

Physics studies matter, energy, motion, and the forces that govern them: the search for the smallest set of laws from which the widest range of phenomena follows. It is the paradigm case of the **empirical** method family and the textbook example of Biglan **hard-pure**: high consensus, convergent methods, theory and experiment in permanent negotiation. Every map files it under natural science without hedging (UNESCO's Physics 22, Dewey 530, Frascati's physical sciences at 1.3): physics is the science the category "natural science" was built around. Its deepest internal seam, theory versus experiment, is a real culture divide, and it shapes how the domain is learned.

## 1. What the domain studies

Physics studies the fundamental behavior of the physical world: motion, force, energy, matter, heat, light, electricity, and the structures underneath them, from atoms to fields to spacetime. Its characteristic questions are "what is the law?" and "what does this law force to happen?": given a few principles, what must this system do, to the precision of a number?

What counts as progress is equally distinctive: a law that unifies phenomena that looked separate, a measurement that discriminates between rival theories, a prediction confirmed where no one thought to look. Physics made **prediction** a virtue: a theory that cannot in principle be checked against the world is not yet doing physics. The price is abstraction: the world of physics is the stripped world of frictionless planes and point particles, and the messier remainder is left to the neighbors in Section 4.

Two observations the table is begging to make. First: this is the folder's least contested placement. Every map files physics under natural science without a footnote, and most name it directly. Other domains are merged, demoted, or split; physics is simply *there*. That unanimity is worth sitting with: the category "natural science" was not drawn around physics by accident. Second: Aristotle's bare "theoretical" hides a later split. In his scheme physics (natural philosophy) and mathematics and theology were the three theoretical sciences: the domain was knowledge of nature for its own sake. The modern distinction between physics the pure science and its engineering applications lives mostly at the Frascati boundary, where 1.3 (physical sciences) and field 2 (engineering and technology) are neighbors with a busy border.

What the maps cannot show is the culture inside the door. TOK files physics inside "natural sciences" rather than giving it an area of its own, and that is fair to the domain's reach but unfair to its internal divide: theory and experiment are two tribes with different tastes, and the field advances only in their conversation.

## 3. Its method and culture

How claims earn trust here: **experiment**, disciplined by mathematics. Physics' two engines are the derivation (from laws to prediction) and the measurement (from world to number), and neither is trusted alone: a theory earns its place by surviving tests it did not design for itself, and a measurement earns its place by being reproducible. This is the empirical family at its most exacting: the world, in the end, gets a veto.

In Biglan's terms physics is **hard-pure**: high agreement, convergent, aimed at understanding rather than application. But the craft runs on **knowing-how** as much as knowing-that: choosing a coordinate system, designing a measurement that cancels its own errors, sensing which approximation is legitimate. Theorists spend the day in equations; experimentalists spend it in the lab or at a telescope; both share a taste for the elegant experiment and the clean result. The culture's self-image is honest error-correction: when theory and experiment disagree, one of them is wrong, and more and better data settles it.

A good day in the domain: a calculation you worked on for weeks predicts a number, and someone's instrument returns exactly that number. Or the reverse: an impossible measurement forces a law to change. Either way the universe arbitrates, which is why [[04_logical-fallacies]] matters here but never has the last word.

## 4. Its seams and neighbors

- [[01_mathematics]]: the language seam. Mathematics is physics' working tongue, and the border question is Wigner's old puzzle: why does abstract structure fit the world so well? Physics treats mathematics as its language; mathematics treats physics as its best customer.
- [[03_chemistry]]: where does physics stop and chemistry start? Atoms obey physics' laws, but substances, reactions, and the lab culture of making things belong to chemistry: the seam runs through physical chemistry.
- [[05_earth-and-space-sciences]]: astrophysics and geophysics are physics turned on the sky and the earth, and the question of whose knowledge the models are never quite settles.
- [[08_engineering-and-technology]]: the applied seam. Once the laws are used to build rather than to explain, the deed changes, and Frascati files it under field 2 instead.

## 5. How to learn this domain

> Status: STUB. The full treatment comes in the meta-learning note on learning each kind of knowledge (question 2).

The empirical family's practice here is **problems and measurements**, and the practitioner's deed is deriving and testing. Physicists learn the domain the way they build it: by solving problems until the standard techniques become reflexes, and by rebuilding the great derivations to feel where the ideas came from. Physics is famous for its problem sets because the deed of the field is a solved problem matched to the world. The calibration lesson of [[05_dunning-kruger-effect]] applies brutally here: you do not know a law until you can derive its consequences yourself.

## Key takeaways

- Physics is the paradigm natural science: every map files it there without a footnote, the least contested placement in the folder.
- Its method is the double engine of derivation and measurement: a theory earns trust by surviving tests it did not design, and the world holds a veto.
- Biglan hard-pure, but the culture is genuinely double: theory and experiment are two tribes whose conversation makes the field.
- The maps file the applications elsewhere (Frascati field 2): knowing nature and building with it are separated by a map line practitioners feel less clearly.
- To learn it is to do it: the physicist's deed is the solved problem matched to the world, so problems and rebuilt derivations beat reading.
