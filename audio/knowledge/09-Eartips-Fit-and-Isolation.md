# Eartips, Fit, and Isolation

## Why fit is so important

An IEM forms an acoustic seal with the ear canal. A leak usually reduces bass first, while insertion depth and nozzle angle can change upper-mid and treble response. The same IEM can therefore sound like different products in different ears.

Fit also determines comfort, isolation, stability, and how much outside noise makes you turn the volume up.

## A useful fit test

1. Play a familiar track with steady bass.
2. Gently press the IEMs into the ears.
3. If bass becomes much stronger, your normal seal is leaking.
4. Try a different tip size or style before changing EQ.
5. Check both ears separately; asymmetry is normal.

## Tip variables

| Variable | Possible result |
|---|---|
| Larger size | Stronger seal, more bass, but pressure or discomfort |
| Smaller size | Comfort, but possible bass leak |
| Deeper insertion | Can improve stability; may alter treble and comfort |
| Foam tip | Strong isolation and seal; can change treble and wears out |
| Wide bore | Often changes upper-mid/treble presentation |
| Narrow bore | Often changes seal and perceived warmth |

These are tendencies, not guarantees. Your ear canal is the final acoustic system.

## Fit before EQ

If bass is missing, solve the seal first. If one channel sounds quieter, check fit, wax, cable seating, and the recording before assuming a driver failure. If treble is painful, try a better fit, lower volume, different tips, or gentle EQ.

## Health and safety

- Start with the volume at minimum.
- Increase slowly.
- Take listening breaks.
- Do not use isolation as a reason to listen dangerously loud.
- Clean tips and nozzles safely; do not push debris into the driver.

## Value ranking

For an IEM owner, the usual low-cost experiment order is:

```text
Correct insertion and seal
→ tip size/style
→ listening volume
→ EQ
→ only then consider new hardware
```
