# Listening and A/B Testing

## Why casual comparisons mislead

The louder version usually sounds more exciting. Newness, expectation, and memory also influence judgments. A reliable comparison controls volume, changes one variable, and uses familiar passages.

## Basic method

1. Choose a 20–60 second section you know well.
2. Set up a baseline.
3. Change exactly one thing: EQ, Dolby, tips, gain, or output path.
4. Match loudness as closely as possible.
5. Switch quickly, then repeat in a different order.
6. Write what changed before deciding what you prefer.
7. Return to the baseline after a break.

## What to listen for

| Feature | Useful question |
|---|---|
| Bass | Is the kick deeper, or merely louder? |
| Vocals | Are words clearer, or just more forward? |
| Treble | Is more detail present, or is it simply brighter? |
| Imaging | Can you locate instruments consistently? |
| Fatigue | Does the sound remain comfortable after 20–30 minutes? |
| Dynamics | Are quiet/loud contrasts preserved, or is it just louder? |

## Testing your setup

### Dolby/Xiaomi effects

Keep UAPP and MX1 settings fixed. Toggle only the phone effect. If the sound does not change, the direct USB path may be bypassing it.

### UAPP PEQ

Keep vendor effects off. Compare PEQ on/off with matched volume. Use a negative preamp for positive filters.

### Eartips

Use the same track and volume. Check bass seal and comfort. Tip changes can be large enough to invalidate memory-based comparisons.

### Balanced output

Do not compare 3.5 mm and 4.4 mm at different levels. A louder output is not automatically better.

## Decision rule

Prefer the setup that is:

```text
clearly better at matched volume
+ comfortable for long listening
+ reliable in daily use
+ not dependent on a placebo claim
```

If the difference disappears when levels are matched, it may not be a meaningful upgrade.
