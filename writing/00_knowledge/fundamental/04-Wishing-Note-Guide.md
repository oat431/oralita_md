# Wishing Note Guide

> Small words, high stakes: a wish is writing measured by how it is *received*, not how it reads. Up to parent map [[writing/00_knowledge/fundamental/00-Writing-Types]].

## What Makes a Wish Work

A wish succeeds when the recipient feels **seen**, not processed. Three principles:

1. **Specific beats grand** — one true shared detail ("you stayed up fixing my build before my demo") outperforms ten generic blessings. Specificity is proof you thought of *them*.
2. **Register = relationship** — match closeness. Too formal feels distant; too casual feels careless.
3. **Ground backward, wish forward** — anchor in something real from the past, then wish something concrete for their future.

## The Skeleton

```
1. ADDRESS   — use their name (a wish without a name is a template)
2. GROUND    — one specific memory / trait / achievement of THIS person
3. WISH      — 1–2 concrete hopes ("best at WHAT?" — never just "all the best")
4. CLOSE     — warmth scaled to the relationship
```

**Length by medium**

| Medium | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Card | 2–4 sentences |
| Chat / LINE | 1–3 lines |
| Speech / toast | 30–60 seconds |
| Social post | 1–2 lines — the photo carries the rest |

## Occasion Playbook

| Occasion | Ground with | Wish concretely | ⚠️ Avoid |
|----------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| Birthday | their year: growth, survival, wins | success framed on *their* goals | age jokes unless very close |
| Graduation | the hard stretch nobody saw | the chapter opening next | "welcome to the real world" cliché |
| New job / promotion | what they're actually good at | growth, impact, a good team | salary/title assumptions |
| Anniversary | a detail from the origin story | more years like their best one | comparing them to other couples |
| Farewell / project end | one shared moment of the work | their next step + staying in touch | corporate emptiness ("valuable asset") |
| Condolence | the person's qualities, honestly | specific presence & support | "at least…", silver linings, your own grief |

Condolence rule of thumb: **acknowledge, don't erase.** Say the loss is real, offer something specific ("I'll bring food Thursday"), never explain why it's fine.

## Thai ↔ English Register Map

Same content, different delivery — this is where bilingual writers win or slip:

| Layer | Thai | English |
|-------|------|---------|
| Formality ladder | particles (ครับ/ค่ะ/นะ/จ้ะ) + pronoun choice (ผม/ฉัน/เรา) | contractions + word choice carry tone |
| Blessing style | ขอให้… constructions; elders expect stacked blessings (สุขภาพแข็งแรง ประสบความสำเร็จ) | personal hopes ("this year I hope you finally…") |
| Humor | safe among peers, risky upward | more tolerated, still read the room |
| Elders / bosses | honorific register, no bare first names | first names usually fine |

⚠️ **Thai Speaker Trap (in English):** translating a ขอให้… blessing stack literally ("may you have good health, wealth, happiness and success in everything") reads as greeting-card generic. English wishes land through ONE specific personal hope instead of five abstract nouns.

⚠️ **English Speaker Trap (in Thai):** a short casual English-style wish can feel curt toward Thai elders — restore the particle, restore the blessing stack.

## Common Failure Modes

1. **Copy-paste energy** — anything Googleable is worth zero
2. **Making it about yourself** — "I remember when *I*…" hijacks their day
3. **Wrong-register humor** — condolence jokes, roasting your boss
4. **Abstract-noun stacking** — health/wealth/happiness with no actual person in it
5. **Over-length** — a wish is a gift, not an essay

## Fill-in Template

```markdown
# Wishing Note: <Recipient> — <Occasion> (<Date>)

**Relationship:** … · **Medium:** card/LINE/speech · **Language:** TH/EN

## Ground (one real thing)
<specific memory or trait>

## The wish
<concrete hope tied to THEIR goal>

## Final text
<the actual note>
```
