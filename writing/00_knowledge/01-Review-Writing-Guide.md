# Review Writing Guide

> How to write a review that is *useful*, not just an opinion. A review is an argument: **criteria → evidence → verdict**. Up to parent map [[00-Writing-Types]].

## What a Review Actually Is

Not a plot summary with stars at the end. A review answers three questions in order:

1. **What is it?** — orient the reader (expository)
2. **How well does it do what it tries to do?** — evaluate against criteria (the core job)
3. **Should *you* (the reader) spend time/money on it?** — recommendation matched to a reader type

The golden rule of evaluation: **judge the work against its own ambitions**, not against the work you wish existed. A B-movie that nails being a fun B-movie can earn a better verdict than a prestige drama that fumbles its own goals.

## The Skeleton

```
1. HOOK          — one sharp observation or scene, not "This movie was..."
2. CONTEXT       — what it is, who made it, where it sits (franchise/genre/author history)
   ⚠️ SPOILER LINE — say where spoilers start
3. CRITERIA      — name your yardsticks BEFORE measuring (2–4, medium-dependent)
4. EVIDENCE      — for each criterion: specific scenes/chapters/features + why they work or don't
5. WEAKNESSES    — honest, specific; a review with no flaws is marketing, not criticism
6. VERDICT       — score/rating + who should consume it and who shouldn't
```

The verdict comes last on the page but **first in your head**: decide your overall judgment before writing, then let every section serve it. Reviews organized around criteria rather than plot chronology read as professional; reviews retelling the plot scene-by-scene read as amateur.

## Criteria Cheat-Sheet by Medium

| Medium | Core criteria (pick 2–4) |
|--------|--------------------------|
| **Book** | Prose style · plot construction · character depth · theme payoff · pacing |
| **Film / Series** | Script · direction · acting · cinematography · sound/music · editing pace |
| **Game** | Core loop (is the minute-to-minute fun?) · story · art direction · audio · technical state (bugs, perf) · value-for-price |
| **Tech product** | Build quality · real-world performance vs claims · battery/lifespan · price-to-value · support/warranty |
| **Restaurant** | Food execution · service · atmosphere · price fairness · location/access |
| **Travel place** | Experience density · crowds · cost · access · best time to visit |

Rule: if a criterion doesn't change anyone's decision, cut it.

## Evidence Discipline

- **Specific beats general.** "The fight choreography in the stairwell scene sells every impact" > "action was great."
- **Describe the effect, not just the feature.** Don't only say what something is; say what it *did to you* (bored you, made you check the runtime, gave you chills).
- **One counterexample minimum.** For each praise, ask "where did it fail?" — balance is credibility.
- **No spoilers past the line.** Tease with setup, never reveal payoff.

## Verdict Formats

| Format | Best for |
|--------|----------|
| Score `/10` or `/5` | Quick scanning, aggregators — define what a 7 means to you and stay consistent |
| Verdict phrase ("Worth a matinee, not full price") | More honest than scores — carries nuance a number loses |
| Audience-matched recommendation ("Buy if X; skip if Y") | Most useful single sentence a review can contain |
| Pros/cons table | Tech and game reviews where readers compare specs |

Pick one primary format and be consistent across your reviews so your readers calibrate to you.

## Common Failure Modes

1. **Plot-recap disease** — 70% summary, 20% opinion, 10% conclusion. Invert it.
2. **Criteria drift** — praising a comedy for lacking drama. Judge intent.
3. **Vague adjectives** — "amazing," "boring," "mid" with zero evidence behind them.
4. **False balance** — forcing exactly 3 pros + 3 cons when the truth is lopsided. Report the lopsidedness.
5. **Reviewing the audience** — mocking fans instead of evaluating the artifact.

## Fill-in Template

```markdown
# Review: <Title> (<Year>)

**Type:** film/book/game/product · **Consumed:** theater/PC/format · **Time invested:** ~Xh

## One-line hook
<your shardest observation>

## Context
<what it is, who made it, what it's trying to be>

## Evaluation
### <Criterion 1, e.g. Story>
<evidence + effect>
### <Criterion 2>
<evidence + effect>
### <Criterion 3>
<evidence + effect>

## Weaknesses
- <specific flaw + why it matters>

## Verdict
**Score:** X/Y · **In one phrase:** <verdict phrase>
**Play/watch/use it if...** <reader profile>
**Skip it if...** <disqualifying profile>
```
