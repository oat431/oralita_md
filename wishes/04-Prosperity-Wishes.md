## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**The prosperity formula that is real**, Freyr's blessing. In both Gylfaginning and Ynglinga saga, Freyr is the god "to invoke for plenty and peace," and he governs *fésæla* (men's wealth):

> Gylfaginning 24: "…ok á hann er gott at heita til árs ok friðar. Hann ræðr ok fésælu manna.": "…and it is good to invoke him for good seasons and peace. He also governs the wealth of men."

This is THE attested Norse prosperity-peace phrase. *(Chapter numbers vary ±1 by edition. Flag when citing.)*

**The Fehu poems are honest about wealth**. The rune poems are cautionary, not celebratory. No "abundance ritual" reading exists in any medieval source (that part is modern). Both poems in the reference box below.

### Snippets

**1. Til árs ok friðar** 🏪💼 *(the blessing, opener for any venture)*

```
Til árs ok friðar.
```

*EN:* "For plenty and peace." *(ON pronunciation: till OWRS ok FRITH-ar; á ≈ "ow".)*
*Source:* Gylfaginning 24; Ynglinga saga 13: "…ok blótuðu mest til árs ok friðar alla ævi síðan": "…and sacrificed chiefly for plenty and peace ever afterwards." Use as the blessing over a new shop, job, or year.

**2. The Fehu couplet (Anglo-Saxon)** 🍀🏪 *(wealth with generosity, the "share it" philosophy)*

```
Feoh byþ frofur fira gehwylcum;
sceal ðeah manna gehwylc miclun hyt dælan…
```

*EN (Dickins):* "Wealth is a comfort to all men; yet must every man bestow it freely…" *(Source: Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem.)*
Use: for someone opening a business, wealth as comfort *and* community.

**3. Hávamál 36: the cottage blessing** 🏪🍀 *(counsel register: "enough beats grasping")*

```
Bú er betra, þótt lítit sé.
```

*EN (Bellows):* "Better a house, though a hut it be." *(Source: Hávamál 36. The stanza continues about a home of one's own being better than begging; counsel register, not a blessing. Send only to the right friend, tone-labeled.)*
⚠️ Per verification: **no Hávamál wealth stanza is a blessing**. 36 is counsel, 78 is a warning ("Wealth is as swift as a winking eye, of friends the falsest it is"). Label tones when quoting.

**4. Reference: the Icelandic Fehu poem** *(the warning voice)*

```
Fé er frænda róg ok flæðar viti ok grafseiðs gata.
```

*EN (Dickins):* "Wealth, source of discord among kinsmen, and fire of the sea, and path of the serpent." *(Source: Old Icelandic Rune Poem.)*
⚠️ This is the **Icelandic** poem (not Norwegian, common mix-up); the Norwegian variant reads "Fé vældr frænda róge; føðesk ulfr í skóge" ("wealth is a source of discord; the wolf lives in the forest"). Both are medieval MSS, and both warn.

*Vocabulary notes: auðr (wealth) · fé (cattle→money; cousin of Latin pecūnia and English "fee") · félag (partnership; literally "fee-laying," from fé + lag) · fésæla (wealth/abundance).*

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Cato's harvest prayer** 🏪📈 *(the oldest Roman "prosper the works of my hands")*

```
Uti tu fruges frumenta vineta virgultaque
grandire dueneque evenire siris.
```

*EN:* "…that you let crops, grains, vines, and orchards grow and come forth well." *(Duene = archaic spelling of bene. This is 2nd-century-BC prayer register.)*
*Source:* Cato, *De agri cultura* 141 (Mars prayer for the fields; the full prayer also asks "duonam salutem valetudinemque mihi domo familiaeque nostrae": "good health and strength for me, my house, and my household"). **Adaptation:** swap "crops and vineyards" for their actual field (shop, studio, portfolio) and label it as adapted.

**2. Rem facias, rem** 🍻💼 *(the cynic's creed, use with a wink)*

```
Rem facias, rem, si possis recte,
si non, quocumque modo rem.
```

*EN:* "Get money, money; if you can, honestly; if not, money by any means." *(Source: Horace, Epistles 1.1.65–66, quoted as the voice of pure greed; send only as a joke with close friends who know the tone.)*

**3. Pecunia e lotio est** 💼🍀 *(the corrected quote)*

```
Atqui, inquit, e lotio est.
```

*EN:* "Yet," he said, "it comes from urine." *(Source: Suetonius, Vespasian 23.3, Vespasian sniffing tax money; his point: all revenue is clean once it reaches the treasury.)*
⚠️ **"Pecunia non olet" ("money doesn't smell") is NOT verbatim Suetonius**. It's the later condensed proverb (also in Dio 65.14.5). Cite it as a proverb, never as a Suetonius quote. Best use: the startup-humor card for friends funding something unglamorous.

**4. Culture: Dis Pater & Ops** *(reference only)*
Cicero (*ND* 2.66): Dis Pater is "dives" ("rich"), like Greek Πλούτων; **Ops**, goddess of abundance (consort of Saturn, festivals Opalia Dec 19 / Opiconsivia Aug 25). Two gods of wealth for the lore shelf. No snippets needed.

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. ἀγαθοῦ δαίμονος** 🍻🏪 *(the toast, raise a cup for the deal)*

```
ἀγαθοῦ δαίμονος
```

*Translit:* agathou daimonos · *EN:* "Of the Good Spirit!", the old drinking cry wishing fortune's favor.
*Source:* Aristophanes, *Wasps* 525, *Knights* 85 and 105–106 ("…σπεῖσον ἀγαθοῦ δαίμονος": "pour a cup of the Good Spirit"); the Suda explains: after dinner they drank "of the Good Daimon," and when about to depart, "of Zeus the Saviour."
⚠️ Zeus Soter was the **parting/third** toast, not the second. Don't sequence it wrong. (For luck-file cross-ref: ἀγαθῇ τύχῃ is the *opener*; this is the *cup*.)

**2. Hesiod: Work is no disgrace** 💼📈

```
ἔργον δ' οὐδὲν ὄνειδος, ἀεργίη δέ τ' ὄνειδος.
```

*Translit:* ergon d' ouden oneidos, aergiē de t' oneidos · *EN:* "Work is no disgrace; idleness is." *(Source: Works and Days 311. The next lines promise that with wealth come virtue and glory, τὴν γὰρ εὐτυχίην…; use for someone grinding toward their goal.)*

**3. Plutus's blindness** 🍀📈 *(the honest one)*

```
ὁ δέ μ' ἐποίησεν τυφλόν,
ἵνα μὴ διαγιγνώσκοιμι τούτων μηδένα.
```

*Translit:* ho de m' epoiēsen typhlon, hina mē diagignōskōimi toutōn mēdena · *EN:* "[Zeus] blinded me, so that I cannot tell the just apart from the rest." (Wealth himself explains why the good aren't always rich.)
*Source:* Aristophanes, *Plutus* 87–91. ⚠️ Plutus is from **Aristophanes** (and Hesiod, Theogony 969–974: Demeter bore Plutus; whoever meets him "he makes rich and grants great ólbos"), **not** Aristotle (common misattribution). Culture: Πλούτων = Hades, "the rich one" (Plato, Cratylus 403a).

*Vocabulary: πλοῦτος (wealth) · ὄλβος (prosperity/bliss, Homeric flavor) · εὐπορία (plenty, ease) · εὐτυχία (good fortune).*

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. Śrī Sūkta v. 1: Lakṣmī's hymn** 🙏🏪 *(Vedic liturgical, recite with respect)*

```
हिरण्यवर्णां हरिणीं सुवर्णरजतस्रजाम् ।
चन्द्रां हिरण्मयीं लक्ष्मीं जातवेदो म आवह ॥
```

*Translit:* hiraṇyavarṇāṃ hariṇīṃ suvarṇarajatasrajām | candrāṃ hiranmayīṃ lakṣmīṃ jātavedo ma āvaha
*EN:* "O Jātavedas (Agni), summon to me Lakṣmī, golden-hued, lovely, garlanded in gold and silver, moon-bright, gleaming like gold."
*Source:* Śrī Sūkta v.1, a Khila hymn of the Ṛgveda (Bāṣkala recension; recited in Lakṣmī worship). ⚠️ Some web copies carry a typo ("हिरण्यवर्णाम"). Use the clean reading above.

**2. धनधान्यसमृद्धिरस्तु** 🏪🍀 *(the attesting word, "wealth and grain prosperity be!" )*

```
धनधान्यसमृद्धिरस्तु।
```

*Translit:* dhana-dhānya-samṛddhir astu · *EN:* "May there be prosperity of wealth and grain."
*Source:* attested āśīrvāda in Sanskrit ritual texts (kūlāla-śāstra pūnyāhavācana: "…धनधान्य समृद्धिरस्तु। पुत्र-पौत्राभिवृद्धिरस्तु। स्वस्तिरस्तु।": "…may wealth and grain prosper; may sons and grandsons increase; may there be well-being."). The full blessing doubles as a family line.

**3. सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये** 🏪🙏 *(the auspiciousness verse, chant register)*

```
सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये, शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके।
शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि, नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते॥
```

*Translit:* sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye, śive sarvārthasādhike | śaraṇye tryambake gauri, nārāyaṇi namo'stu te
*EN:* "O auspiciousness of all things auspicious, Śivā who accomplishes all aims, refuge of all, three-eyed Gaurī, Nārāyaṇī, homage to you."
*Source:* Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa 91.9 (Devī Māhātmya); chanted in Devī/Lakṣmī worship. (Variants साधके/देवि exist in floating recensions; the Purāṇa reading is साधिके/गौरि.) Devotional register; a bow, not a bonus.

**4. Cross-ref: the Pali wealth wisdom** 🍀 *(from [[03-Health-Wishes]], here for balance)*

```
सन्तुट्ठिपरमं धनं
```

*Translit:* santuṭṭhiparamaṁ dhanaṁ · *EN:* "Contentment is the greatest wealth." *(Source: Dhammapada 204, the full verse in [[03-Health-Wishes]].)*
And its shadow, the anti-greed line (Dhp 186): "Na kahāpaṇavassena titti kāmesu vijjati": "Not by a shower of coins is one sated." ⚠️ Wisdom register. Never send as a money wish.
*Culture: अर्थ artha = material prosperity, the second puruṣārtha (dharma–artha–kāma–mokṣa); Kauṭilya's Arthaśāstra = the classical "science of material gain."*

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

**1. ขอให้ร่ำรวย** 🍀💼 *(the everyday, casual-standard)*

```
ขอให้ร่ำรวย
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi râam-ruai · *EN:* "May you be rich."
Festive extension: `ขอให้ร่ำรวยยิ่งๆ ขึ้นไป`: "may you grow ever richer." The standard Thai gloss of 恭喜发财.

**2. ขอให้กิจการเจริญรุ่งเรือง** 🏪 *(for shop owners, polite standard)*

```
ขอให้กิจการเจริญรุ่งเรือง
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi kìt-jà-kaan jà-rəən rûng-rʉang · *EN:* "May your business flourish."
Variant: `ขอให้ธุรกิจเจริญรุ่งเรือง` (slightly more formal). The go-to for openings, wreaths, and cards.

**3. ขอให้ขายดีๆนะ** 🏪 *(for vendors, casual-warm)*

```
ขอให้ขายดีๆนะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi khǎai dii-dii ná · *EN:* "May you sell well."

**4. ขอให้มั่งมีศรีสุข** 🏪🧧 *(the classic, strongest for CNY and Thai-Chinese contexts)*

```
ขอให้มั่งมีศรีสุข
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi mâng-mii sǐi-sùk · *EN:* "May you be wealthy, with fortune and happiness."
มั่งมี = wealthy (RID: "มีเงินมาก"); ศรี = glory/fortune (from Sanskrit *śrī*, the same word as in the Śrī Sūkta above!); สุข = happiness. ⚠️ ศรีสุข isn't a standalone dictionary word. It lives inside this blessing. Not a toss-away to strangers; save for New Year, weddings-adjacent, and elders.

**5. ขอให้เงินทองไหลมาเทมา** 🍀💼 *(money, flowing in)*

```
ขอให้เงินทองไหลมาเทมา
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi ngoen-thɔɔng lǎi maa thee maa · *EN:* "May money flow in abundantly." (จาก หลั่งไหล, to flow like water; RID-attested.) Lively colloquial. Captions love it.

**6. ขอให้มีเงินทองใช้ไม่ขาดมือ** 💼🍀 *(the practical one, never run short)*

```
ขอให้มีเงินทองใช้ไม่ขาดมือ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi mii ngoen-thɔɔng chái mâi khàat-mʉʉ · *EN:* "May you never run short of money." (ไม่ขาดมือ, "never out of hand"; RID-attested idiom.)

**7. ขอให้เจริญๆ นะ** 🏪 *(what customers say leaving a shop)*

```
ขอให้เจริญๆ นะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi jà-rəən-jà-rəən ná · *EN:* "May you prosper."
THE classic customer farewell to a small business. ⚠️ Elsewhere เจริญ can carry a wry edge: friends-only outside the shop context (your native QA call).

**8. Raise-day** 💼 *(workplace casual, verified natural)*

```
ขอให้ได้ขึ้นเงินเดือนเยอะๆ นะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi dâai khʉ̂n ngoen-dʉan yə́-yə́ ná · *EN:* "May you get a big raise!"

*Culture notes: 🧧 ไฉ่ซิงเอี๊ย (財神爺, Caishen), the god of wealth, worshipped as the first deity of Chinese New Year; believed to descend once a year (offerings set 23:00–01:00, bankbooks and wallets on the table). 🔥 เจ้าพ่อเจ้าแม่ trade shrines, ศาลเจ้า culture, busiest at ตรุษจีน/ง่วนเซียว/สารทจีน; traders pray for เงินทอง/โชคลาภ. Mention with respect: living beliefs.*

## The Shop-Opening Send: assembled example `[craft]`

For a friend opening a shop or going freelance:

```
Til árs ok friðar: for plenty and peace.
ขอให้กิจการเจริญรุ่งเรืองนะ
May the door swing open and the till stay full.
```

And for the deal-closing toast, raise the cup:

```
ἀγαθοῦ δαίμονος
(Of the Good Spirit!)
```

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **"Pecunia non olet" is NOT verbatim Suetonius**: a later proverb; the actual line is "Atqui… e lotio est." (Vesp. 23.3). Cite accordingly
- **Fehu's "abundance ritual" readings are modern esoteric**. The attested poems are *cautionary* (share it / it's discord / it evaporates)
- **"Fé er frænda róg" is the Icelandic rune poem**, not Norwegian (common mix-up)
- **No Hávamál wealth stanza is a blessing**: 36 = counsel, 78 = warning; label tones
- **Hávamál stanza numbering drifts** by edition (Bellows runs ±1); Gylfaginning/Ynglinga chapters too
- **Zeus Soter was the parting/third toast**, not "the second"
- **Plutus belongs to Aristophanes & Hesiod**, not Aristotle (misattribution)
- **Śrī Sūkta copies carry typos/variants** (हिरण्यवर्णाम; साधके/देवि). Use the clean Purāṇa/clean-Khila reading
- **Dhp 186 is anti-greed wisdom**. Never send as a money wish; Dhp 204's contentment line is cross-ref only
- **Caishen's Thai spelling varies** (ไฉ่ซิงเอี๊ย / ไฉ่สิ่งเอี้ย). Pick one per context, don't mix in one message
- **เจริญๆ can be sarcastic**, shop-context only unless you know the person
- **No promises**: "may fortune favor," never "this will make you rich"
