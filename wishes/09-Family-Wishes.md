## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**What's actually attested:** the family rune is real but Anglo-Saxon: the **ᛟ Eþel** stanza about the ancestral estate. Old Norse contributes the vocabulary of lineage (*óðal*, *arfr*, *ætt*). ⚠️ The rune poems quoted here are Old English (Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem), Latin-script transmission like the Fehu and Dæg entries; the ON (younger futhark) rune poems have no óðal rune. And there is **no attested Norse wedding blessing** (gap reported below).

### Snippets

**1. ᛟ The Eþel stanza (Anglo-Saxon)** 🏡 *(the ancestral estate: first pick for home & lineage)*

```
Eþel byþ oferleof æghwylcum men, gif he mot ðær
rihtes and gerysena on brucan on bolde bleadum oftast.
```

*EN (Dickins 1915):* "An estate is very dear to every man, if he can enjoy there in his house whatever is right and proper in constant prosperity."
*Source:* Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem st. 26 (Dickins vv. 71–73). Rune-name Eþel/Œþel (the family and inheritance rune); the ON cognate is óðal.

**2. ᛗ The kin line** 👪 *(the "Man" rune poems: two verified half-lines)*

```
Man byþ on myrgþe his magan leof.
```

*EN (Dickins 1915):* "The joyous man is dear to his kinsmen." *(Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem vv. 59–62: quote only this line; the stanza turns to mortality.)*
```
Maðr er manns gaman ok moldar auki.
```

*EN:* "Man is man's delight and the increase of the earth." *(Icelandic Rune Poem, Maðr stanza, line 1; quoted in Dickins 1915's apparatus. ⚠️ Partially verified: the quoted line only.)*

**3. Modern rune flavor** `[modern esoteric: 20th-c. runology, not Viking-age fact]`

- **ᛒ Berkanan**: modern readings, family/fertility/the mother. ⚠️ The attested Beorc line is a BARRENNESS image ("tanas butan tudder": twigs without offspring). Label modern; never sell as Viking-age fact.

*Vocabulary notes: óðal (ancestral property, patrimony; flýja óðul sín, to abandon one's home) · arfr (inheritance; taka arf eptir e-n, to be heir to someone) · frændi (kinsman; frænd-leifð, patrimony) · ætt (family, kin; auka ætt sina, to increase one's family) · barn (child; ⚠️ vera með barni, to be with child: fertility register).*

**Gap note:** no attested Norse wedding blessing or toast exists in the saga material checked. The closest are legal-customary (Grágás marriage law) and the post-Christian "Heilags Anda skál" toast. Reported as a gap; nothing invented.

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. O Hymenaee Hymen** 💍 *(Catullus 61: the Roman wedding song)*

```
qui rapis teneram ad virum virginem,
o Hymenaee Hymen, o Hymen Hymenaee.
```

*EN:* "who carry off the tender maiden to her husband: O Hymen, Hymenaee!"
*Source:* Catullus 61.3–5 (the epithalamium for Manlius Torquatus and Aurunculeia); the refrain recurs at 39–40, 49–50 and on.

**2. Vesper adest, iuvenes** 💍 *(the wedding dawn-call: Catullus 62!)*

```
Vesper adest, iuvenes, consurgite: Vesper Olympo
exspectata diu vix tandem lumina tollit.
```

*EN:* "The Evening Star is here: up, young men, arise! At last from Olympus the long-awaited Star lifts his light."
*Source:* Catullus 62.1–2 (the exametrum carmen nuptiale). ⚠️ NOT Catullus 61 (see Misuse Notes), and possibly not even by Catullus.

**3. Concordia** 👪 *(Sallust: the family-harmony maxim)*

```
Nam concordia parvae res crescunt,
discordia maximae dilabuntur.
```

*EN:* "For by concord small things grow; by discord the greatest things crumble away."
*Source:* Sallust, *Jugurtha* 10.6 (the dying Micipsa's advice to his heirs). Originally about a royal household; the everyday family reading is a warm extension.

**4. The Penates** 🏡 *(the household gods: culture and one sendable line)*

```
Sacra suosque tibi commendat Troia penates;
hos cape fatorum comites, his moenia quaere.
```

*EN:* "Troy commends to you her sacred rites and her Penates; take them as the companions of your fate, seek out for them a city."
*Source:* Vergil, *Aeneid* 2.293–294 (Hector's ghost). The Dii Penates guard the household store (*penus*), family provisioning and lineage: warm home-and-lineage material.

**5. O fortunatos nimium** 🏡 *(Vergil: the peaceable household)*

```
O fortunatos nimium, sua si bona norint,
agricolas!
```

*EN:* "O farmers, blest beyond measure, if they knew their own good!"
*Source:* Vergil, *Georgics* 2.458–460 (for them the earth pours an easy living, far from the clash of arms). Household happiness sentiment.

*Culture note: **Juno Pronuba** presides over marriage (*Aen.* 4.166–167, "prima et Tellus et pronuba Iuno dant signum"). ⚠️ That particular scene is doom-laden: culture note only, never a wish.*

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. Ὑμὴν Ὑμέναι' ὦ** 💍 *(the wedding cry)*

```
Ὑμὴν Ὑμέναι' ὦ.
```

*Translit:* Hymēn Hyménai' ō · *EN:* "Hymen, Hymenaeus, O!"
*Source:* Aristophanes, *Peace* 1331–1335 (Trygaios' wedding; the chorus repeats it, and the makarismos follows: "ὦ τρὶς μάκαρ ὡς δικαίως τἀγαθὰ νῦν ἔχεις," "O thrice-blessed, how justly you now hold good things!"); companion attestation *Birds* 1731ff (the Zeus–Hera wedding song). ⚠️ NOT in Thesmophoriazusae.

**2. Sappho's epithalamia** 💍 *(verified greetings: pick per couple)*

```
χαῖρε, νύμφα, χαῖρε, τίμιε γάμβρε.
```

*EN:* "Hail, bride! Hail, honoured bridegroom!" *(Sappho fr. 116 V; quoted by Servius. Transliteration omitted: not returned by the verifier this round.)*
```
ὄλβιε γάμβρε, σοὶ μὲν δὴ γάμος ὠς ἄραο ἐκτετέλεστ'.
```

*Translit:* olbie gambre, soi men dē gamos ōs arao ektelest' · *EN:* "Happy bridegroom: your wedding is fulfilled as you prayed." *(Sappho fr. 112 V.)*
Two more verified gems: fr. 115 V ("To what shall I liken you, dear bridegroom? To a slender sapling I liken you best") and fr. 117 V ("Let the bride rejoice, and let the bridegroom rejoice!"). ⚠️ The "happy bridegroom" line is fr. 112, not fr. 44.

**3. Odysseus on parents and home** 🏡👥 *(Od. 9.34–36)*

```
ὣς οὐδὲν γλύκιον ἧς πατρίδος οὐδὲ τοκήων
γίγνεται, εἴ περ καί τις ἀπόπροθι πίονα οἶκον.
```

*Translit:* hōs ouden glykion hēs patridos oude tokēōn… *(romanization as returned by the verifier)* · *EN:* "Nothing is sweeter than one's own country and one's own parents, even if a man lives in a rich house in a foreign land."
*Source:* *Odyssey* 9.34–36 (Odysseus to Alcinous). Tone: longing and homesick; a lineage sentiment, not a toast.

**4. Culture: the pomegranate of Hera** 🍏 *(marriage's own fruit)*

```
τῶν χειρῶν τῇ μὲν καρπὸν φέρει ῥοιᾶς, τῇ δὲ σκῆπτρον.
```

*EN:* "In one hand she carries a pomegranate, in the other a scepter." *(And the guide adds: "Of the pomegranate I must say nothing, for its story is a matter too sacred to tell.")*
*Source:* Pausanias 2.17.4 (the gold-and-ivory Hera of Polykleitos at the Argive Heraion). The pomegranate belongs to Hera, goddess of marriage. ⚠️ Persephone's pomegranate is underworld register (never for weddings); the bridal pomegranate-smashing custom is modern Greek folklore, not classical.

*Word notes: οἶκος (house, home, household; Od. 9.35) · τέκνον (child, offspring; Il. 1.362) · στέφανος (wreath, crown; Paus. 2.17.4). (Glosses are standard LSJ senses; the LSJ headwords themselves were not line-checked this round.)*

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. समञ्जन्तु विश्वे देवाः: the wedding blessing** 💍 *(safe core)*

```
समञ्जन्तु विश्वे देवाः समापो हृदयानि नौ ।
सं मातरिश्वा सं धाता समु देष्ट्री दधातु नौ ॥
```

*Translit:* samañjantu viśve devāḥ sam āpo hṛdayāni nau | saṃ mātariśvā saṃ dhātā sam u deṣṭrī dadhātu nau ||
*EN:* "May all the gods unite us; may the waters unite our hearts; may Mātariśvan, Dhātṛ, and the Arranger unite us."
*Source:* Ṛgveda 10.85.47 (Sūryā's marriage hymn). No offspring content: a couple's card line.

**2. गृभ्णामि ते सौभगत्वाय हस्तं: the hand-taking** 💍 *(the pāṇigrahaṇa verse)*

```
गृभ्णामि ते सौभगत्वाय हस्तं मया पत्या जरदष्टिर्यथासः ॥
```

*Translit:* gṛbhṇāmi te saubhagatvāya hastaṃ mayā patyā jaradaṣṭir yathāsaḥ ||
*EN:* "I take your hand for good fortune: that with me as husband you may reach old age."
*Source:* Ṛgveda 10.85.36 (first line of the hand-taking rite).

**3. पश्येम शरदः शतं जीवेम शरदः शतम्: the hundred autumns** 🎂👥

```
पश्येम शरदः शतं जीवेम शरदः शतम् ॥
```

*Translit:* paśyema śaradaḥ śataṃ jīvema śaradaḥ śatam ||
*EN:* "May we see a hundred autumns; may we live a hundred autumns."
*Source:* Ṛgveda 7.66.16 (parallel: Śukla YV 36.24, recited at a child's first outing). ⚠️ NOT RV 1.89.9 (that verse is "where sons are with fathers"). The singular birthday form "jīva tvaṃ śaradaḥ śatam" is a modern adaptation. Wedding variant: RV 10.85.39 ("may he live a hundred autumns").

**4. Culture box: the wedding rites** 💍
- **सप्तपदी** saptapadī, the seven steps: "sakhā saptapadā bhava" ("be a friend by seven steps"), the seventh step seals the marriage. *(Kāṭhaka Gṛhya Sūtra 25.41.1; Āśvalāyana GS 1.7.19; Pāṇini 5.2.2.)* ⚠️ Gṛhya-Sūtra liturgy, NOT RV 10.85.
- **माङ्गलसूत्र** maṅgalasūtra: ⚠️ NOT Vedic (the Gṛhya Sūtras are silent); medieval Smārta and regional (tāḷi in the south). The Laghu-Āśvalāyana Smṛti 15.33 tying-mantra ends "sā jīva śaradaḥ śatam" ("live, blessed one, a hundred autumns").

*Word notes: सन्तान saṃtāna (offspring, lineage; MBh 1.103.10) · वंश vaṃśa (lineage; Amarakośa 2.7.1) · कुटुम्ब kuṭumba (family, kinsmen; Manu 11.22) · गृह gṛha (home; RV 10.85.26 "gṛhān gaccha gṛhapatnī," "go to your home, mistress of the house").*

**5. Pali: the family quarters** 👪 *(DN 31: the safe family-ethics core)*

```
Mātāpitā disā pubbā, ācariyā dakkhiṇā disā;
puttadārā disā pacchā, mittāmaccā ca uttarā.
```

*Translit:* Mātāpitā disā pubbā, ācariyā dakkhiṇā disā; puttadārā disā pacchā, mittāmaccā ca uttarā
*EN (Sujato):* "Parents are the east, tutors the south, wives and child the west, friends and colleagues the north." (The sutta closes: "By honoring these quarters a householder does enough for their family.")
*Source:* Sigālovāda Sutta, DN 31 §34. ⚠️ There is no "six ways of cherishing" in DN 31: the duties are fivefold, set in six directions.

**6. Pali: the debt to parents** 👪 *(AN 2.33)*

```
Dvinnāhaṃ, bhikkhave, na suppatikāraṃ vadāmi.
Katamesaṃ dvinnaṃ? Mātu ca pitu ca.
```

*Translit:* Dvinnāhaṃ, bhikkhave, na suppatikāraṃ vadāmi. Katamesaṃ dvinnaṃ? Mātu ca pitu ca
*EN (Sujato):* "There are two people who are not easy to repay. Which two? Mother and father."
*Source:* Aṅguttara Nikāya 2.33 (and: one repays them by grounding them in faith, ethics, generosity, and wisdom).

**7. Pali: the husband's duties** 💍 *(DN 31 §30.2: the sendable half)*

```
sammānanāya anavamānanāya anaticariyāya
issariyavossaggena alaṅkārānuppadānena.
```

*Translit:* sammānanāya anavamānanāya anaticariyāya issariyavossaggena alaṅkārānuppadānena
*EN (Sujato):* "by treating her with honor, by not looking down on her, by not being unfaithful, by relinquishing authority to her, and by presenting her with adornments."
*Source:* Sigālovāda Sutta, DN 31 §30.2. *(The wife's reciprocal list reads period-typical: this half is the sendable one.)*

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

*Every line awaits your native sign-off. Transliteration is the verifier's scheme. 🚩 items carry the handle-with-care rule.*

**1. ขอให้ครองคู่กันอย่างมีความสุขนะ** 💍

```
ขอให้ครองคู่กันอย่างมีความสุขนะ
```

*Translit:* khǎw hâi khrɔɔng khûu kan yàang mii khwaam sùuk ná · *EN:* "May you two live as a couple, happily."
*(Source: ครองคู่ attested in Lexitron and subtitle corpora; the full pattern is a wedding-list standard.)*

**2. รักกันนานๆ นะ** 💍

```
รักกันนานๆ นะ
```

*Translit:* rák kan naan naan ná · *EN:* "Love each other for a long time!"
*(Source: wedding-wish lists; very warm, friends-to-the-couple register.)*

**3. ขอให้รักกันยืนยาว** 💍

```
ขอให้รักกันยืนยาว
```

*Translit:* khǎw hâi rák kan yʉʉn-yaao · *EN:* "May your love last long."
*(Source: ยืนยาว attested in Lexitron; short card/LINE wish.)*

**4. ขอให้ครอบครัวมีความสุขนะ** 👪

```
ขอให้ครอบครัวมีความสุขนะ
```

*Translit:* khǎw hâi khròp-khrua mii khwaam sùuk ná · *EN:* "May your family be happy."
*(Source: wide natural use; the standard gloss of 家庭幸福 in Thai press.)*

**5. ยินดีด้วยกับสมาชิกใหม่ของครอบครัวนะ** 👶

```
ยินดีด้วยกับสมาชิกใหม่ของครอบครัวนะ
```

*Translit:* yin-dii dûay gàp sà-maachik mài kɔ̌ɔng khròp-khrua ná · *EN:* "Congratulations on the new member of the family!"
*(Source: card-text guides and e-card categories; used after a birth, so it welcomes an existing baby rather than wishing for fertility.)*

**6. ขอให้แม่และลูกแข็งแรงนะ** 🤰🚩 *(only when wanted)*

```
ขอให้แม่และลูกแข็งแรงนะ
```

*Translit:* khǎw hâi mâe láe lûuk khɛ̂ɛng-raaeng ná · *EN:* "May mother and baby be strong."
⚠️ The dictionary-correct "แม่และเด็ก" is clinical (hospital register, แผนกแม่และเด็ก): prefer "แม่และลูก" as above. Warm companion line for late pregnancy: **คลอดง่ายๆ นะ** ("may the delivery be easy," attested in the wild); for the baby: **ขอให้ลูกน้อยแข็งแรง** ("may the little one be healthy").

**7. ขอให้มีทายาทสืบสกุล** 🚩 *(handle with care: formal and explicitly pro-natal)*

```
ขอให้มีทายาทสืบสกุล
```

*Translit:* khǎw hâi mii thaayaat sʉ̀ʉp-sà-kun · *EN:* "May you have heirs to continue the family line."
⚠️ Formal, literary, patrilineal (ทายาท carries legal register). Only when children are explicitly wanted. If you want warmth without fertility pressure, use line 4; if children ARE wanted, the traditional abundance register exists too ("ขอให้มีลูกเต็มบ้าน มีหลานเต็มเมือง") and carries the same flag.

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **The Eþel and Beorc rune poems are Anglo-Saxon (Old English), not Old Norse**: the ON younger-futhark poems have no óðal rune; ON material here is the óðal vocabulary plus the Icelandic Maðr line
- **The guessed Œþel text ("...gesælðig werian, in gifena gehwylcum") is not the attested text**: the poem reads "gif he mot ðær rihtes and gerysena on brucan on bolde bleadum oftast" (Dickins)
- **"Vesper adest, iuvenes..." is Catullus 62, not 61** (and possibly not Catullus at all); Catullus 61's refrain is "o Hymenaee Hymen, o Hymen Hymenaee"
- **The Hymen cry is not in Thesmophoriazusae**, attested in *Peace* 1331ff and *Birds* 1731ff
- **Sappho fr. 112 is the "happy bridegroom" fragment**: fr. 44 is the Hector–Andromache narrative poem
- **Berkanan "family/fertility/mother" is modern esoteric**: the attested Beorc line is a barrenness image ("twigs without offspring")
- **Juno Pronuba's scene (Aen. 4.166) is doom-laden**: culture note only; and **Persephone's pomegranate is underworld register**: the bridal pomegranate custom is modern folklore, not classical
- **"jīvema śaradaḥ śatam" is RV 7.66.16, not 1.89.9**: RV 1.89.9 is the "where sons are with fathers" verse; the singular birthday form is a modern adaptation
- **Saptapadī mantras are Gṛhya-Sūtra liturgy, not RV 10.85**; and **the maṅgalasūtra is not Vedic** (Gṛhya Sūtras silent; medieval Smārta and regional)
- **"पुत्रपौत्राभिवृद्धिरस्तु" is not the attested pūṇyāhavācana wording**: attested forms: "putra-pautra-samṛddhir astu" / "putra-pautra-saṃpad-astu" (which travel right after the धनधान्य line in [[04-Prosperity-Wishes]]); the -ābhivṛddhi- compound is solid as Lakṣmī Sahasranāma name 973
- **DN 31 has no "six ways of cherishing"**: fivefold duties, six directions
- **Thai "แม่และเด็ก" is clinical** (hospital register); prefer "แม่และลูก"
- **🚩 Fertility/children register: handle with care, only when wanted**: ทายาทสืบสกุล is formal and patrilineal; "many sons" lines (including RV 10.85.45's "ten sons") stay flagged or excluded
- **Never promise outcomes**: "may you be happy," never "this will keep you together"
- **Cross-ref, don't stack**: Hector's prayer stays in [[05-Protection-Wishes]]; the relatives-dedication and wish-tree in [[08-Dream-Wishes]]; ร่มโพธิ์ร่มไทร in [[03-Health-Wishes]]
