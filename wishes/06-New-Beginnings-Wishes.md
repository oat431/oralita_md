## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**What's actually attested:** the Norse "new beginning" material is small and precious: a preserved **morning invocation** hailing day itself (Sigrdrífumál st. 3, the valkyrie's waking prayer), the everyday greeting **"be hale"** (*ves heill / ver þú heill*, Hymiskviða 11), and the calendar's own poem about Day (Anglo-Saxon rune poem, "Dæg"). No attested Norse "new year blessing" survives, and the famous runic "fresh start" readings are modern; they get labeled as such below.

### Snippets

**1. Heill dagr!** 🌱🪜 *(the morning hail, Sigrdrífumál st. 3)*

```
Heill dagr! Heilir dags synir!
Heil nótt ok nift!
```

*EN (Bellows 1923):* "Hail, day! Hail, sons of day! And night and her daughter now! Look on us here with loving eyes, that waiting we victory win."
*Source:* Sigrdrífumál st. 3, Poetic Edda: Sigrdrífa's waking invocation to day, night, and the gods; a preserved pagan morning acclamation. (Some editions read *nipt* for *nift*; Bellows numbers it st. 2.)

**2. Ver þú heill** 🪜 *(the "be hale" greeting, a send-off for any new chapter)*

```
Ver þú heill!
```

*EN:* "Be hale [whole, well]!", the attested Norse greeting on meeting or parting. Full attested line (Hymiskviða st. 11): *"…Ver þú heill, Hymir, í hugum góðum"*: "Be hale, Hymir, in good spirits"; Finnur Jónsson's edition prints *Ves heill* in the same line. *(Swapping in a name is an adaptation, labeled.)* ⚠️ The drinking-toast "wassail" story is medieval, not Viking. See Misuse Notes.

**3. Reference: "Dæg", the day-rune poem** 🎆 *(a poem about Day, not a blessing)*

```
Dæg byþ drihtnes sond, deore mannum,
mære metodes leoht, myrgþ and tohiht.
```

*EN (Dickins 1915):* "Day, the glorious light of the Creator, is sent by the Lord; it is beloved of men, a source of hope and happiness…"
*Source:* Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem (10th-c. MS Cotton Otho B.x), in Dickins, *Runic and Heroic Poems of the Old Teutonic Peoples* (1915), ll. 74–76. ⚠️ Never spoken to a person, pure "new day" imagery; "Dagaz = breakthrough" is 20th-c. esoteric runology, not in the poem.

*Vocabulary notes: upphaf (beginning, origin; "at upphafi," at first) · nýr (new) · dagsbrún (dawn, literally "day's brow") · heill (good luck, omen; "góðu heilli" = bono augurio; the word behind both "hale" and "wassail").*

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Quod bonum, faustum felixque sit** 🎆 *(the Roman formula, spoken whenever anything new begins)*

```
Quod bonum, faustum felixque sit.
```

*EN:* "May it be good, auspicious, and happy" (the phrase a Roman magistrate, king, or speaker used when opening an undertaking or announcing a plan).
*Source:* Livy 1.17 (*"…regem create"*) and 1.28. ⚠️ The fuller form "quod bonum faustum felix *fortunatumque* sit" (abbrev. Q.B.F.F.S.) is epigraphic (the Arval Acts) and Suetonius has the short "quod bonum felixque sit" (*Cal.* 15.3). Don't attribute the long form to Livy.

**2. Dimidium facti, qui coepit, habet; sapere aude, incipe** 🌱🎓 *(the commencement pair)*

```
Dimidium facti, qui coepit, habet;
sapere aude, incipe.
```

*EN:* "He who has begun has half the deed done; dare to be wise, begin."
*Source:* Horace, *Epistles* 1.2.40–41 (advice to Lollius); "sapere aude" later became Kant's Enlightenment motto.

**3. Omnium enim rerum principia parva sunt** 🌱 *(small beginnings, the reflective one)*

```
Omnium enim rerum principia parva sunt.
```

*EN:* "For the beginnings of all things are small."
*Source:* Cicero, *De Finibus* 5.58 (Piso's discourse). ⚠️ Quote-sites drop the "enim". The text has it.

**4. Bonis avibus!** 🪜 *(the send-off: "under good omens")*

```
Bonis avibus!
```

*EN:* "With good birds!" = under favourable omens, the classical phrase for starting a journey or venture.
*Source:* Ovid, *Met.* 15.640 ("Ite bonis avibus…"), *Fasti* 1.513. ⚠️ "bonis auspiciis" is NOT the classical set phrase. Use *bonis avibus*.

**5. Reference: Janus at the hinge of the year** 🎆 *(the calendar's own beginning)*

```
Bruma novi prima est veterisque novissima solis:
principium capiunt Phoebus et annus idem.
```

*EN:* "Midwinter is the first day of the new sun and the last of the old: Sun and year alike take their beginning there."
*Source:* Ovid, *Fasti* 1.163–164: Janus explaining the year's start. (His two-faced invocation stands just above, 1.65–66.)

**6. Reference: "Principiis obsta"** 🍀 *(cautionary, not a blessing)*

```
Principiis obsta; sero medicina paratur.
```

*EN:* "Resist the first beginnings; medicine comes too late."
*Source:* Ovid, *Remedia Amoris* 91: a didactic warning; usable only for a "start now / quit now" send, never as a wish.

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. The far-shining face** 🎓🪜 *(Pindar's own proem, first pick for a new work)*

```
ἀρχομένου δ' ἔργου πρόσωπον
χρὴ θέμεν τηλαυγές.
```

*Translit:* archomenou d' ergou prosōpon chrē themen tēlauges · *EN:* "At the beginning of a work one must set a far-shining face."
*Source:* Pindar, *Olympian* 6.3–4: the ode's opening program. ⚠️ It is NOT a fragment, whatever quote-sites say.

**2. ἀρχὴ ἥμισυ παντός** 🌱🎆 *(the proverb, "well begun")*

```
ἀρχὴ ἥμισυ παντός.
```

*Translit:* archē hēmisy pantos · *EN:* "The beginning is half of the whole."
*Source:* quoted by Plato, *Laws* 6.753e: "ἀρχὴ γὰρ λέγεται μὲν ἥμισυ παντὸς ἐν ταῖς παροιμίαις ἔργου…": "for the beginning is said, in the proverbs, to be half of the whole work." An old proverb Plato quotes, not his coinage. ⚠️ Not to be confused with Hesiod's different proverb (*πλέον ἥμισυ παντός*, WD 40: "the half is MORE than the whole").

**3. Aratus: "let us begin from Zeus"** 🪜 *(the incipit formula)*

```
ἐκ Διὸς ἀρχώμεσθα, τὸν οὐδέποτ' ἄνδρες ἐῶμεν ἄρρητον.
```

*Translit:* ek Dios archōmestha, ton oudepot' andres eōmen arrhēton · *EN:* "Let us begin from Zeus, whom we men never leave unspoken."
*Source:* Aratus, *Phaenomena* 1: the classic "begin every undertaking with the god" proem.

**4. Homer's dawn** 🌱 *(quote-flavor, "first light"; not a blessing)*

```
ἦμος δ' ἠριγένεια φάνη ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς.
```

*Translit:* hēmos d' ērigeneia phanē rhododaktylos Ēōs · *EN (Murray):* "When early-born rose-fingered Dawn appeared."
*Source:* the daybreak formula: *Iliad* 1.477 & 24.788; *Odyssey* twenty times (2.1, 3.404, …). Epic narration. Send as dawn imagery, never as a spoken blessing.

*Culture: the Noumenia (νουμηνία):* in Athens, the first day of each lunar month was the **Noumenia**: sacred to Selene, Apollo **Noumenios**, Hestia, and the household gods; "the holiest of days," kept free of other festivals, marked by a public rite on the Acropolis and private offerings (incense, garlands, wine, barley cakes) on freshly cleaned altars. Herodotus 6.57: the Spartan kings received a victim for Apollo "on every new moon." ⚠️ The modern line "auspicious day to begin new projects" is revival, not ancient. The attested day is a calendar festival. (Mikalson, *HTR* 65 (1972) 291–296; *The Sacred and Civil Calendar of the Athenian Year*, 1975.)

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. वक्रतुण्ड महाकाय…: the Gaṇapati invocation** 🪜📝 *(before any undertaking, prayer register)*

```
वक्रतुण्ड महाकाय सूर्यकोटि समप्रभ ।
निर्विघ्नं कुरु मे देव सर्वकार्येषु सर्वदा ॥
```

*Translit:* vakratuṇḍa mahākāya sūryakoṭi samaprabha | nirvighnaṁ kuru me deva sarvakāryeṣu sarvadā ||
*EN:* "O Lord of the curved trunk, mighty-bodied, radiant as a crore of suns, make my undertakings free of obstacles, always."
*Source:* the traditional Gaṇeśa *mangalācaraṇa*: text form standard across stotra collections. ⚠️ No single śāstric locus. Label it "traditional prayer"; don't cite a Purāṇa (the Mudgala Purāṇa's Vakratuṇḍa is a deity-form, not this verse).

**2. ॐ गं गणपतये नमः: the mūla-mantra** 🪜 *(japa before beginnings)*

```
ॐ गं गणपतये नमः
```

*Translit:* oṁ gaṁ gaṇapataye namaḥ · *EN:* "Oṁ, homage to Gaṇapati."
*Source:* Gaṇapati Atharvashirsha, Mantra-prakaraṇa v. 7.

**3. नववर्षस्य हार्दाः शुभकामनाः** 🎆 *(new year, modern Sanskritized register)*

```
नववर्षस्य हार्दाः शुभकामनाः।
```

*Translit:* nava-varṣasya hārdāḥ śubhakāmanāḥ · *EN:* "Heartfelt good wishes for the New Year."
*Source:* modern Sanskritized greeting (attested in sanskritdocuments' New Year wishes; Hindi kin: "नववर्ष की शुभकामनाएँ"). ⚠️ Use **नववर्ष**. "नूतनवर्ष" is effectively unused (0 vs 118 full-text hits).

**4. Ārambhatha…: the effort verse** 🌱 *(Pali, "begin!")*

```
Ārambhatha nikkamatha, yuñjatha buddhasāsane;
dhunātha maccuno senaṁ, naḷāgāraṁva kuñjaro.
```

*Translit:* ārambhatha nikkamatha, yuñjatha buddhasāsane; dhunātha maccuno senaṁ, naḷāgāraṁva kuñjaro
*EN:* "Begin! Go forth! Yoke yourselves to the Buddha's teaching. Shake off Death's army like an elephant a reed-hut."
*Source:* Theragāthā 3.13 (Abhibhūtattheragāthā), PTS Th 256. ⚠️ NOT Dhammapada 382 (the internet says so; it isn't), and editions vary: SC "ārambhatha nikkamatha," PTS "ārabhatha nikkhamatha."

**5. Etaṁ maṅgalam uttamaṁ: the blessing seal** 🎆🙏 *(Pali, chanted in Thailand as มงคลสูตร)*

```
Etaṁ maṅgalam uttamaṁ.
```

*Translit:* etaṁ maṅgalam uttamaṁ · *EN:* "This is the highest blessing."
*Source:* Khuddakapāṭha 5 = Sutta Nipāta 2.4, the refrain closing every verse of the Maṅgalasutta. (SC prints the sandhi joined: *maṅgalamuttamaṁ*.)

**6. Bhavatu sabba maṅgalaṁ: the full blessing chant** 🏠🎆 *(traditional paritta, for ceremonies and new homes)*

```
Bhavatu sabba maṅgalaṁ, rakkhantu sabbadevatā;
sabba-buddhānubhāvena, sadā sotthī bhavantu te.
```

*EN:* "May every blessing be yours; may all the devas protect you; through the power of all the Buddhas, may you always be well."
*Source:* traditional Theravāda paritta/blessing chant (post-canonical, not in the Tipiṭaka). Protection-adjacent, cf. [[05-Protection-Wishes]].

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

**1. สวัสดีปีใหม่** 🎆

```
สวัสดีปีใหม่
```

*Translit:* sà-wàt-dii pii-mài · *EN:* "Happy New Year."
Works for 1 Jan and loosely at Songkran; add นะ/ครับ/ค่ะ; classic extension: ขอให้มีความสุขในปีใหม่นะ. *(Source: standard greeting; th.wiktionary.)*

**2. สุขสันต์วันสงกรานต์** 🎆

```
สุขสันต์วันสงกรานต์
```

*Translit:* sùk-sǎn wan sǒng-krān · *EN:* "Happy Songkran Day."
⚠️ The spelling is **สุขสันต์**. "สุขสันติ์" is not a dictionary word (it survives only in names). *(Source: standard card line; media-verified.)*

**3. ขอแสดงความยินดีกับบัณฑิตใหม่** 🎓

```
ขอแสดงความยินดีกับบัณฑิตใหม่
```

*Translit:* khɔ̌ɔ sà-dɛɛng kwaam-yin-dii kàp bandìt mài · *EN:* "Congratulations to the new graduate."
Campus shorthand for the about-to-graduate: **ว่าที่บัณฑิต** ("soon-to-be graduate"). *(Source: attested congrats patterns; RID for บัณฑิต.)*

**4. ขอให้เจริญก้าวหน้า** 🎓🪜

```
ขอให้เจริญก้าวหน้า
```

*Translit:* khɔ̌ɔ-hâi jà-rəən kâao-nâa · *EN:* "May you advance and prosper."
⚠️ Bare เจริญ can turn sarcastic between friends. The **ก้าวหน้า** collocation is the safe blessing.

**5. ขอให้งานใหม่ราบรื่นนะ** 🪜

```
ขอให้งานใหม่ราบรื่นนะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̌ɔ-hâi ngaan mài râap-rʉ̂ʉn ná · *EN:* "May the new job go smoothly."
ราบรื่น: RID's own example sense is exactly work-smoothness ("งานสำเร็จลงอย่างราบรื่น"). Variant: ขอให้ประสบความสำเร็จในงานใหม่: "may you succeed in the new job."

**6. ยินดีกับบ้านใหม่นะ** 🏠

```
ยินดีกับบ้านใหม่นะ
```

*Translit:* yin-dii kàp bâan mài ná · *EN:* "Congrats on the new home."
Alternative: ขอให้มีความสุขในบ้านใหม่นะ: "may you be happy in the new home." The protection-register housewarming line stays in [[05-Protection-Wishes]]. Cross-ref, don't double. *(Source: real usage; ขึ้นบ้านใหม่ RID.)*

**7. ขอให้เป็นการเริ่มต้นที่ดีนะ** 🌱 *(natural construction, awaiting native sign-off)*

```
ขอให้เป็นการเริ่มต้นที่ดีนะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̌ɔ-hâi pen kaan-rə̂əm-tôn thîi dii ná · *EN:* "May it be a good beginning."
Built from verified parts (เริ่มต้น, การ-nominalization, นะ softener); exact-string usage not yet corpus-attested, flagged for native QA. Variant: ขอให้โชคดีในการเริ่มต้นใหม่: "good luck with the fresh start."

**8. ฟ้าหลังฝน** 🌱 *(the consolation idiom)*

```
ฟ้าหลังฝน
```

*Translit:* fáa lǎng fǒn · *EN:* "The sky after the rain", brighter days after hardship. Full proverb: ฟ้าหลังฝนย่อมงดงามเสมอ: "the sky after rain is always beautiful." *(Source: Thai proverb usage, songs, captions.)*

## The Fresh-Start Send: assembled example `[craft]`

For a friend beginning anything, a move, a year, a chapter:

```
Heill dagr! Heilir dags synir!
Quod bonum, faustum felixque sit.
ขอให้เป็นการเริ่มต้นที่ดีนะ
May your first step be small enough to take, and sure enough to matter.
```

For a graduate:

```
ἀρχομένου δ' ἔργου πρόσωπον χρὴ θέμεν τηλαυγές: begin with a far-shining face.
ขอแสดงความยินดีกับบัณฑิตใหม่
May the world be as ready for you as you are for it.
```

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **Rune "new beginnings" readings are modern**. "Dagaz = breakthrough" & "Berkanan = growth" are 20th-c. esoteric; the poems say Day is the Lord's messenger, and the berec tree is fruitless yet fair (Dickins glosses beorc even as "poplar")
- **"Ves þú heill" is not attested**. Real forms: *ves heill* (FJ) / *ver þú heill* (Neckel–Kuhn), Hymiskviða 11
- **Wassail ≠ ancient toast**: "be hale" was a greeting; the drinking "wassail/drinkhail" is medieval (Geoffrey's Rowena legend; Layamon's Brut)
- **Roman formula forms**: long "…felix fortunatumque sit" is epigraphic (Arval Acts, Q.B.F.F.S.); Livy uses "quod bonum, faustum felixque sit". Don't attribute the long form to Livy
- **ἀρχὴ ἥμισυ παντός** is a proverb Plato quotes (*Laws* 753e), not his invention, not Aristotle/Pythagoras; ≠ Hesiod's πλέον ἥμισυ παντός ("the half is MORE than the whole," WD 40)
- **The Pindar line is *Olympian* 6.3–4**, not a fragment; "fragment" citations are wrong
- **"Bonis auspiciis" is not classical**. use *bonis avibus* (Ovid *Met.* 15.640; *Fasti* 1.513)
- **"Principiis obsta"** (Ovid *Rem.* 91) is cautionary. never send as a blessing
- **Cicero's sentence has "enim"**: "Omnium enim rerum principia parva sunt" (*Fin.* 5.58); quote-sites drop it
- **Noumenia**: "auspicious day to start projects" is modern revival. The ancient day is a calendar festival (see culture box)
- **Pali verse ≠ Dhp 382**: "Ārambhatha…" is Theragāthā 3.13; editions vary (ārambhatha/ārabhatha)
- **Vakratunda śloka**: label "traditional prayer". No śāstric locus; don't cite a Purāṇa
- **Thai spellings**: สุขสันต์ not สุขสันติ์; नववर्ष not नूतनवर्ष; เจริญก้าวหน้า safe, bare เจริญ risky
- **"ขอให้เป็นการเริ่มต้นที่ดีนะ"**, components verified, exact usage not; native QA pending
- **Never promise outcomes**: "may it be auspicious," never "this makes the fresh start"
- **Cross-ref, don't stack**: 05's housewarming line (อยู่เย็นเป็นสุข) and this file's moving lines are different registers. Pick one per message
