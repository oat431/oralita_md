## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**What's actually attested:** Viking-age Thor's-hammer pendants **were** protective amulets, and one Danish find, the **Købelev amulet** (10th c., found 2014), literally says so in runes. Actual "may Thor protect/hallow" formulas survive across amulets and stones. The protective vocabulary: *vernd* (defence, the primary word), *hlíf* (cover, shelter; as of a shield; paired in "vernd ok hlíf").

### Snippets

**1. The Canterbury charm** 🛡️✈️ *(the protection formula, 1073)*

```
Þórr vígi þik, þursa dróttinn.
```

*EN:* "May Thor hallow you, lord of thurses [trolls]." *(Full attested line: "Þōrr vígi þik, þursa drōttinn…", Canterbury charm, BL Cotton Caligula A.xv; earliest English-written charm context.)*
Use: the all-purpose warding, before travel, exams, surgeries.

**2. The hallowing formula** 🏠🛡️ *(from Denmark's runestones)*

```
Þórr vígi þessi rúnar.
```

*EN:* "May Thor hallow these runes." *(Attested on the Glavendrup stone, DR 209, and, as "Þor wigi runaʀ", the Sønder Kirkeby runestone, DR 220.)*
Use: hallow a space, an object, or, meta-cutely, the message itself.

**3. The Kvinneby amulet** ✈️👁️ *(11th c., Öland; the fullest protection prayer)*

```
May Þórr protect him with that hammer
which came out of the sea.
```

*EN translation of the amulet's Rundata reading. The full charm runs: "Here I carved protection for you, Bófi… may the lightning hold all evil away… flee from evilness! … The gods are under him and over him." (Swap "him" for the name when adapting, labeled adaptation.)*
*Source:* Kvinneby amulet (Öl SAS1989;43); quote the translation only. The normalized ON varies by edition.

**4. The amulet that says what it is** 🛡️ `[craft wrapper, attested inscription]`

```
ᚼᛘᛅᚱ × ᛁᛋ: "This is a hammer."
```

*EN of hmar × is (Købelev amulet): "This is a hammer."* The world's only Thor's-hammer pendant that labels itself. Two-line amulet-card version: `This is a hammer. Let it be enough.` `[craft]`
*Source:* National Museum of Denmark, 2014 (runologist Lisbeth Imer). ⚠️ Don't cite a Rundata ID: unconfirmed.

**5. Reference: the famous 'helm' (with the big warning)** 🛡️ *(Fáfnismál 16–17)*

```
Ægishjalm bar ek of alda sonum, meðan ek of menjum lák…
```

*EN (Bellows):* "The fear-helm I wore to afright mankind, while guarding my gold I lay…"
⚠️ **The Ægishjálmr here is a mythic helmet, not a stave sigil.** The familiar crossed-staves symbol is **17th-century Icelandic magic** (Galdrabók era), never Viking-age. Grímnismál contains no ægishjálmr at all (st. 14 is about Fólkvangr). *(Sigrdrífumál 6's victory runes, "Sigrúnar skaltu kunna…" / "Winning-runes learn, if thou longest to win", are the other famous stanza; victory-register, magical instruction, not a casual blessing. Bellows numbers it st. 6.)*

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Di te servent** 🛡️🌙 *(the acclamation)*

```
Di te servent.
```

*EN:* "May the gods keep you." *(Source: Historia Augusta, Severus Alexander 6.3–4, shouted acclamations: "Auguste innocens, di te servent…" ⚠️ NOT Horace: verified absence; the internet misattributes it.)*
Variants: comic Afranius line "Di te sospitem servent tuis": "may the gods keep you safe for your own"; or `Di te servent, di conservent.`: "may the gods keep you, preserve you."

**2. Vade retro me, Satana** 🛡️ *(the Vulgate line, heavy register)*

```
Vade retro me, Satana.
```

*EN:* "Get behind me, Satan." *(Source: Mark 8:33, Vulgate. The St. Benedict medal shortens it to "Vade retro satana." Use for exes, bad habits, and truly awful job offers, with a knowing smile.)*

**3. The St. Benedict medal verse** 🏠🙏 *(Catholic tradition: 15th-c. manuscript, medal formalized 1741)*

```
Crux sacra sit mihi lux,
non draco sit mihi dux.
Vade retro satana,
nunquam suade mihi vana.
```

*EN:* "May the holy cross be my light; may the dragon never be my guide. Begone, Satan. Never tempt me with vanities." (The full medal verse continues: "Sunt mala quae libas: what you offer is evil; ipse venena bibas: drink the poison yourself.")
*Source:* Mettener manuscript (1415, BSB Clm 8201), approved by Benedict XIV, 1741–42. ⚠️ Later ecclesiastical Latin, not classical; and "Non draco" is the medal's spelling, "Numquam" the fuller reading.

**4. Procul, o procul este, profani** 🏠 *(warding a space, the Sibyl's cry)*

```
Procul, o procul este, profani.
```

*EN:* "Away, away, O ye unhallowed!" *(Source: Vergil, Aeneid 6.258, the Sibyl opening the rites. Ritual exclusion line: great for housewarmings, new offices, and dramatic door-signs.)*

*Culture note: the **fascinum** (Pliny, NH 28.39), the amulet that "defends even generals from envy" and hung beneath triumphal chariots; and Roman crowds invoking Nemesis against bewitchment. The evil eye was public infrastructure in Rome.*

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. Theocritus's anti-evil-eye charm** 👁️ *(3rd c. BC, the spitting rite)*

```
ὡς μὴ βασκανθῶ δέ, τρὶς εἰς ἐμὸν ἔπτυσα κόλπον.
```

*Translit:* hōs mē baskanthō de, tris eis emon eptysa kolpon · *EN:* "So that I be not bewitched, three times I spat into my bosom." *(Source: Theocritus, Idyll 6.39. ⚠️ Exact wording: "τρὶς εἰς **ἐμὸν** ἔπτυσα κόλπον". The googled shorter variant garble omits "ἐμόν." Baskania = the evil eye; cf. Pliny's nurses spitting thrice.)*
Fun fact for the recipient: the next line credits the technique: "the old woman Kotyttaris taught me this."

**2. The guard line** 🛡️✈️ *(Diomedes to Athena, Iliad 10.291)*

```
ὣς νῦν μοι ἐθέλουσα παρίσταο καί με φύλασσε.
```

*Translit:* hōs nyn moi ethelousa paristao kai me phylasse · *EN:* "So now stand by me willingly and guard me." *(Source: Homer, Iliad 10.291.)*
The best short attested Greek "guard me" prayer. Send it to anyone facing a gauntlet.

**3. Hector's prayer for his son** 🎂🛡️ *(Iliad 6.476–481, blessing over a child)*

```
Ζεῦ ἄλλοι τε θεοί, δότε δὴ καὶ τόνδε γενέσθαι
παῖδ᾽ ἐμόν, ὡς καὶ ἐγώ περ, ἀριπρεπέα Τρώεσσιν…
```

*Translit:* Zeu alloi te theoi, dote dē kai tonde genesthai paid' emon, hōs kai egō per, ariprepea Trōessin… · *EN:* "Zeus and ye other gods, grant that this my child may likewise prove, even as I, pre-eminent amid the Trojans…" *(Source: Iliad 6.476–478; the prayer continues: valiant in might, and that some man say of him returning from war, "He is better far than his father.")*

*Culture: "apotropaic" comes from ἀποτρόπαιος, "averting evil", an epithet of Apollo; the Gorgoneion's glare was the classic averting device (Athena's aegis, Agamemnon's shield).*

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**The paritta tradition**, the framing that matters: *paritta* (Pali) literally means **"protection"**. Protective chanting is a documented Theravada practice, and Thailand's royal chanting book (1880) is the standard paritta collection. These lines are **prayers**, solemnity register.

**1. The Ratana Sutta refrain** 🙏🛡️ *(chanted protection, the most-recited line in paritta)*

```
Etena saccena suvatthi hotu.
```

*Translit:* etena saccena suvatthi hotu · *EN:* "By this truth, may you be well!" *(Source: Kp 6 / Sn 2.1, the refrain closing each verse of the Ratana Sutta. ⚠️ Must include "Etena". The bare "saccena suvatthi hotu" is not the canonical line.)*
Bonus close from the same sutta: `Buddhaṁ namassāma suvatthi hotu.`: "We bow to the Buddha, may you be safe!"

**2. The made-safeguard line** 🛡️ *(from the snake-bite paritta, AN 4.67)*

```
Katā me rakkhā, katā me parittā.
```

*Translit:* katā me rakkhā, katā me parittā · *EN:* "I have made this safeguard. I have made this protection." *(Source: AN 4.67, the Khandha Paritta, taught by the Buddha for a monk's safety. ⚠️ Cite AN 4.67, NOT "SN 20.6" (that's the Archers sutta, unrelated).)*

**3. Protect me, lotus-eyed Vishnu** 🙏 *(Skanda Purāṇa, devotional)*

```
त्राहि मां पुण्डरीकाक्ष विष्णो संसारसागरात्।
```

*Translit:* trāhi māṁ puṇḍarīkākṣa viṣṇo saṁsārasāgarāt · *EN:* "Protect me, O lotus-eyed Viṣṇu, from the ocean of worldly existence." *(Source: Skanda Purāṇa, Puruṣottama-Māhātmya 2.2.44.9; "trāhi māṁ" is a stock protection formula across the Purāṇas.)*

**4. The triple peace** 🌙🛡️ *(seals every Vedic prayer)*

```
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥
```

*Translit:* oṁ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ · *EN:* "Om, peace, peace, peace." *(Source: closes the śānti-pāṭha of the Taittirīya Upaniṣad, Śikṣāvallī; the triple repetition is canonical, the ancient "warding complete" seal.)*

*Available but heavy (use with care):* Gītā 18.66: "सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज। अहं त्वां सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः॥": "Abandoning all duties, come to me alone for refuge; I will free you from all sins. Do not grieve." A full surrender verse, longer than a chat wish.

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

**1. Safe travels** ✈️

```
ขอให้เดินทางปลอดภัยนะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi dəən-thaang plɔ̀ɔt-phai ná · *EN:* "May your journey be safe."
Formal/written variant (RID's own example): `ขอให้เดินทางโดยสวัสดิภาพ`: "may you travel safely."

**2. The Buddhist protection** 🙏🛡️

```
ขอให้พระคุ้มครองนะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi phrá khúm-khrɔɔng ná · *EN:* "May the Buddha protect you." (พระ = the Buddha/the sacred; the everyday caring blessing.) Drop นะ / add ครับ–ค่ะ for elders.

**3. The spared-from-harm wish** 🛡️ *(amulet-context classic)*

```
ขอให้แคล้วคลาดปลอดภัย
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi khláew-khlâat plɔ̀ɔt-phai · *EN:* "May you be spared all harm and kept safe." *(RID: แคล้วคลาด "รอดไป, พ้นไป.")* Jokey-protective variant: `ขอจงแคล้วคลาดกลับมาครบ 32`: "may you come back with all 32 [body parts]."

**4. New home** 🏠 *(RID ties this to ขึ้นบ้านใหม่ ceremonies)*

```
ขอให้อยู่เย็นเป็นสุข
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi yùu yen pen-sùk · *EN:* "May you live cool and happy", the classic housewarming blessing, for body, home, and heart.

**5. May the sacred powers keep you** 🛡️ *(living modern formula, components dictionary-verified)*

```
ให้สิ่งศักดิ์สิทธิ์คุ้มครองนะ
```

*Translit:* hâi sìng sàk-sìt khúm-khrɔɔng ná · *EN:* "May the sacred powers protect you."

**6. The safety sign-offs** 🌙 *(the loving check-ins)*

```
ดูแลตัวเองด้วยนะ
```

*Translit:* duu-lææ tua-eeng dûai ná · *EN:* "Take care of yourself, okay?"

```
ถึงแล้วบอกด้วยนะ
```

*Translit:* thʉ̌ng-lɛ́ɛw bɔ̀ɔk dûai ná · *EN:* "Tell me when you arrive." Love disguised as logistics.

*Culture notes (living practices, respect register):* **สายสิญจน์ sai sin**: the white thread monks hold while chanting and tie on wrists, for blessing and protection · **พระเครื่อง phra khrueang**: Buddha-image amulets, "revered as protection from danger" (RID) · **สักยันต์ sak yant**: protective tattoos with real obligations; recipients follow each school's rules (never fashion) · **ศาลพระภูมิ san phra phum**: the spirit house, a home for the land's guardian · **พุทโธ phuttho**: "the Buddha," used as a recitation word in the forest tradition (⚠️ not พุทโธ่, which is the "oh dear" sigh, different word).

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **"Þur uiki þer" has no attestation. Do not use.** Real formulas: *Þórr vígi þik* (Canterbury charm) and *Þórr vígi þessa rúnar* (Glavendrup DR 209; Sønder Kirkeby DR 220 is a *stone*, not an amulet)
- **Købelev's Rundata ID is unconfirmed**. Cite the National Museum/Imer instead
- **The Ægishjálmr stave symbol is 17th-c. Icelandic, not Viking**, and Grímnismál contains none; the Eddic helm is Fáfnir's mythic helmet
- **"Di te servent" is NOT Horace**. It's a Historia Augusta acclamation; also Cicero's "Fam. 14 conservent" line couldn't be verified. Don't spread it
- **The St. Benedict verse is medieval/ecclesiastical Latin**. Never call it classical; "Non" vs "Numquam" spelling varies
- **Kvinneby: quote the translation only**. Normalized ON text varies by edition
- **Theocritus exact wording** includes "ἐμόν". Use "τρὶς εἰς ἐμὸν ἔπτυσα κόλπον," not the short internet variant
- **No φύλαξον/σῶσον exists in the Homeric Hymns** (verified absence); Sophocles' σῶσον (Phil. 501) addresses a human, not a god
- **"καλῶς εἰσελθόντι" is not real**. Never use
- **Khandha Paritta = AN 4.67**, not SN 20.6; **"Nidhīnaṁ va pavattāraṁ" is Dhp 76** (about wise critics), not a protection text; the Ratana refrain needs its "Etena"
- **พุทโธ ≠ พุทโธ่**. Mind the mai ek
- **Sak Yant, amulets, sai sin are living practices with rules**. Respectful register always; never costume
- **Protection ≠ promise**: "may you be kept safe," never "nothing will happen to you"
