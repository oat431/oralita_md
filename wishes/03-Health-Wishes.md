## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**The healing runes that are real** — Sigrdrífumál (Poetic Edda) contains the actual attested healing-rune stanzas: **Bjargrúnar** (st. 9, birth-help runes) and **Limrúnar** (st. 11, for would-be healers). ⚠️ But both are *rune-lore — magical instructions*, not casual blessings. And the sagas carry a warning: in Egils saga (ch. 73), wrongly-carved runes made a girl ill, and Egil had to scrape them off and re-carve properly —

```
Skalat maðr rúnar rísta,
nema ráða vel kunni.
```

*EN:* "A man should not carve runes unless he can read them well." — the oldest "don't play with what you don't understand" in the Germanic record. *(Source: Egils saga ch. 73.)*

There's also a rune people reach for in health wishes: **ᚢ Uruz**. Attested meaning: **aurochs** (Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem: "proud… very savage beast… fights with its horns"). The strength/vitality/healing reading is **modern**, not attested — labeled, like all rune readings in this library.

### Snippets

**1. The heill ok sæll send-off** 🏥🤒 *(attested warrior-age formula)*

```
Far heill ok sæll.
```

*EN:* "Fare hale and happy." *(Source: Magnús saga blinda, farewell: "Far heill ok sæll, herra byskup".)*
Use before someone heads into treatment — and when they come home, flip it to the welcome form:

```
Kom heill ok sæll.
```

*EN:* "Come hale and happy." *(Source: Njáls saga ch. 116: "Kom heill ok sæll, frændi…".)*
⚠️ Use Kom/Far forms — *"Ver heill ok sæll" is modern Icelandic, not classical.*

**2. The healing-hands line** 🤒💪 *(from Sigrdrífumál st. 4)*

```
ok læknishendr, meðan lifum
```

*EN:* "…and healing hands, while we live." *(Source: Sigrdrífumál st. 4 — part of the poem's solemn blessing list.)*
Use: for caretakers and patients alike — a line about the hands, not the magic.

**3. Eir's line** 🏥 `[craft — attested epithet as invocation]`

```
Eir, læknir beztr — watch over this one.
```

*EN:* "Eir, best of leeches [physicians] — …" *(Source: Gylfaginning ch. 35: "Þriðja er Eir. Hon er læknir beztr." — "The third is Eir, who is the best leech.")*
⚠️ Note: the manuscript reads **beztr**, not the common "best" copy. Eir is cited, not conjured — keep it light.

**4. Reference — the attested healing-rune stanzas** 🏥 *(rune-lore register: read as history, don't deploy casually)*

```
Bjargrúnar skaltu kunna, ef þú bjarga vilt
ok leysa kind frá konum.
(Birth-help runes — Sigrdrífumál st. 9)
```

```
Limrúnar skaltu kunna, ef þú vilt læknir vera,
ok kunna sár at sjá.
(Branch-runes — Sigrdrífumál st. 11; some editors read lifrúnar, "life-runes")
```

*Bellows translations: "Birth-runes learn, if help thou wilt lend…" / "Branch-runes learn, if a healer wouldst be…"* ⚠️ Bellows numbers these stanzas 8 and 10 — his list runs one lower than Neckel/Kuhn.

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Si vales, bene est, ego valeo** 🤒💪 *(the letter formula — the original "hope you're well")*

```
Si vales, bene est, ego valeo.
```

*EN:* "If you are well, it is well; I am well."
*Source:* Cicero, *Ad Familiares* 14.8 (his own get-well letter to Terentia, who had fallen into a fever); also Fam. 5.1, 14.15. Seneca notes it as the old Roman custom (*Ep.* 15.1). Murray's 1917 study lists ~15 instances plus abbreviations and adds it's "constantly found in Egyptian papyri." Modern abbreviation: S.V.B.E.E.V. *(the abbreviation is modern — don't call it classical)*.

**2. Orandum est ut sit mens sana in corpore sano** 💪

```
Orandum est ut sit mens sana in corpore sano.
```

*EN:* "You should pray for a sound mind in a sound body."
*Source:* Juvenal, *Satire* 10.356. It is *literally* a prayer-format line — Juvenal's point is that this, not money or power, is what's worth asking the gods for. (Next line: "fortem posce animum" — "ask for a brave spirit.")

**3. Vale / Valete** 🍀🤒 *(the everyday Latin "be well")*

```
Vale.
```

*EN:* "Be well." (plural: `Valete.`)
*Source:* Cicero, *Fam.* 14.8: "Cura, ut valeas. Vale."; plural to family in *Fam.* 14.2: "Valete, mea desideria, valete." From *valeo*, to be well/strong — the standard Roman sign-off.

**4. Salus — reference** *(Roman goddess of health)*
The personification of health and public well-being; her state temple was vowed in 303 BC (Livy 9.43; 10.1). Greek counterpart: Hygieia. One-liner for context, not a wish.

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. ὑγίαινε** 🏥🤒 *(the health close)*

```
ὑγίαινε
```

*Translit:* hygiaine · *EN:* "Be in good health."
*Source:* standard variant closing of Greek letters (papyri 1st c. BC–AD: BGU VIII 1874, BGU XVI 2661, SB XXIV 15909; Nachtergaele 2015). ⚠️ **No plural form** — ὑγιαίνετε is unattested; for plural use ἔρρωσθε below.

**2. ἔρρωσο / ἔρρωσθε** 🏥💼 *(the universal close)*

```
ἔρρωσο
```

*Translit:* errhōso · *EN:* "Be strong." (plural: `ἔρρωσθε` *errhōsthe*)
*Source:* the standard letter-closing (Nachtergaele ch. 7: almost all variants studied are "verbs other than ἔρρωσο"); plural appears in twelve letters; Acts 15:29 (SBLGNT) ends "Ἔρρωσθε." *(Don't cite Acts 23:30 — that closer is Textus Receptus-only.)*

**3. ἐρρῶσθαί σε εὔχομαι** 🍀🤒 *(the full topos)*

```
ἐρρῶσθαί σε εὔχομαι
```

*Translit:* errhōsthai se euchomai · *EN:* "I pray that you are well."
*Source:* the complete health-wish formula (SB XX 14453; P.Oxy. VII 1063). The ancient ancestor of "sending you good vibes."

**4. The Hippocratic Oath opening** 🏥🙏 *(oath-prayer — respect register)*

```
Ὄμνυμι Ἀπόλλωνα ἰητρὸν καὶ Ἀσκληπιὸν
καὶ Ὑγείαν καὶ Πανάκειαν…
```

*Translit:* Omnymi Apollōna iētron kai Asklēpion kai Hygieian kai Panakeian… · *EN:* "I swear by Apollo Healer, by Asclepius, by Hygieia, by Panacea…" (Loeb 1923)
*Source:* the Hippocratic Oath opening. Spelling varies across editions (Ὑγείαν / Ὑγίειαν). Send it to a doctor, not as a meme — it's their tradition.
*(Asclepius context: Homeric Hymn 16 — "ἰητῆρα νόσων Ἀσκληπιόν" — "Asclepius, healer of sicknesses.")*

*(Note: "εἰς ὑγίειαν" appears in ancient Greek — LXX Ezekiel 47:12 — but as "for health," not a toast. The drinking-toast is modern Greek. Don't cite it as an ancient tradition.)*

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. Dhammapada 204 — health is the greatest gain** 💪🍀

```
आरोग्यपरमा लाभा, सन्तुट्ठिपरमं धनं;
विस्सासपरमा ञाति, निब्बानं परमं सुखं।
```

*Translit:* ārogyaparamā lābhā, santuṭṭhiparamaṁ dhanaṁ; vissāsaparamā ñāti, nibbānaṁ paramaṁ sukhaṁ.
*EN:* "Health is the most precious gain, contentment the greatest wealth; a trustworthy person the best kinsman, Nibbāna the highest bliss." (Buddharakkhita)
*Source:* Dhammapada 204 (Sukhavagga; SuttaCentral + Access to Insight). In Thai teaching it's the first of the "four supremes" (สี่บรม).
*Thai rendering — the traditional one is:* `ความไม่มีโรคเป็นลาภอันประเสริฐ` — ⚠️ **not** "สุขภาพเป็นลาภอันประเสริฐ" (modern wordplay; the traditional form says "the absence of disease").

**2. Mṛtyuñjaya mantra — the death-conquering prayer** 🏥🙏 *(living prayer — for serious illness, with respect)*

```
ॐ त्र्यम्बकं यजामहे सुगन्धिं पुष्टिवर्धनम्।
उर्वारुकमिव बन्धनान्मृत्योर्मुक्षीय माऽमृतात्॥
```

*Translit:* oṃ tryambakaṁ yajāmahe sugandhiṁ puṣṭivardhanam | urvārukam iva bandhanān mṛtyor mukṣīya mā'mṛtāt
*EN:* "Tryambaka we worship, sweet augmenter of prosperity — as from its stem the cucumber, so may I be released from death, not reft of immortality." (Griffith)
*Source:* Ṛgveda 7.59.12, the Mahāmṛtyuñjaya ("great death-conqueror"), traditionally recited for the sick. (Critical saṃhitā text has no ॐ and reads मामृतात्; the ॐ + माऽमृतात् form is the standard japa/devotional spelling — don't "correct" either.)

**3. चिरं जीव** 🎂 *(elders to juniors — the affectionate one)*

```
चिरं जीव
```

*Translit:* ciraṃ jīva · *EN:* "Live long!"
*Source:* Rāmāyaṇa, Ayodhyākāṇḍa 4.39 — Kauśalyā to Rāma: "वत्स राम चिरं जीव"; also Matsya Purāṇa 275, and Bhāsa's plays. Used by elders to children/juniors.

**4. आयुष्मान् भव** 🎂❤️ *(the classic blessing-reply)*

```
आयुष्मान् भव सौम्य
```

*Translit:* āyuṣmān bhava saumya · *EN:* "May you be long-lived, gentle one."
*Source:* Manusmṛti 2.125 — the prescribed reply to a salutation ("आयुष्मान्भव सौम्येति वाच्यो विप्रोऽभिवादने"). The formal warmth of the Sanskrit world.

**5. सर्वे सन्तु निरामयाः** 🤒 *(the disease-free line)*

```
सर्वे सन्तु निरामयाः
```

*Translit:* sarve santu nirāmayāḥ · *EN:* "May all be free from illness."
*Source:* the health-line of the sarve bhavantu verse (nir + āmaya, "disease" — corpus-confirmed in MBh 10.15.25 "पार्थाः सन्तु निरामयाः"). ⚠️ Traditional blessing with no traced classical locus — do **not** attribute it to the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (unsupported).

*Culture notes: आयुर्वेद āyurveda — "knowledge of life" (āyus + veda), the medicine tradition framed as taught by धन्वन्तरि Dhanvantari, the divine physician.*

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

**1. Get well soon** 🤒 *(informal-warm; add ครับ/ค่ะ for elders)*

```
ขอให้หายเร็วๆนะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi hǎai reo-reo ná · *EN:* "Get well soon."
Variants: `ขอให้หายไวๆนะ` (very common) · `ขอให้หายป่วยไวๆนะ` (explicit from illness). RID: หาย = "พ้นจากการเจ็บป่วย".

**2. May you be healthy** 💪

```
ขอให้สุขภาพแข็งแรง
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi sùk-khà-phâap khæ̌ng-reeng · *EN:* "May you be healthy and strong."
*Source:* dictionary-verified (สุขภาพ = "ภาวะที่ปราศจากโรคภัยไข้เจ็บ"); standard in Songkran and elders' blessings.

**3. The surgery wish** 🏥 *(refined form — keep the การ)*

```
ขอให้การผ่าตัดผ่านไปด้วยดีนะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi kaan phà-tàt phàan pai dûai dii ná · *EN:* "May your surgery go well."
Variant: `ขอให้การผ่าตัดราบรื่นและปลอดภัยนะ` — "may the surgery be smooth and safe." Pairs with `ขอให้ปลอดภัยนะ` (may you be safe). Both ผ่านไปด้วยดี and ราบรื่น dictionary-verified.

**4. Take care of your health** 🍀 *(affectionate sign-off)*

```
ดูแลสุขภาพด้วยนะ
```

*Translit:* duu-lææ sùk-khà-phâap dûai ná · *EN:* "Take care of your health, okay?"
Variant: `ดูแลตัวเองด้วยนะ` — "take care of yourself." Also the affectionate nag: `ไปหาหมอนะ` — "go see the doctor, okay?"

**5. Rest / burnout** 😮💨

```
ขอให้หายเหนื่อยไวๆนะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi hǎai nʉ̀ai wai-wai ná · *EN:* "May your tiredness pass soon."
Softer: `ขอให้ได้พักผ่อนเยอะๆนะ` — "may you get plenty of rest."

**6. Longevity — for elders** 🎂 *(the two classic lines)*

```
ขอให้อายุยืนยาว
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi aa-yú yʉʉn-yaao · *EN:* "May you live long."
And the beautiful one:

```
อยู่เป็นร่มโพธิ์ร่มไทรของลูกหลาน
```

*Translit:* yùu pen rôm-phoo rôm-sai khɔ̌ɔng lûuk-lǎan · *EN:* "May you remain the sheltering tree of your children and grandchildren."
ร่มโพธิ์ร่มไทร (Royal Institute Dictionary 2554): "ที่พึ่ง, ผู้ที่ให้ความคุ้มครองและความอบอุ่นใจ" — a refuge who gives protection and warmth of heart. Used on Mother's/Father's Day and elders' birthdays. *(Temple-term bonus: อายุวัฒนมงคล — the longevity-blessing ceremony term.)*

## The Recovery Ward Kit — assembled example `[craft]`

For a friend heading into treatment, send before, then after:

```
Far heill ok sæll — fare hale and happy.
ἐρρῶσθαί σε εὔχομαι — I pray you are well.
```

```

```

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **The healing runes are rune-LORE** (Sigrdrífumál) — magical instructions, and Egils saga warns against deploying runes carelessly ("a man should not carve runes unless he can read them well"). Cite them; don't craft with them
- **ᚢ Uruz "strength/vitality" is modern esoteric** — attested meaning: aurochs
- **Gylfaginning reads "læknir beztr"**, and **Bellows' Sigrdrífumál stanza numbers run one lower** than Neckel/Kuhn
- **"Ver heill ok sæll" is modern** — classical forms are Kom/Far
- **ὑγιαίνετε does NOT exist** — use ὑγίαινε (sg.) or ἔρρωσθε (pl.)
- **The Mṛtyuñjaya mantra is a living prayer** for the seriously ill — solemnity register, never a joke; the japa spelling is standard, don't "fix" it
- **Dhp 204's Thai form is "ความไม่มีโรคเป็นลาภอันประเสริฐ"** — not the "สุขภาพ…" wordplay
- **"εἰς ὑγίειαν" is not an ancient toast** — modern Greek
- **Serious illness: presence first, poetry second** — for grief, the condolence rules of [[04-Wishing-Note-Guide]] apply; luck-style humor never
