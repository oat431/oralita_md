## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**What's actually attested:** ᚱ **Rad**, the riding rune (wry: easy to talk about indoors, hard on the road), an actual **Eddic send-off** (Frigg blessing Odin's journey in Vafþrúðnismál 4), and a road-wisdom stanza from Hávamál. ⚠️ The Rad poem is Anglo-Saxon (Latin-script transmission, like Fehu, Dæg and Eþel); the Eddic lines are Old Norse.

### Snippets

**1. ᚱ The Rad stanza (Anglo-Saxon)** ✈️ *(the riding rune)*

```
Rad byþ on recyde rinca gehwylcum
sefte ond swiþhwæt, ðamðe sitteþ on ufan
meare mægenheardum ofer milþaþas.
```

*EN (Dickins 1915):* "Riding seems easy to every warrior while he is indoors, and very courageous to him who traverses the high-roads on the back of a stout horse."
*Source:* Anglo-Saxon Rune Poem st. 5 (Dickins vv. 13–15). Wry rune-lore, not a blessing: the road is always easier in the telling.

**2. Heill þú farir!** 🚢 *(Frigg's send-off: the attested Norse departure blessing)*

```
Heill þú farir! Heill þú aftr komir!
Heill þú á sinnum sér!
```

*Translit:* Heill thu farir, heill thu aftr komir, heill thu a sinnum ser · *EN:* "Hail you fare! Hail you come back! Hail on your ways!"
*Source:* Vafþrúðnismál 4, the Poetic Edda: Frigg's words to Óðinn as he sets out to Vafþrúðnir. The closest thing in the Eddas to a formal journey blessing, and distinct from "far heill ok sæll" in [[03-Health-Wishes|03]]. (Cleasby–Vigfusson quote the same formula.)

**3. Hávamál 10: the road burden** ✈️ *(traveller's wisdom)*

```
Byrði betri berr-at maðr brautu at
en sé mannvit mikit.
```

*EN:* "No better burden does a man bear on the road than much good sense."
*Source:* Hávamál 10 (Guðni Jónsson numbering at heimskringla.no). Gnomic advice, not a blessing: a send-off about travelling wisely.
*Vocabulary notes (Cleasby–Vigfusson 1874): ferð (travel, journey; ferðalag, travelling; ferðamaðr, traveller) · vegr (way, road; fara vel til vegar, to be well on one's way) · fara (to go, fare, travel: the verb behind English "fare") · vegfarandi (wayfarer).*

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Bene ambula et redambula** 🚢 *(the Roman "bon voyage")*

```
Bene ambula et redambula.
```

*EN:* "Good walking, and good walking back" (a good journey there and back).
*Source:* Plautus, *Captivi* 899; the shorter "bene ambula" also at *Asinaria*, *Mercator* 326 and *Mostellaria* 851. Everyday colloquial farewell in comedy: warm, casual, not religious.

**2. Patria est, ubicumque est bene** 🌍 *(the migration comfort)*

```
Patria est, ubicumque est bene.
```

*EN:* "My country is wherever I am well."
*Source:* Cicero, *Tusculanae Disputationes* 5.108, quoting the voice of Teucer (Pacuvius' *Teucer*). ⚠️ The familiar "Ubi bene, ibi patria" is a later proverb reshaping, not Cicero's wording.

**3. Navigare necesse est** 🚢 *(the sailor's line, with an honest label)*

```
Navigare necesse est, vivere non est necesse.
```

*EN:* "To sail is necessary; to live is not."
*Source:* ⚠️ Greek in origin: Plutarch, *Life of Pompey* 50, "Πλεῖν ἀνάγκη, ζῆν οὐκ ἀνάγκη": Pompey's retort when the helmsmen hesitated in a gale. The Latin wording is a LATER rendering, not classical Latin. Label it as such.

**Culture: St. Christopher, patron of travellers** ✈️
Greek Χριστόφορος, "Christ-bearer"; a martyr of probably the 3rd century; the legend had spread through France by the mid-9th century; the oldest surviving picture is at Mount Sinai, from Justinian's time (527–565); a 1386 brotherhood in Tyrol and Vorarlberg was founded "to guide travellers over the Arlberg." The medal is later devotional practice: respect register, never a charm. *(Catholic Encyclopedia 1913.)*

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. οἴκαδέ τ' ἐλθέμεναι: the day of return** 🏡 *(νόστος in one line)*

```
οἴκαδέ τ' ἐλθέμεναι καὶ νόστιμον ἦμαρ ἰδέσθαι.
```

*Translit:* oikade t' elthemenai kai nostimon ēmar idesthai · *EN:* "To come home and see the day of return."
*Source:* *Odyssey* 3.233 (the formula repeated at 5.220 and 6.311). νόστος (nostos), "return home," is the epic noun behind "nostalgia" (LSJ; Od. 1.5, 1.13). Epic excerpt, used as a wish.

**2. ὥς κε μάλ' ἀσκηθὴς: the unscathed return** 🏡 *(Od. 5.26)*

```
ὥς κε μάλ' ἀσκηθὴς ἣν πατρίδα γαῖαν ἵκηται.
```

*Translit:* hōs ke mal' askēthēs hēn patrida gaian hikētai · *EN:* "That he may reach his own fatherland all unscathed."
*Source:* *Odyssey* 5.26 (Zeus speaking of Telemachus). Epic narrative excerpt used as a wish.

**3. Ζεὺς Ξένιος: the stranger's god** 🚢👥 *(culture and line)*

```
Ζεὺς δ' ἐπιτιμήτωρ ἱκετάων τε ξείνων τε,
ξείνιος, ὃς ξείνοισιν ἅμ' αἰδοίοισιν ὀπηδεῖ.
```

*Translit:* Zeus d' epitimētōr hiketaōn te xeinōn te, xeinios, hos xeinoisin ham' aidoioisin opēdei · *EN:* "Zeus, protector of suppliants and strangers: Zeus Xenios, who attends on reverend strangers."
*Source:* *Odyssey* 9.270–271. Culture: the **herms**, square stone pillars of Hermes set at doorways and sanctuaries (Thucydides 6.27.1, the night of the mutilation in 415 BC); Hermes escorts travellers and souls. ⚠️ "Hermes protector of travellers" as a fixed cult title is later framing; the classical anchor is the herm itself plus Hermes as escort and messenger.

*Word notes: ὁδός (way, road; Od. 4.389, 1.315) · ξεῖνος (guest-friend, the tie that Zeus Xenios guards).*

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. स्वस्ति न इन्द्रो…: the svasti blessing** 🛤️🙏 *(the Vedic well-being verse)*

```
स्वस्ति न इन्द्रो वृद्धश्रवाः स्वस्ति नः पूषा विश्ववेदाः ।
स्वस्ति नस्तार्क्ष्यो अरिष्टनेमिः स्वस्ति नो बृहस्पतिर्दधातु ॥
```

*Translit:* svasti na indro vṛddhaśravāḥ / svasti naḥ pūṣā viśvavedāḥ / svasti nas tārkṣyo ariṣṭanemiḥ / svasti no bṛhaspatir dadhātu
*EN:* "Well-being be ours from Indra of ancient fame; well-being from Pūṣan, knower of all wealth; well-being from Tārkṣya of the unbroken wheel-rim; may Bṛhaspati place well-being in us."
*Source:* Ṛgveda 1.89.6. ⚠️ Only v.6 is the svasti verse (v.7 is the Maruts; v.8 is a different blessing): quote v.6 alone. The Pūṣan pāda is the journey-apt line.

**2. सुगा नः सुपथा कृणु: Pūṣan, lord of paths** ✈️🛤️

```
अति नः सश्चतो नय सुगा नः सुपथा कृणु ।
पूषन्निह क्रतुं विदः ॥
```

*Translit:* ati naḥ saścato naya sugā naḥ supathā kṛṇu / pūṣann iha kratuṁ vidaḥ
*EN:* "Lead us past those who pursue us; make our paths easy to travel, O Pūṣan: find here the power for it."
*Source:* Ṛgveda 1.42.7. The whole hymn 1.42 is a road-prayer to **Pūṣan**, the Vedic god of roads (also RV 6.53.1, "pathas pate," Lord of the Path; RV 1.89.5, Pūṣan our guardian).

**3. स्वस्ति गच्छ: the epic send-off** 🚢

```
नमो धात्रे विधात्रे च स्वस्ति गच्छ ह्यनामयम् ।
स्वस्ति तेऽस्त्वान्तरिक्षेभ्यः पार्थिवेभ्यश्च भारत ॥
```

*Translit:* namo dhātre vidhātre ca svasti gaccha hy anāmayam / svasti te 'stv āntarikṣebhyaḥ pārthivebhyaś ca bhārata
*EN:* "Homage to the Ordainer and the Disposer: go in well-being, free from harm. May there be well-being for you from the powers of the sky and of the earth, O Bhārata."
*Source:* Mahābhārata 3.37.25 (Āraṇyakaparva), spoken by **Draupadī** (कृष्णा) as Arjuna departs north to Indra for divine weapons. Companion half-verse 3.37.24: "आपृष्टो मेऽसि कौन्तेय स्वस्ति प्राप्नुहि पाण्डव" ("you have taken leave of me; attain well-being"). ⚠️ "svasti prāpta" is not attested (the imperative is svasti prāpṇuhi); "kṣemaṁ gaccha" has zero corpus hits: never print it.

*Word notes: मार्ग mārga (way, road; MBh 3.37.27) · पथिन् páthin (path; RV 6.53.1) · यात्रा yātrā (journey: dictionary gloss only, no citation pinned) · क्षेम kṣema (safety, welfare; BhG 9.22 yoga-kṣema).*

**4. Pali: three journey notes** 🚶
- **cārikaṁ caramāno** ("walking the road"): the Buddha's and the monks' walking tours, the journey itself as practice (AN 3.63 §1.1).
- **DN 32 Āṭānāṭiya**: the protection paritta "for the guarding, protection, non-harming, and comfortable living" of the fourfold assembly (guttiyā rakkhāya avihiṁsāya phāsuvihārāya). ⚠️ DN 32 contains no travel wording; the traveller use is later paritta practice. No quotable blessing line exists in it (gap kept honestly).
- **svāgataṁ** ("welcome"): the arrival greeting, host to guest (AN 6.34 §2.4: "ehi kho, mārisa moggallāna; svāgataṁ"). Use as colour under a Thai welcome-home line, not alone.

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

*Every line awaits your native sign-off. Transliteration is the verifier's scheme.*

**1. ขอให้เดินทางราบรื่นนะ** ✈️

```
ขอให้เดินทางราบรื่นนะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̂ɔ hâi dəən-thaaŋ râap-rɯ̂ɯn ná · *EN:* "May your trip go smoothly."
*(Source: RID 2554 ราบรื่น "smooth, free of obstacles"; live collocation เดินทางราบรื่น. NOT a safety wish, so it does not duplicate the protection file.)*

**2. ขอให้สนุกกับการเดินทางนะ** ✈️

```
ขอให้สนุกกับการเดินทางนะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̂ɔ hâi sà-nùk gàp gaan dəən-thaaŋ ná · *EN:* "Enjoy the journey."
*(Source: live usage and press gloss. Leisure travel only: fun is the wrong frame for emigration.)*

**3. ขอให้โชคดีระหว่างเดินทางนะ** 🚢

```
ขอให้โชคดีระหว่างเดินทางนะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̂ɔ hâi chôok-dii rá-wàang dəən-thaaŋ ná · *EN:* "Good luck on your travels."
*(Source: โชคดี = "bon voyage" in Volubilis; ระหว่างเดินทาง attested in live posts. Short casual variant.)*

**4. ขอให้ชีวิตใหม่ในต่างแดนราบรื่นนะ** 🌍

```
ขอให้ชีวิตใหม่ในต่างแดนราบรื่นนะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̂ɔ hâi chii-wít mài nai tàang-daen râap-rɯ̂ɯn ná · *EN:* "May your new life abroad go smoothly."
*(Source: ชีวิตในต่างแดน attested collocation; ราบรื่น as above. ⚠️ Keep ใน: "ชีวิตใหม่ต่างแดน" is headline-compressed and not the attested form.)*

**5. ขอให้ตั้งตัวได้ไวๆ นะ** 🌍

```
ขอให้ตั้งตัวได้ไวๆ นะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̂ɔ hâi tâng-tua dâi wai-wai ná · *EN:* "May you get established quickly."
*(Source: RID 2554 / Lexitron ตั้งตัว "establish oneself." Warm and practical.)*

**6. ขอให้เดินทางถึงบ้านโดยสวัสดิภาพนะ** 🏡

```
ขอให้เดินทางถึงบ้านโดยสวัสดิภาพนะ
```

*Translit:* khɔ̂ɔ hâi dəən-thaaŋ thʉ̌ng-bâan dooi sà-wàt-dì-phâap ná · *EN:* "May you get home safely."
*(Source: RID 2554 สวัสดิภาพ; press gloss of "get home safely." Prefer the full เดินทางถึงบ้าน over clipped ถึงบ้าน. Homecoming variant of the protection file's line: cross-reference, don't duplicate.)*

**7. ยินดีต้อนรับกลับบ้าน** 🏡

```
ยินดีต้อนรับกลับบ้าน
```

*Translit:* yin-dii dtɔ̂ɔn-ráp glàp-bâan · *EN:* "Welcome home."
*(Source: ยินดีต้อนรับ attested in subtitles and social posts; the standard welcome-home.)*

⚠️ **ไปดีนะ** is NOT in this file: it doubles as a death or breakup farewell in many contexts ("ไปดีนะลูก," breakup songs). Fine between close friends who both know the frame; risky in a cheerful travel card.

## The Journey Sends: assembled examples `[craft]`

For a farewell at the door:

```
Heill þú farir! Heill þú aftr komir!
Bene ambula et redambula.
ขอให้เดินทางราบรื่นนะ
Go well, and take the good weather with you.
```

For a homecoming:

```
οἴκαδέ τ' ἐλθέμεναι καὶ νόστιμον ἦμαρ ἰδέσθαι.
ยินดีต้อนรับกลับบ้าน
Welcome back. Nothing changed, except how much you were missed.
```

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **Catullus 101 "ave atque vale" is a funeral epicedium** (spoken "ad inferias," at the grave offerings): never a travel farewell, despite the travel language of its first line
- **"Ubi bene, ibi patria" is not Cicero verbatim**: the attested line is "Patria est, ubicumque est bene" (*Tusc.* 5.108)
- **"Navigare necesse est" is Greek in origin** (Plutarch, *Pomp.* 50); the Latin is a later rendering, not classical Latin
- **The Rad rune poem is Anglo-Saxon**, not Old Norse (like Fehu, Dæg and Eþel); the guessed text needed fixing ("sefte ond," and "ðamðe" is one word)
- **Vafþrúðnismál 4 is the real Eddic send-off**: "far heill ok sæll" in 03 is not the only Norse departure formula
- **Zoëga 1910 was unreachable this round**: the word glosses are Cleasby–Vigfusson 1874; do not relabel them
- **RV 1.89.6 only** is the svasti verse (v.7 = Maruts; v.8 = a different blessing)
- **"svasti prāpta" and "kṣemaṁ gaccha" are not attested**; the epic imperative is svasti prāpṇuhi (MBh 3.37.24)
- **MBh 3.37's blessing is spoken by Draupadī**, not Kuntī (the "kaunteya" vocative misleads)
- **DN 32 has no travel wording**: the Āṭānāṭiya's traveller use is later paritta practice; avihiṁsā is literally "non-harming," not "safety"
- **Thai**: keep ใน in ชีวิตใหม่ในต่างแดน; ไปดีนะ is a death/breakup farewell, not a cheerful send-off; ราบรื่น (smooth) ≠ ปลอดภัย (safe), which is why line 1 does not duplicate [[05-Protection-Wishes|05]]
- **"Hermes protector of travellers" is later framing**: the classical anchor is the herm itself plus Hermes as escort and messenger
- **Never promise outcomes**: "may you arrive well," never "you will be safe"
- **Cross-ref, don't stack**: travel-warding lines live in [[05-Protection-Wishes]]; Far/Kom heill ok sæll in [[03-Health-Wishes]]; Od. 9.34–36 in [[09-Family-Wishes]]
