## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

### The luck runes (Elder Futhark — attested meanings only)

| Rune | Name | Attested meaning |
|------|------|------------------|
| ᚠ | **Fehu** | livestock → wealth |
| ᚹ | **Wunjo** | joy |
| ᛃ | **Jera** | season/year → harvest, plenty (ON *ár*) |
| ᛞ | **Dagaz** | day |
| ᛖ | **Ehwaz** | horse |
| ᛉ | **Algiz** | elk |

⚠️ "Algiz = divine protection" is **modern esoteric reinterpretation**, not attested (the name means elk/elk-sedge). The rune poems are medieval manuscripts, not Viking-age blessings. Keep the flavor, know the history.

### Hamingja — the lendable luck 🎰

**Hamingja** (ON *hamingja*): a female guardian spirit embodying a person's or family's fortune — attested to **pass to descendants, and to be LENT to a friend before a perilous venture**. Hjalti Skeggiason asked King Olaf II to lend him his hamingja before a dangerous voyage. *(Sources: Orchard 1997, Simek 2007 — via Wikipedia "Hamingja")*

This is why the Viking Rune Wish worked: lending luck is a real Norse act of friendship. Ceremony is the gift.

**2. Hávamál 164 — the heill blessing** 🍀📝💼

```
Heill sá, er kvað, heill sá, er kann, njóti sá, er nam, heilir, þeirs hlýddu.
```

*Translit:* heill sa er kvad, heill sa er kann, njoti sa er nam, heilir theirs hlyddu
*EN:* "Hail to him who spoke, hail to him who knows; may he profit who learned; hail to all who listened."
*Source:* Hávamál 164 (Poetic Edda), closing benediction. *Heill* = "whole, hale, lucky" — the authentic Norse blessing word. Use for any send-off, esp. after someone prepared hard.

**4. Hávamál 76 — the fame line** 📝💼 *(fame, not luck — defiant register)*

```
Deyr fé, deyja frændr, deyr sjálfr it sama;

```

*EN:* "Cattle die, kinsmen die, you yourself will die; but fair fame never dies for him who earns it."
*Source:* Hávamál 76–77 (10th c.). For big exams and interviews, not lotteries.

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Fortis fortuna adiuvat** 💼🎰📝 *(the all-purpose Roman pep talk)*

```
Fortis fortuna adiuvat.
```

*EN:* "Fortune helps the brave."
*Source:* Terence, *Phormio* 203 — Geta urging Antipho to keep his nerve; Cicero already calls it a *vetus proverbium* ("old proverb"). Also Pliny the Elder's reported last words before sailing toward Vesuvius (Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 6.16.11).

**2. Audentis Fortuna iuvat** 🎰🎟

```
Audentis Fortuna iuvat.
```

*EN:* "Fortune helps the bold."
*Source:* Vergil, *Aeneid* 10.284 (Turnus' speech). ⚠️ **Do not** cite "Fortuna favet fortibus" to the Aeneid — that wording is a later misattribution; *Audentis Fortuna iuvat* is the attested line.

**3. Quod bonum, faustum, felix fortunatumque esset** 🍀💼 *(the opener)*

```
Quod bonum, faustum, felix fortunatumque esset.
```

*EN:* "May it be good, auspicious, propitious and fortunate."
*Source:* Cicero, *De Divinatione* 1.102 — the Roman state formula prefixed to all public business (still used to open academic years). Use as an **opener** before an undertaking.

**4. Audentem Forsque Venusque iuvat** ❤️🎰 *(bonus — luck + love)*

```
Audentem Forsque Venusque iuvat.
```

*EN:* "Fortune and Venus both favor the bold."
*Source:* Ovid, *Ars Amatoria* 1.608. Use: romantic gambles, or gacha pulls for a waifu/husbando banner. Honest labeling: Ovid was writing pickup strategy, and it's glorious.

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. ἀγαθῇ τύχῃ** 🍀 *(the opener)*

```
ἀγαθῇ τύχῃ
```

*Translit:* agathēi tychēi · *EN:* "With good fortune / To Good Fortune!"
*Source:* standard formula heading Greek inscriptions and decrees (epigraphic record). Use like "Here's hoping…" — open any wish with it.

**2. εὐτύχει** 🍀💼 *(the authentic "good luck!")*

```
εὐτύχει
```

*Translit:* eutychei · *EN:* "Farewell — be fortunate!"
*Source:* standard epistolary closing in Greek papyrus letters (plural: εὐτυχεῖτε *eutycheite*). The closest ancient equivalent of sending "good luck!" in chat.

**3. The Iliad line (Hector before Achilles)** 📝💼

```
μὴ μὰν ἀσπουδί γε καὶ ἀκλειῶς ἀπολοίμην,
ἀλλὰ μέγα ῥέξας τι καὶ ἐσσομένοισι πυθέσθαι.
```

*Translit:* mē man aspoudi ge kai akleiōs apoloimēn, alla mega rexas ti kai essomenoisi puthesthai.
*EN:* "Only let me not die without a struggle and without glory, but let me first do some great thing that shall be told of men hereafter."
*Source:* Homer, *Iliad* 22.304–305 (verified against PerseusDL corpus; Murray trans.). Also the first movement of Christopher Tin's *Live Gloriously* (Civ VII main theme) — the quote that seeded this project. ⚠️ Defiant-heroic register: exams, finals, competitions — never lighthearted pulls.

**4. Pindar's prayer to Tyche** 🍀🎟 *(the guardian wish)*

```
Λίσσομαι, παῖ Ζηνὸς Ἐλευθερίου,
Ἱμέραν εὐρυσθενέ' ἀμφιπόλει, Σώτειρα Τύχα.
```

*Translit:* lissomai, pai Zēnos Eleutheriou, Himēran eurysthene' amfipolei, Sōteira Tykha.
*EN:* "I entreat you, child of Zeus the Deliverer, Saving Fortune — watch over broad-strong Himera."
*Source:* Pindar, *Olympian* 12.1–2 (verified against PerseusDL; Svarlien trans.). Swap Himera for their city/cause to adapt. *(Τύχη Tyche = Greek goddess of luck; Roman Fortuna.)*

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. The sleeping lion** 📝💼 *(effort > luck)*

```
उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति कार्याणि न मनोरथैः।
न हि सुप्तस्य सिंहस्य प्रविशन्ति मुखे मृगाः॥
```

*Translit:* udyamena hi sidhyanti kāryāṇi na manorathaiḥ | na hi suptasya siṁhasya praviśanti mukhe mṛgāḥ ||
*EN:* "Tasks succeed by effort, not by mere wishing — prey does not walk into the mouth of a sleeping lion."
*Source:* *Hitopadeśa*, prologue (verified via wisdomlib.org + concordant shloka sources). Bless the work, not the dice.

**2. Sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ** 🍀 *(the universal blessing)*

```
सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः, सर्वे सन्तु निरामयाः।
सर्वे भद्राणि पश्यन्तु, मा कश्चिद्दुःखभाग्भवेत्॥
```

*Translit:* sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ, sarve santu nirāmayāḥ | sarve bhadrāṇi paśyantu, mā kaścid duḥkha-bhāg bhavet ||
*EN:* "May all be happy, may all be free from illness, may all see what is good — may no one suffer."
*Source:* widely used śānti/blessing formula closing pūjā and peace-chanting (exact Vedic attribution commonly claimed but not pinpointed in critical editions — "traditional blessing").

**3. Gāyatrī mantra, first line** 🙏 *(prayer — use with respect)*

```
ॐ भूर्भुवः स्वः। तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यं…
```

*Translit:* oṃ bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ | tat savitur vareṇyaṃ…
*EN:* "…the splendor of that divine Savitṛ — may we meditate upon / be worthy of it."
*Source:* Ṛgveda 3.62.10 — among the most documented mantras in existence. ⚠️ A living prayer, not a charm. Never gacha-flip it.

**4. शुभं भवतु** 🍀

```
शुभं भवतु
```

*Translit:* śubhaṃ bhavatu · *EN:* "May it be auspicious / may good come to pass."
*Status:* grammatically sound and used in modern liturgical speech; no classical single-source attestation found — label "traditional formula". Short, warm, safe for everyday wishes.

**5. The Pali bridge** 🍀 *(→ feeds the Thai blessing below)*

```
Abhivādana-sīlissa, niccaṃ vuḍḍh'āpacāyino;
cattāro dhammā vaḍḍhanti — āyu, vaṇṇo, sukhaṃ, balaṃ.
```

*EN:* "For one who honors and respects elders, four things grow: long life, beauty, happiness, strength."
*Source:* Dhammapada 109 (corroborated by SuttaCentral AN 5.43 for āyu/vaṇṇa/sukha as canonical desirable things).

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

**1. ขอให้โชคดี** 🍀 *(the everyday — neutral/casual, universally safe)*

```
ขอให้โชคดี
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi chôhk dii · *EN:* "May you have good luck!"
Casual short form:

```
โชคดีนะ
```

*Translit:* chôhk dii ná

**2. The gacha pull** 🎰 *(casual — ดวง is THE game-luck word)*

```
ขอให้ดวงดี สุ่มได้ของดีนะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi duang dii, sùm dâai khɔ̌ɔng dii ná · *EN:* "May your luck be good — may you pull something good!"

**3. Before the exam** 📝 *(สู้ๆ is THE standard encouragement — never omit it)*

```
ขอให้สอบผ่านนะ สู้ๆ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi sɔ̀ɔp phàan ná, sûu sûu · *EN:* "May you pass — fighting spirit!"

**4. Lottery day** 🎟 *(half-joking; draws on the 1st & 16th monthly)*

```
ขอให้ถูกรางวัลที่ 1 นะ
```

*Translit:* kɔ̌ɔ hâi thùuk raang-wan thîi nùeng ná · *EN:* "May you win first prize!"
Cultural note: เลขเด็ด (*lêk dèt*, "hot numbers") from dreams and omens circulate before every draw — a mainstream phenomenon. Cultural fact, no endorsement; the joke is the gift.

**5. โชคดีมีชัย** 🍀💼 *(slightly formal/celebratory — cards and banners)*

```
โชคดีมีชัย
```

*Translit:* chôhk dii mii chai · *EN:* "Good luck and victory!"

**6. อายุ วรรณะ สุขะ พละ** 🎂🍀 *(the classic blessing — formal-warm)*

```
อายุ วรรณะ สุขะ พละ
```

*Translit:* aa-yú wan-ná sù-ká phá-lá · *EN:* "Long life, beauty, happiness, strength."
The Thai reading of Pali Dhammapada 109 (see Sanskrit §5). The classic birthday/merit-making blessing (given by monks after ตักบาตร).

## The Viking Rune Wish — Reproducible Recipe 🎰

The founding wish, as a template:

1. **Pick 3 runes:** one for the *outcome* (ᚠ Fehu wealth / ᛃ Jera harvest), one for the *feeling* (ᚹ Wunjo joy), one for the *moment* (ᛞ Dagaz day-break / ᛖ Ehwaz movement)
2. **Write the rune string:** e.g. `ᚠᚹᛃ`
3. **One incantation line** naming the three runes
4. **Seal it with hamingja** (the attested move): "I lend you my luck for this one."
5. **Send it BEFORE the pull.** Ceremony is the gift — the rare item is just the story.

Ready-to-send assembled example `[craft]`:

```
ᚠ ᚹ ᛃ
By Fehu's gold, Wunjo's joy, Jera's harvest —
may fortune find your hand.
I lend you my luck for this one. Go pull. 🎰
```

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **Never promise outcomes** — "may the runes favor you," never "this will get you the 5-star"
- **Iliad/Hávamál-76 register is defiant-heroic** — exams and duels, not lottery tickets
- **Gāyatrī is a living prayer** — respect or nothing
- **Algiz "protection" is modern esoteric** — fine as flavor, don't present it as Viking-age fact
- **Condolence/grief is not a luck occasion** — wrong folder entirely → [[04-Wishing-Note-Guide]]
