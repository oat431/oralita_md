## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**What the Norse actually had:** a precise vocabulary of peace: *friðr* (peace, sanctuary), *huggun* (consolation), *ró* (rest), *kyrr* (still); but no verified comfort send. So this section keeps the vocabulary, two attested reference lines, and one house `[craft]` built from attested words. (The popular "patience / fair-weather-after-foul" stanza doesn't exist; see Misuse Notes.)

### Snippets

**1. Friðr ok kyrrleikr** 🕊️🌙 `[craft, attested words, house composition]`

```
Friðr ok kyrrleikr.
```

*EN:* "Peace and tranquillity."
*Source:* both words attested in Zoëga 1910 (*friðr*, *kyrr-leikr*); the two-word wish itself is composed in house style (like [[01-Luck-Wishes|01]]'s "hamingja lend"), not an attested phrase.

**2. Reference: the weather changes** 🌊 *(Hávamál 74; wry realism, not patience)*

```
Fjölð of viðrir á fimm dögum,
en meira á mánuði.
```

*EN (Bellows 1923):* "Full oft does the weather change in a week, and more in a month's time."
*Source:* Hávamál 74 (ON text, heimskringla.no; Bellows via Wikisource). ⚠️ The famous "endure, and fair weather comes after foul" version is a modern proverb. The real stanza is weather-lore, and its flavor is wry.

**3. Reference: the remembrance couplet** 🕊️ *(Hávamál 76–77, register-conditional)*

```
Deyr fé, deyja frændr, deyr sjalfr it sama,
en orðstírr deyr aldregi, hveim er sér góðan getr.
```

*EN (Bellows 1923):* "Cattle die, and kinsmen die, and so one dies one's self; but a noble name will never die, if good renown one gets."
*Source:* Hávamál 76 (standard numbering; Bellows prints it at 77–78, reordering the Fitjung stanza to 76). ⚠️ Register: memento-mori opener, remembrance consolation; **only** for messages honoring someone who died; too heavy for general comfort notes.

*Vocabulary notes: friðr (peace; biðja e-n friðar, to sue for peace; friðar-hof, sanctuary) · huggun (consolation; hugganar-orð, a word of comfort; veita e-m huggun, to grant consolation) · ró (rest, calm; gefa ró reiði, to give rest to wrath) · kyrr (still, quiet; kyrr-latr, calm, peaceful) · sæll (blessed, happy; sæla, bliss; friðsæll, blessed with peace).*

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Ō passi graviōra, dabit deus hīs quoque fīnem** 🌊 *(the opener of Aeneas's consolation)*

```
O passi graviora, dabit deus his quoque finem.
```

*EN:* "You who have suffered worse: a god will grant these troubles an end too."
*Source:* Vergil, *Aeneid* 1.199: Aeneas to his storm-wrecked men (the speech runs 1.198–207; he speaks with heavy heart, faking hope).

**2. Forsan et haec olim meminisse iuvabit** 🌊🕊️ *(the famous line, and the closer)*

```
Forsan et haec olim meminisse iuvabit.
```

*EN:* "Perhaps one day it will be a joy to recall even these things."

```
Durate, et vosmet rebus servate secundis.
```

*EN:* "Endure, and preserve yourselves for better times."
*Source:* *Aeneid* 1.203 and 1.207: the same speech. ⚠️ "Durate…" is line **207**, not 203.

**3. Curae leves loquuntur, ingentes stupent** 😮‍💨 *(when it's too big to talk about)*

```
Curae leves loquuntur, ingentes stupent.
```

*EN:* "Light troubles speak; crushing ones are struck dumb."
*Source:* Seneca, *Phaedra* 607 (Act II, Phaedra's confession). *(la.wikisource prints the spelling variant "locuntur.")*

**4. Bene qui latuit bene vixit** 🌙 *(the quiet-life motto)*

```
Crede mihi, bene qui latuit bene vixit, et intra
fortunam debet quisque manere suam.
```

*EN:* "Believe me, he who has lived hidden has lived well; everyone should stay within his own fortune."
*Source:* Ovid, *Tristia* 3.4.25–26: exile-Ovid advising a friend toward quiet living.

**5. Nil admirari** 🌙 *(serenity, Stoic-tinged)*

```
Nil admirari prope res est una, Numici,
solaque quae possit facere et servare beatum.
```

*EN:* "To be astonished by nothing is, roughly, the one thing, Numicius, that can make a man happy and keep him so."
*Source:* Horace, *Epistles* 1.6.1–2.

**6. Reference: Pax vobis** 🕊️ *(the blessing, with a correction)*

```
Pax vobis.
```

*EN:* "Peace be with you."
*Source:* Vulgate (Clementine), Luke 24:36; John 20:19/21/26: Jesus greeting the disciples. ⚠️ The Bible text is "Pax vobis"; "Pax vobiscum" is the later **liturgical** blessing form. Don't cite it as the verse. Christian register, respect or nothing.

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. Achilles to Priam** 🕊️ *(Il. 24.522–524, the tenderest consolation in Homer)*

```
ἄλγεα δ' ἔμπης ἐν θυμῷ κατακεῖσθαι ἐάσομεν ἀχνύμενοί περ·
οὐ γάρ τις πρῆξις πέλεται κρυεροῖο γόοιο.
```

*Translit:* algea d' empēs en thumō katakeisthai easomen achnumenoi per; ou gar tis prēxis peletai kryeroio gōoio · *EN:* "…let us let our sorrows lie in the heart, grieved though we are; for there is no profit in chilling lament."
*Source:* *Iliad* 24.522–524: Achilles consoling Priam at the ransoming of Hector.

**2. Let what has happened be** 💔 *(Il. 19.65–66, the reconciliation line)*

```
ἀλλὰ τὰ μὲν προτετύχθαι ἐάσομεν ἀχνύμενοί περ,
θυμὸν ἐνὶ στήθεσσι φίλον δαμάσαντες ἀνάγκῃ.
```

*EN:* "But let what has happened be, grieved though we are, taming the dear spirit in our breasts by necessity."
*Source:* *Iliad* 19.65–66: Achilles reconciling with Agamemnon (same wording at 18.112–113; a shorter form at 16.60). ⚠️ This line is NOT at 24.522. See Misuse Notes. *(Romanization omitted this round; the verifier returned no transliteration for this item.)*

**3. τέτλαθι δή, κραδίη** 😮‍💨🌊 *(Od. 20.18; endure, my heart)*

```
τέτλαθι δή, κραδίη, καὶ κύντερον ἄλλο ποτ' ἔτλης.
```

*Translit:* tétlathi dē, kradíē, kaí kýnteron állo pot' étlēs · *EN:* "Endure, my heart. You have borne something even more shameful before."
*Source:* *Odyssey* 20.18: Odysseus steeling his heart in the hall before the suitors. The definitive "endure" line for humiliation and stress.

**4. The praise of Eirene** 🌙🕊️ *(Pax 974–977, Peace herself)*

```
ὦ σεμνοτάτη βασίλεια θεὰ πότνι' Εἰρήνη,
δέσποινα χορῶν, δέσποινα γάμων.
```

*Translit:* ō semnotatē basileia thea potni' Eirēnē, despotina chorōn, despotina gamōn · *EN:* "O most august queen and goddess, Lady Eirene: mistress of dances, mistress of weddings."
*Source:* Aristophanes, *Peace* 974–977 (Trygaeus's prayer at the sacrifice; full line adds "accept our sacrifice"; the epithet πολυτιμήτη "much-honored" follows at 978). ⚠️ The popular epithet "deep-rich peace" is not in the text.

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः: the universal benediction** 🙏🕊️ *(with the big correction)*

```
सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः । सर्वे सन्तु निरामयाः ।
सर्वे भद्राणि पश्यन्तु । मा कश्चिद्दुःखभाग्भवेत् ॥
```

*Translit:* sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ / sarve santu nirāmayāḥ / sarve bhadrāṇi paśyantu / mā kaścid duḥkhabhāg bhavet
*EN:* "May all be happy, may all be free from illness, may all see auspicious things, may no one have a share of suffering."
*Source:* ⚠️ NOT any Upaniṣad: the internet's "Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.14" citation is false. Defensible attestations are near-variants: Garuḍa Purāṇa 35.51, Bhaviṣya Purāṇa 3.2.35.14, Itihāsa Samuccaya āśīrvacanam 2, and the colophon of Uvaṭa's Mantrabhāṣya (c. 11th c.); the popular four-line form is a modern standardization. Register: universal, deity-less benediction, temple/pūjā closing.

**2. ॐ सह नाववतु: the harmony chant** 🙏 *(Taittirīya śāntipāṭha)*

```
ॐ सह नाववतु । सह नौ भुनक्तु । सह वीर्यं करवावहै ।
तेजस्वि नावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै ॥
```

*Translit:* oṃ saha nāvavatu / saha nau bhunaktu / saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai / tejasvi nāvadhītam astu mā vidviṣāvahai
*EN:* "May He protect us both together; may He nourish us both together; may we work together with great vigor; may our study be bright and effective; may we bear no hatred toward one another."
*Source:* Taittirīya Upaniṣad (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda): the śānti pāṭha closing the Brahmānandavallī and Bhṛguvallī (Śaṅkara glosses *saha nau* as teacher and student). ⚠️ Not "Yajurveda Saṃhitā" and not Bṛhadāraṇyaka, whatever the internet says. Register: joint recitation, harmony, sober.

**3. असतो मा सद्गमय: the passage prayer** 🙏🌊 *(Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3.28, verified)*

```
असतो मा सद्गमय । तमसो मा ज्योतिर्गमय ।
मृत्योर्मा अमृतं गमय ॥
```

*Translit:* asato mā sad gamaya / tamaso mā jyotir gamaya / mṛtyor mā 'mṛtaṃ gamaya
*EN:* "Lead me from the unreal to the real, from darkness to light, from death to the deathless."
*Source:* Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.3.28: confirmed verbatim. ⚠️ Solemnity register: funeral/commemorative prayer and great-passage benedictions; a bow, not a chat sticker. (Its free-standing public use is later liturgical custom; in the text it is the prastotṛ's japa.)

**4. लोकाः समस्ताः सुखिनो भवन्तु: the modern benediction** 🌙🙏 `[modern]`

```
लोकाः समस्ताः सुखिनो भवन्तु
```

*Translit:* lokāḥ samastāḥ sukhino bhavantu
*EN:* "May all worlds, all beings, be happy."
*Source:* ⚠️ NOT Vedic or Upaniṣadic: the earliest written locus is a medieval *svasti-prajābhyaḥ* mangala verse (Sangama-dynasty inscriptions, 1336–1485); its real life is modern (Ashtanga yoga closing chant, Amma, Jivamukti). Warm, universal; label modern.

**5. Pali: the Mettā refrain** 💔🙏 *(Khp 9 / Sn 1.8, its proper home)*

```
Sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā.
```

*Translit:* sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā · *EN (Sujato):* "May all beings be happy!"
*Source:* Khuddakapāṭha 9 = Sutta Nipāta 1.8: the Mettā Sutta refrain (root reads the sandhi form *sabbasattā*; *Sabbe sattā…* is the standard normalized chanting form). First appeared in [[02-Love-Wishes]]. Quote from here. Register: loving-kindness chant; universal goodwill.

**6. Pali: the suffusion formula** 💔🕊️ *(AN 10.176 / MN 41)*

```
Sabbe sattā averā hontu, abyāpajjhā hontu,
anīghā hontu, sukhī attānaṁ pariharantu.
```

*Translit:* sabbe sattā averā hontu, abyāpajjhā hontu, anīghā hontu, sukhī attānaṁ pariharantu · *EN (Sujato):* "May all beings be free of enmity and ill will, untroubled, and keep themselves happy."
*Source:* canonical formula at AN 10.176 (Cundasutta) and MN 41; systematized in Paṭisambhidāmagga II 130 and Visuddhimagga ch. 9. ⚠️ Not a line of the Mettā Sutta itself, and *sukhī* takes a long ī.

**7. Pali: Dhp 5** 💔 *(hatred never ends by hatred)*

```
Na hi verena verāni sammantīdha kudācanaṁ;
averena ca sammanti, esa dhammo sanantano.
```

*Translit:* na hi verena verāni sammantīdha kudācanaṁ / averena ca sammanti / esa dhammo sanantano · *EN (Sujato):* "For never is hatred laid to rest by hate. It's laid to rest by love: this is an ancient teaching."
*Source:* Dhammapada 5, Yamakavagga.

**8. Pali: Dhp 184** 🌊 *(patience under provocation)*

```
Khantī paramaṁ tapo titikkhā,
nibbānaṁ paramaṁ vadanti buddhā.
```

*Translit:* khantī paramaṁ tapo titikkhā / nibbānaṁ paramaṁ vadanti buddhā · *EN (Sujato):* "Patient acceptance is the ultimate fervor. Extinguishment is the ultimate, say the Buddhas."
*Source:* Dhammapada 184, Dhammaṭṭhavagga (the full verse continues on non-harming).

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

*Romanization note: this round's verification lane returned spellings and translations but no romanization, so the `*Translit:*` layer is **omitted rather than improvised** (house rule). Hints from already-verified house tokens: ขอให้ = *kɔ̌ɔ hâi* ("may you…"), นะ = *ná* (softening particle). Add full romanization in your native pass, or ask for a source-based backfill. Every line below awaits your native sign-off.*

**1. ขอให้จิตใจสงบนะ** 🕊️

```
ขอให้จิตใจสงบนะ
```

*EN:* "May your mind be at peace." *(Source: สงบ attested: th.wiktionary, Lexitron; press usage.)*

**2. ขอให้มีความสงบสุขนะ** 🕊️

```
ขอให้มีความสงบสุขนะ
```

*EN:* "May you have peace and happiness." *(Source: สงบสุข attested: Lexitron, Volubilis. A notch more formal/dignified, good for cards.)*

**3. ขอให้ความทุกข์คลายลงนะ** 💔

```
ขอให้ความทุกข์คลายลงนะ
```

*EN:* "May your suffering ease." *(Source: คลาย attested: th.wiktionary; ความทุกข์คลายลง press collocation.)*

**4. ขอให้ผ่านช่วงเวลานี้ไปได้นะ** 🌊

```
ขอให้ผ่านช่วงเวลานี้ไปได้นะ
```

*EN:* "May you get through this time." Formal variant: ขอให้ผ่านพ้นช่วงเวลานี้ไปได้ด้วยดี. *(Source: ผ่านพ้น attested: Lexitron; press/social usage.)*

**5. ทำใจสบายๆ** 😮‍💨

```
ทำใจสบายๆ
```

*EN:* "Set your mind at ease / take it easy." *(Source: ทำใจ attested: RID 2554; usage corpus.)* ⚠️ Bare ทำใจ carries "brace yourself / come to terms with loss"; keep the สบายๆ, and don't use in acute grief ("just relax" jars there).

**6. ไม่เป็นไรนะ / สู้ๆ** *(register check, small comfort and encouragement only)*

```
ไม่เป็นไรนะ
```

*EN:* "It's okay / don't worry." *(Source: Lexitron, Volubilis; usage corpus.)* Right for minor mishaps, apologies, check-ins. ⚠️ **Flippant for death or major loss.** สู้ๆ ("hang in there!") is energizing cheer for exams/work/treatment. ⚠️ **Flippant in fresh grief** (it pressures the grieving to fight).

**Condolences live elsewhere:** ขอแสดงความเสียใจ ("please accept my condolences") and เสียใจด้วยนะ are **social phrases** (formal written vs spoken) and belong to the condolence rules of [[04-Wishing-Note-Guide]], not to ritual snippets.

## The Comfort Send: assembled example `[craft]`

For a friend in a rough patch:

```
O passi graviora, dabit deus his quoque finem.
ขอให้ผ่านช่วงเวลานี้ไปได้นะ
This too is a season. May it turn.
```

For grief or heartbreak:

```
ἄλγεα δ' ἔμπης ἐν θυμῷ κατακεῖσθαι ἐάσομεν ἀχνύμενοί περ.
Sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā: may all beings be happy.
ขอให้จิตใจสงบนะ
May your grief be gentle with you, and time gentler still.
```

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **"τὰ μὲν προτετύχθαι…" is NOT Iliad 24.522–524**: that's the Priam consolation with different wording; the famous "let what has happened be" line is 19.65–66 (also 18.112–113; 16.60 without the participle)
- **Vergil's "Durate…" is Aen. 1.207**: the consolation speech runs 1.198–207; don't cite "forsan" (1.203) and "durate" as one line
- **"Pax vobiscum" is not the Vulgate text**: the Bible reads "Pax vobis" (Luke 24:36; John 20:19); "vobiscum" is the later liturgical blessing; "pax et bonum" provenance is unverified. Don't use
- **No Hávamál "patience / fair weather after foul" stanza exists**: only st. 74 (weather changes fast, wry not patient); the "fair weather" version is a modern proverb
- **"Deyr fé" numbering**: standard ON = Hávamál 76–77; Bellows prints 77–78 (he reorders the Fitjung stanza). Register: remembrance/obituary only
- **"Curae leves loquuntur, ingentes stupent" = Seneca, Phaedra 607** (Act II): not an unnumbered "Seneca quote"
- **Aristophanes' "deep-rich peace" epithet does not exist**: the real epithets: σεμνοτάτη βασίλεια θεά, πότνια Εἰρήνη, δέσποινα χορῶν/γάμων (Pax 974–978)
- **"सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः…" is NOT Bṛhadāraṇyaka** (nor Taittirīya 2.2.1): real attestations are Purāṇic/medieval variants (Garuḍa Purāṇa 35.51 etc.); the popular four-line form is modern
- **"lokāḥ samastāḥ sukhino bhavantu" is not Vedic/Upaniṣadic**: medieval mangala inscription + modern yoga closing; label modern
- **Mettā wording precision**: root "sabbasattā bhavantu sukhitattā" (normalized "Sabbe sattā…"); the "averā" formula is AN 10.176/MN 41 with long ī in *sukhī*; not a Mettā Sutta line
- **"sabbe sattā dukkhā pamuccantu" has no canonical locus**: a later karuṇā appendage in modern chanting books; don't quote
- **saha nāvavatu = Taittirīya Upaniṣad** (Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda) śāntipāṭha: not "Yajurveda Saṃhitā," not Bṛhadāraṇyaka
- **asato mā sad gamaya = Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3.28** (that citation holds), but solemnity register: funeral/commemorative, great passages
- **Thai register screens**: bare ทำใจ = "brace yourself"; ไม่เป็นไร/สู้ๆ are flippant in fresh grief; condolences → [[04-Wishing-Note-Guide]]
- **Never promise outcomes**: "may you find peace," never "this will heal you"
- **Cross-ref, don't stack**: ॐ शान्तिः lives in 05; "Bhavatu…" and ฟ้าหลังฝน in 06; pick one register per message
