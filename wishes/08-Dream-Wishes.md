## 1. ᚠ Viking Runes (Old Norse)

**What the Norse actually had:** the word for wish itself (*ósk*, the old cousin of English "wish"), a dream-vocabulary that includes the wish-fulfillment formula "a dream proves true" (*draumr rætist*), and two famous dream-literature items that are reference-only (register verdicts below). No attested Norse wish *formula* exists — the gap is reported honestly.

### Snippets

**1. Yðr gengr allt at óskum** 💫 *(the one true attested wish-line)*

```
Yðr gengr allt at óskum.
```

*EN:* "Everything succeeds to your wishes."
*Source:* the attested idiom, Zoëga 1910 s.v. ósk. Register: warm, a shade formal — perfect for a card.

**2. Draumr rætist** 🌠 *(the attested dream-comes-true formula)*

```
Draumr rætist.
```

*EN:* "A dream proves true."
*Source:* Zoëga 1910 s.v. draumr ("d. rætist, a dream proves true"). Send it as "may the dream come true" — the sentiment is attested; the imperative use is house adaptation.

**3. Óska-byrr** 🌠💫 `[craft — attested compound]`

```
Óska-byrr!
```

*EN:* "A wish-fair wind!" — literally "wish-wind": a fair wind to one's heart's content.
*Source:* the compound is attested in Zoëga 1910 s.v. ósk; the one-word send is composed in house style (like [[01-Luck-Wishes|01]]'s "hamingja lend").

**4. Reference — Draumkvæde** 🌠 *(the Norwegian dream-ballad — reference-only)*

```
Blest is he who in this life gave shoes to the needy poor.
```

*EN (Grøndahl, trans. of Moltke Moe's text):* the refrain runs "The moon it shines, and the roads do stretch so wide"; the ballad's protagonist Olav Åsteson sleeps Christmas Eve to Epiphany on a soul-journey.
*Source:* the medieval Norwegian visionary ballad (NMB 54 / TSB B 31; oral Telemark transmission, written down from the 1840s; Liestøl 1946). ⚠️ The "Blest is he who…" stanzas are alms-beatitudes, not wishes — reference-only; quoted in English only (Norwegian stanza not verified this pass).

**5. Reference — Baldrs draumar: excluded** 🚫 *(register verdict)*
The Eddic dream-poem is Baldr's death-dreams and Odin's necromancy — funeral register, misused online as a generic "dream poem." Excluded from sends; noted here as dream-literature footnote. (Also: it survives only in AM 748 I a 4to, NOT the Codex Regius.)

*Vocabulary notes: ósk (wish, desire — the old cousin of English "wish"; óskasonr, beloved/adopted son) · œskja (to wish — œskja e-m e-s, to wish something for someone) · draumr (dream — segja e-m draum, tell a dream; ráða draum, read a dream).*

## 2. 🦅 Latin

**1. Voti compos** 🎂💫 *(the attested "wish granted" idiom, as a send)*

```
Voti compos!
```

*EN:* "May you be possessor of your wish!"
*Source:* the classical idiom for having one's wish granted — Suetonius, *Caligula* 13 has the accusative "voti compotem fecit" ("he made the Roman people possessors of their wish"); the dictionary form is "compos voti." *(Standalone use is house adaptation of the attested idiom.)*

**2. The gates of dreams** 🌠 *(Aeneid 6.893–898)*

```
Sunt geminae Somni portae, quarum altera fertur
cornea, qua veris facilis datur exitus umbris.
```

*EN:* "Twin are the gates of Sleep; one is said to be of horn, by which easy exit is given to true spirits" (the other, gleaming ivory, whence the Manes send false dreams).
*Source:* Vergil, *Aeneid* 6.893–898 (Anchises' farewell at the end of Book 6). ⚠️ Aeneas himself exits through the IVORY gate (6.898) — the famous crux; don't "fix" it. House adaptation for sends: "May it come to you through the gate of horn." `[craft]`

**3. Reference — the birthday poems** 🎂 *(register: poems, not greetings)*

```
Lucanum canimus, favete linguis;
vestra est ista dies, favete, Musae.
```

*EN:* "We sing Lucan; bring grace with your tongues — that day is yours, bring grace, Muses."
*Source:* Statius, *Silvae* 2.7 (*Genethliacon Lucani ad Pollam*). ⚠️ It is **Silvae 2.7**, not 4.5 — and it is posthumous (Lucan already dead; commissioned by his widow Polla). Horace, *Odes* 4.11 has the other usable birthday color ("almost more sacred than my own birthday" — Maecenas' day; a party invitation). **No short attested classical birthday-wish line exists** — this slot stays an honest gap.

**4. Reference — Ovid's House of Sleep** 🌠 *(text confirmed; line numbers not pinned)*

```
Somnia, quae veras aequent imitamine formas.
```

*EN:* "Dreams that match true things in their imitative shape."
*Source:* Ovid, *Met.* 11 (Ceyx & Alcyone — the House of Sleep). ⚠️ Text confirmed in the corpus this pass; exact line numbers not pinned.

## 3. 🏛️ Ancient Greek

**1. Penelope's gates of dreams** 🌠 *(Od. 19.560–567 — the original "true dreams" text)*

```
δοιαὶ γάρ τε πύλαι ἀμενηνῶν εἰσὶν ὀνείρων·
αἱ μὲν γὰρ κεράεσσι τετεύχαται, αἱ δ' ἐλέφαντι.
```

*Translit:* doiàì gár te pýlai amenēnôn eisìn oneírōn; hai mèn gàr keráessi tetéuchatai, hai d' eléphanti · *EN (Murray):* "For two are the gates of shadowy dreams, and one is fashioned of horn and one of ivory. Those dreams that pass through the gate of sawn ivory deceive men… but those that come forth from the gate of polished horn bring true issues to pass, when any mortal sees them."
*Source:* Homer, *Odyssey* 19.560–567 (Penelope to the disguised Odysseus). House adaptation for sends: "May it come to you through the gate of horn." `[craft]`

**2. The horn of plenty** 🪙 *(culture — κέρας Ἀμαλθείας)*

```
ὅπερ ἂν εὔξαιτό τις, παρέχειν.
```

*EN:* "…whatever one might wish for, it provides."
*Source:* Apollodorus, *Bibliotheca* 2.7.5 — the horn of Amaltheia "had such power that it provided whatever food or drink one might wish for" (the power itself is attributed to Pherecydes). Diodorus 3.68/4.35: the fruitful land was named "Amaltheia's horn" — the cornucopia image. *(Transliteration omitted this round — not returned by the verifier.)* The wish-abundance symbol *par excellence*.

*Word notes: εὐχή (prayer, vow, wish — Od. 10.526) · ὄνειρος (dream — Od. 19.560: "dreams verily are baffling and unclear of meaning").*

## 4. 🕉️ Sanskrit

**1. The wish-tree homage** 🪙🙏 *(kalpavṛkṣa — devotional register)*

```
नमस्ते कल्पवृक्षाय चिन्तितान्नप्रदाय च।
विश्वम्भराय देवाय नमस्ते विश्वमूर्त्तये॥
```

*EN:* "Homage to you, wish-fulfilling tree, giver of food thought of; homage to you, all-sustaining god of universal form."
*Source:* verse from the Dānasāgara, cited in the Śabdakalpadruma s.v. kalpavṛkṣa. The tree itself: one of the five divine trees (Amarakośa 1.1.50); attested in the MBh., Meghadūta, Kumārasambhava 6, Śakuntalā. *(Transliteration omitted this round.)* ⚠️ कल्पद्रुम and कल्पवृक्ष are the same motif, not two myths; "Kalpadruma" as a lexicon name (Śabdakalpadruma) is allusive — a lexicon grants whatever word you seek.

**2. The wish-jewel** 🪙🌠 *(cintāmaṇi — Hindu and Buddhist)*

```
यथा चिन्तामणिं स्पृष्ट्वा लौहं काञ्चनतां भजेत्।
```

*EN:* "As by touching the cintāmaṇi, iron turns to gold."
*Source:* Padmapurāṇa, Uttarakhaṇḍa, cited in the Śabdakalpadruma s.v. cintāmaṇi ("the jewel that grants all desires thought of"); the motif also runs through the Harivaṁśa and, in Buddhism, the Vairocanābhisambodhi-sūtra (Avalokiteśvara's wish-fulfilling jewel; Tibetan *yid bzhin nor bu*). *(Transliteration omitted this round.)* Respect register: living devotional symbolism in both traditions.

**3. जन्मदिन की शुभकामनाएँ** 🎂 `[modern Hindi]`

```
जन्मदिन की शुभकामनाएँ
```

*Translit:* janmadin kī śubhkāmnāẽ · *EN:* "Birthday wishes / happy birthday."
*Source:* modern standard Hindi — slightly more formal than जन्मदिन मुबारक हो ("happy birthday"; *mubārak* is Arabic, via Urdu). ⚠️ Modern Hindi, not Sanskrit.

*Word notes: मनोरथ (manoratha — wish, heart's desire; literally "mind's chariot"; Amarakośa 1.7.27 among the synonyms of इच्छा; attested MBh., Kāv.) · स्वप्न (svapna — dream, sleep; Amarakośa 1.7.36; Suśruta's dream theory).*

**4. Pali — the merit dedication** 💫🙏 *(traditional formula, not a sutta quote)*

```
Idaṃ me puññaṃ āsavakkhayāvahaṃ hotu.
```

*Translit:* idaṃ me puññaṃ āsavakkhayāvahaṃ hotu · *EN:* "May this merit of mine be conducive to the destruction of the taints."
*Source:* the standard Theravāda dedication formula — daily-chanting and paritta books (morning chanting). ⚠️ No canonical sutta locus; it is a traditional chanting formula.

**5. Pali — the dedication to relatives** 🙏 *(register: merit-making days)*

```
Idaṃ me ñātīnaṃ hotu, sukhitā hontu ñātayo.
```

*Translit:* idaṃ me ñātīnaṃ hotu, sukhitā hontu ñātayo · *EN:* "May this be for my relatives; may my relatives be happy."
*Source:* Petavatthu 27 (the *me* form, for one's own); the offering-model form "Idaṃ **vo** ñātīnaṃ hotu…" ("your relatives") is Khuddakapāṭha 7 (Tirokuṭṭasutta). ⚠️ Don't cross-cite the forms. Register: funerals, kathina, memorial days — solemn, not casual.

**6. Culture — the great dreams** 🌠 *(canonical dream lore)*
The Bodhisatta's **five great dreams** before Awakening (AN 5.196, Supina Sutta) and King Pasenadi's **sixteen dreams** (Ja 77, Mahāsupina Jātaka) are the canonical dream set. ⚠️ There is no "four kinds of dreams" sutta (that taxonomy is commentarial and unverified here) — see Misuse Notes.

## 5. 🇹🇭 Thai

*Every line below awaits your native sign-off. Romanization is the lane's plain scheme (no tone marks) — normalize if you like.*

**1. สุขสันต์วันเกิด** 🎂

```
สุขสันต์วันเกิด
```

*Translit:* suk-san wan koet · *EN:* "Happy birthday."
*(Source: th.wiktionary (RID-backed); Lexitron usage; the fixed greeting.)*

**2. ขอให้คำอธิษฐานเป็นจริงนะ** 🎂 *(the candle wish)*

```
ขอให้คำอธิษฐานเป็นจริงนะ
```

*Translit:* kho hai kam athitthan pen ching na · *EN:* "May your wish come true." (คำอธิษฐาน = the wish you make and stake on a result.)
*(Source: real candle-moment usage; the folk belief that candle-smoke carries the wish upward is documented modern folklore.)*

**3. ขอให้สมปรารถนา** 💫

```
ขอให้สมปรารถนา
```

*Translit:* kho hai som prathana · *EN:* "May your wishes be fulfilled."
*(Source: Lexitron s.v. สมปรารถนา ("fulfil one's wish"); birthday and festival usage. Slightly elevated register.)*

**4. ขอให้สมหวังนะ** 💫

```
ขอให้สมหวังนะ
```

*Translit:* kho hai som wang na · *EN:* "May it go as you hope."
*(Source: th.wiktionary s.v. สมหวัง; the most natural all-purpose "may it come true." Variant: ขอให้ได้ดังหวังนะ — a touch more literary.)*

**5. ขอให้ฝันเป็นจริงนะ** 🌠

```
ขอให้ฝันเป็นจริงนะ
```

*Translit:* kho hai fan pen ching na · *EN:* "May your dream come true." (Fuller written variant: ขอให้ความฝันเป็นจริงนะ.)
*(Source: multiple independent usage instances — song title, social posts.)* ⚠️ ฝัน is native Tai (Proto-Tai *hwan) — NOT from Pali *supina* or Sanskrit *svapna*.

**6. ขอให้ฝันดีนะ** 🌠 *(sweet dreams)*

```
ขอให้ฝันดีนะ
```

*Translit:* kho hai fan di na · *EN:* "Sweet dreams."
*(Source: Lexitron s.v. ฝันดี; subtitle usage. The bedtime wish.)*

*Word notes: คำอธิษฐาน (kam athitthan — wish, vow, prayer-pledge; < Sanskrit अधिष्ठान adhiṣṭhāna). ⚠️ The Pali parallel adhiṭṭhāna canonically means "determination, resolve" — the Thai "wish" sense is a vernacular development.*

## The Wish Send — assembled example `[craft]`

For a birthday (candles lit):

```
Voti compos!
สุขสันต์วันเกิด
ขอให้คำอธิษฐานเป็นจริงนะ
May the candle hear you, and the year agree.
```

For a friend chasing a big dream:

```
May it come to you through the gate of horn.
ขอให้ฝันเป็นจริงนะ
May everything you're hoping for be already on its way.
```

## ⚠️ Misuse Notes

- **"May it come through the gate of horn" is house craft** — no attested greeting formula exists; the sources are descriptive (Od. 19.562–567; Verg. *Aen.* 6.893–898)
- **Aeneas exits through the IVORY gate** (false dreams) — the famous crux of *Aen.* 6.898; don't "fix" it
- **The Roman birthday poem is Statius *Silvae* 2.7, not 4.5** — and it's posthumous (dead addressee, commissioned by his widow); birthday POEMS ≠ greetings; no attested classical birthday-wish line exists
- **"Compos voti" is dictionary Latin** — the corpus form is Suetonius' accusative "voti compotem" (*Cal.* 13)
- **Baldrs draumar = death-dreams and necromancy** — funeral register, never a dream wish; also NOT a Codex Regius poem (AM 748 I a 4to only)
- **Draumkvæde**: oral Telemark ballad, written down in the 1840s; its "Blest is he who…" stanzas are alms-beatitudes, not wishes
- **Zoëga 1910 OCR garbles** the archive.org scan — quoted snippets were cleaned; cite the lexicon, not raw OCR
- **कल्पद्रुम = कल्पवृक्ष** (same motif); "Kalpadruma" lexicon names are allusive, not a separate origin story
- **cintāmaṇi is Hindu AND Buddhist** (Avalokiteśvara's jewel; *yid bzhin nor bu*) — respect both traditions
- **"Idaṃ me puññaṃ āsavakkhayāvahaṃ hotu" is a traditional chanting formula** — not a sutta quote
- **Khp 7 reads "Idaṃ vo ñātīnaṃ hotu"** ("your relatives"); the *me* form is Petavatthu 27 — don't cross-cite
- **No "four kinds of dreams" sutta** — AN 5.196 is the Bodhisatta's FIVE great dreams; the sixteen are King Pasenadi's (Ja 77)
- **Thai ฝัน is native Tai** — not from *supina*/*svapna*; and **जन्मदिन…शुभकामनाएँ / मुबारक are modern Hindi** (मुबारक Arabic via Urdu), not Sanskrit
- **इच्छासिद्धि could not be verified** — excluded on purpose; so is any Pali "may hopes be fulfilled" formula (gap kept, nothing invented)
- **Never promise outcomes** — "may it come true," never "it will"
