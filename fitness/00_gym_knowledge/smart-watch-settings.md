## Heart Rate Zones

Max HR (220 - age 25): **195 bpm**

| Zone | % of Max | BPM Range | When to Be Here |
|------|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Zone 1 — Recovery | 50-60% | 98-117 | Walking, warm-up, cool-down |
| Zone 2 — Fat Burn | 60-70% | **117-137** | ⭐ Your run/walk sessions. This is where fat oxidation peaks. Stay here on Wednesdays |
| Zone 3 — Cardio | 70-80% | 137-156 | Gym: during accessory work, moderate sets |
| Zone 4 — Threshold | 80-90% | 156-176 | Gym: heavy compound sets (squat, deadlift, bench). You'll touch this briefly, not stay here |
| Zone 5 — Max | 90-100% | 176-195 | Sprint finishes only. Don't chase this |

### Zone 2 Is Your Money Zone

For fat loss + marathon base building, **Zone 2 (117-137 bpm)** is where you want your Wednesday run/walk. At your current 12:00/km pace with walk/jog intervals, you're probably already in Zone 2. As you get fitter, you'll run faster at the same heart rate — that's the adaptation we're chasing.

**How to check:** During Wednesday run, glance at your watch. If HR is above 137, slow down. If below 117, speed up slightly. The goal isn't speed — it's time in zone.

## PAI Score (If Your Watch Has It)

Xiaomi watches use **PAI (Personal Activity Intelligence)** — a 7-day rolling score based on heart rate elevation.

| Target | 100 PAI per week |
|--------|------------------|
| How to earn it | Any activity that raises your HR above resting. Higher HR = faster PAI accumulation |
| What 100 PAI means | You're getting enough cardiovascular stimulus for health benefits |
| Your path to 100 | 4 gym sessions + 1 run/walk + daily walking will clear 100 PAI easily |

Don't obsess over the daily number — it's a rolling 7-day total. If you're at 80 on Saturday, add an extra walk.

## Additional Features to Use

| Feature | How to Use It |
|---------|---------------|
| **Sleep tracking** | Wear watch to bed. Check deep sleep duration each morning. If deep sleep is consistently <1 hour, flag it — sleep quality matters as much as quantity |
| **Resting HR** | Track weekly trend. As you get fitter, resting HR should drop. Starting point likely 65-80 bpm. A 5-10 bpm drop over 8 weeks = real cardiovascular adaptation |
| **SpO2** | Spot-check, don't obsess. Below 94% consistently = flag. Normal is 95-100% |
| **GPS tracking** | Use for outdoor runs. Tracks pace, distance, route. More accurate than treadmill distance |
| **Workout mode** | Start a "Strength" workout during gym sessions. Don't trust the calorie estimate (wrist HR during lifting is inaccurate) — use it for rest timer and heart rate trend only |

## Caveat: Wrist HR During Lifting Is Inaccurate

When you grip a barbell, the wrist flexor muscles constrict blood vessels, throwing off the optical HR sensor. Your watch will under-report HR during bench, squat, deadlift, and OHP. **Don't panic if your gym session shows 90 bpm.** Use the watch for:

- ✅ Rest timer between sets
- ✅ Run/walk HR (moving without gripping = accurate)
- ✅ Step count
- ✅ Sleep tracking
- ✅ Resting HR trend
