# Fundamentals: Alignment, Pre-Workout & Visuals

> One note, everything visual. Triggered by gym-trainer feedback: *"you need basic correct before we go further, basic aliment / bone aliment."* The words are almost certainly **"Basic Alignment"** and **"Bone Alignment"**, Thai fitness shorthand for การจัดแนวร่างกาย / จัดโครงสร้างร่างกาย (body / postural alignment). "จัดกระดูก" (bone setting) is the chiropractic-adjacent version of the same idea.

## 1. What "aligned" looks like

![|320](https://proactivehealth.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/posture.jpg)

**Plumb line:** ear over shoulder → ribs over pelvis → hips over knees → knees over ankles. You don't need to stand like a soldier at rest, aligned means the position you can hold without effort.

![|320](https://www.wasilzafar.com/images/series/human-anatomy/posture-plumb-line-ideal-alignment.webp)

**Why it matters for you (119 kg, waist 130, 9 wks detrained):** the belly pulls the pelvis into anterior tilt and the upper back into rounding. Normal physics, not a defect. Alignment work = counteracting that drift under load and in daily posture, **not** a gate you must pass before training. It's a skill built through controlled reps, video checks and cues.

## 2. Bracing: before every heavy set

![|400](https://images.ctfassets.net/hjcv6wdwxsdz/6w61lQRDOGixh2Ktc0u9xb/9fc62a1ea5f2a54233696249ef0a86ec/abdominal-bracing-hold.png?w=1200)

1. Inhale into the belly **and sides** (360°), not the chest.
2. Brace like you're about to take a light punch: the whole wall tightens.
3. Hold through the rep; exhale at the top, re-brace before the next rep.

- **Why:** raises intra-abdominal pressure → stiffens the spine; evidence supports bracing for heavy single-plane lifts (ACE, 2018).
- **Self-test:** hands on obliques; inhale, ribs and belly expand sideways; if the chest lifts and shoulders shrug, you're breathing into the chest.
- **Where:** every squat, deadlift, bench and OHP working set.

## 3. Hip hinge: the deadlift pattern

![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/1459-rR0LJzx.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/0044-XlZ4lAC.gif)

*Dumbbell RDL (left) · Barbell good morning (right)*

- Push the hips **back**, not the chest down.
- Spine long and neutral. **Dowel test:** broom touching head, upper back, sacrum; hinge to ~45°, the broom must not lift off any contact point.
- Stop when the hamstrings pull. That's your range today.
- **Where:** Friday deadlift; also every heavy thing you pick up off the floor.

## 4. Squat pattern

![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/1462-iYzB0Cz.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/1685-QChZi3x.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/3119-75Bgtjy.gif)

*Side-view squat · Squat-to-overhead-reach · Deep squat hold*

- Knees travel over the **second toe**: push them out, never let them collapse in.
- Heels planted. If they lift, ankle mobility (§5) is the limiter, not willpower.
- Depth = lowest point with a neutral spine. **Side view is the only honest view of depth.**
- **Where:** Wednesday back squat; leg press.

## 5. Ankle & hip mobility: the squat-depth limiters

![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/1377-m0tCHqc.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/1368-uL9CsKm.gif)

*Calf stretch at the wall · Ankle circles*

**Wall test:** toes ~10 cm from the wall, drive the knee toward the wall, heel stays down. Can't touch = limited dorsiflexion. Fix: stretch 2 × 30 sec/side before squats + deep squat hold 3 × 20 sec.

## 6. Scapular control: pack the shoulders

![|200](https://www.strengthlog.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Band-Pull-Apart.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/0688-uTBt1HV.gif)

*Band pull-apart · Scapular pull-up*

- Shoulder blades **down and back**: never shrug up toward the ears.
- Pull-apart: arms straight, pull to chest height, 2-sec hold at the end.
- Scapular pull-up: hang, pull the blades down without bending the elbows, small movement, big control.
- **Where:** bench, OHP, rows, face pulls; activation in the warm-up.

## 7. Core drills: anti-movement, not crunches

![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/0276-iny3m5y.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/3013-u0cNiij.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/3544-5VXmnV5.gif) ![|200](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset/main/videos/0464-CosupLu.gif)

*Dead bug · Low glute bridge · Incline side plank · Front plank (hold it, ignore the twist in the demo)*

- Dead bug: lower back pressed flat the whole time, if it arches, shorten the range.
- Glute bridge: squeeze glutes at the top, ribs down, don't overextend the lower back.
- Side plank: straight line ear–hip–ankle, no hip drop.
- **Where:** planks Wed; dead bugs / side planks optional Sat.

## 9. Pre-workout: what it actually means

"Pre-workout" is three things people conflate: **the warm-up**, **priming**, and **supplements**. They have different evidence and different value for you.

### The warm-up: the part that matters

**Evidence:** structured dynamic warm-ups improve power, sprint performance and range of motion vs. static stretching or none (JSCR systematic review, 2013; Scand J Med Sci Sports, 2006, sport-specific warm-ups win even in less total time). A 2010 meta-analysis (Fradkin et al.) found structured warm-ups improved performance in **79% of reviewed studies**.

**The 4-phase protocol** (12–21 min total; full version on squat/DL days):

| Phase | Duration | Target | Purpose |
|-------|----------|--------|---------|
| 1. General cardio | 3–5 min | HR 100–130, light sweat | Raise core temp, blood flow |
| 2. Dynamic mobility | 4–6 min | Controlled full ROM | Prep joints + patterns |
| 3. Activation | 2–4 min | RPE 5–6 | Fire glutes/core/scapular muscles |
| 4. Ramp-up sets | 3–6 min | Progressive load | Rehearse the lift under load |

**Activation menu** (demos: §6–7): glute bridges 2×10 before squat/DL days · band pull-aparts 2×15 before pressing days · dead bugs 2×6/side for bracing.

**Static stretching before lifting: skip it**; it can transiently reduce power output. Static work goes after the session or on rest days.

### Priming: the 5 minutes before your first set
Two bracing reps at 60% of working weight, 30 sec rest; one "feel" rep at 80%. That's it: no 20-minute activation circus.

### Supplements: the honest version

| Thing | Evidence | For you right now |
|-------|----------|-------------------|
| Caffeine | 3–6 mg/kg improves strength/power; reliable | **Wait for the BP baseline.** 200 mg max to start (one strong coffee) |
| Creatine monohydrate | 3–5 g/day, most-studied supplement, strength + lean mass | Fine to start: 5 g/day, any time, no loading needed |
| Whey | Convenient protein, no magic | Already in your food system as a protein anchor |
| Beta-alanine / citrulline / etc. | Small effects, not worth the spend | Skip |

**Bottom line:** the supplement is the least valuable third of "pre-workout." A 10-minute warm-up + activation does more than any tub of powder, and it's free.

## 10. The trainer's comment: real observation or upsell?

Two possible motives, and you don't need to know which:

1. **Genuine form observation:** trainers watch hundreds of squats; if they saw knees caving or a rounded back, that's free information.
2. **PT-package sales script:** Thai chain gyms run posture assessments that end at a package price. "Fix alignment before you can train" is a sales gate, not a physiological one.

**The test to apply:** ask them to *show you*: "which rep, what exactly is out of line, and what cue fixes it?" A real observation comes with a demonstration and a cue ("your left knee collapses on the way up, push it out"). A sales pitch comes with a package and a timeline. If it's the second, smile and keep training.

**What you do NOT need:** posture-perfect at rest before training. Your W1 loads (bench 32.5, squat 30, DL 50) are light enough that form gets built *while* training; that's what the RPE gate is for.

## 11. This week's 10-minute homework

1. Film one working set each of squat, deadlift, bench: phone at 45°, side + front. 60 sec per lift.
2. Check against the tests: dowel contact (§3), knee track (§4), shoulder pack (§6).
3. Log one line in `../training/logs/daily/training-log.md` Notes: what looked off, which cue you'll try next session.
4. If a trainer offers a cue, test it: a good cue you feel immediately. If you can't feel it, the cue is wrong for you, not you for the cue.
