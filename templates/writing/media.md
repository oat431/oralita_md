# Media Script Templates

> Fill-in masters for performative writing. Copy ONE block into a project folder. Formats explained: [[03-Media-Script-Guide]] · structures: [[02-Storytelling-Guide]].
>
> **Language rule for scripts:** format vocabulary stays English industry-standard everywhere (INT./EXT., V.O., B-ROLL) — but spoken/dialogue content goes in your **audience's** language. Never translate the format layer.

## ▶️ YouTube Video Essay

```markdown
---
date: YYYY-MM-DD
tags: [script, youtube]
target_length: 8min
---

# Video: <Working title>

**Promise:** viewer walks away knowing <the one insight>
**Structure:** hook → setup → sections (claim→evidence→payoff) → payoff + CTA

## HOOK (0:00–0:15)
Spoken: <most surprising claim/question — NEVER "hey guys welcome back">
Visual: <the most arresting clip/graphic you own>

## SETUP (0:15–0:45)
Spoken: what this video covers + why it matters to THIS viewer

## SECTION 1: <claim>
Spoken: <evidence → mini-payoff>
B-roll/text on screen: <list shots>

## SECTION 2: <claim>          ← re-hook between sections: zoom, joke, graphic
...

## PAYOFF + ENDING
Deliver the promised insight in one sentence.
CTA: exactly ONE ask (subscribe OR watch next video, never both).

---
Shot notes table:
| Time | Spoken line (draft) | Visual |
|------|--------------------|--------|
```

## 🎬 Screenplay scene — universal format, content in any language

```markdown
---
date: YYYY-MM-DD
tags: [script, screenplay]
---

# <Project Title> — Scene <N>

INT./EXT. <LOCATION> - NIGHT    ← sluglines ALWAYS in English caps, regardless of dialogue language

<Action lines: present tense, only what camera/mic captures.
 Dialogue: your audience's language. Extensions: (V.O.) voice-over, (O.S.) off-screen.>

Example:

INT. CONVENIENCE STORE - 2 AM

Fluorescent light hums over empty shelves. SOMCHAI, 20s,
counts change he doesn't have.

                    SOMCHAI
          (to the cashier, not looking up)
          Can you take the chips out?
          I'm ten baht short.

The cashier says nothing. The register dings anyway.

                    CASHIER (O.S.)
          Pay me next time.
```

> Why: film/TV industries worldwide schedule and budget off the Hollywood-standard format — sluglines and transitions are machine vocabulary read by production tools. Dialogue belongs to the audience's language. Mixing anything else breaks both.

## 📺 AV Script (:30–:90 ads) — two-column

```markdown
---
date: YYYY-MM-DD
tags: [script, av]
length: :60
---

# AV: <Product/Campaign> (:60)

| VIDEO | AUDIO |
|-------|-------|
| <one camera event per row> | VO/SFX/MUSIC per row — VO word count ≈ runtime: :60 ≈ 150 words, :30 ≈ 75 |
| Close-up: hand hesitates over alarm clock | SFX: harsh buzz cuts to silence |
| Whip-pan: face, eyes snap open | VO: "<hook>" |
| Product hero shot | MUSIC: sting · VO: "<benefit>" |
| Logo endcard | VO: "<CTA>" |
```

## 🎙️ Podcast segment / audio drama cue sheet

```markdown
---
date: YYYY-MM-DD
tags: [script, audio]
segment: 5min
---

# Segment: <Topic>

[AMBIENCE: <establish location within first seconds>]

HOST: <identify speakers BY NAME in early lines — ears can't see>

[SFX: <cue>] · [MUSIC: <mood> under dialogue]

HOST: <narrate movement/actions verbally — listeners cannot see
      "watch your step — the floor's wet here", not just steps>

GUEST (<name>): ...
```
